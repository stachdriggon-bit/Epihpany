## What changed in the geographic model

Draft 02 resolves the most important first-pass ambiguities with explicit, reversible working choices:

1. **Ilan:** source on the east-facing slopes of the Western Ilmar Uplands; basin-crossing main stem; east-facing estuary. Eiraven sits on the lower navigable river. Ilanford is the upstream crossing town, and Three-Arch Ferry is its landmark rather than a duplicate place.
2. **Orath lake:** the Ambrin lake is treated as a closed / seasonally contracting basin for now. No permanent outlet is drawn without a demonstrated lower saddle.
3. **Nirvan:** Pellisar's lake drains through a southern valley toward the Sea of Stillness; Mireva belongs to a separate lower wetland/outlet system. Both outlets must follow a downhill corridor, not cross the highland crest.
4. **Tharima:** Namaris has a groundwater-supported oasis, Kharif has a separate upland spring, and Saffra stands on raised ground beside a seasonal wadi. Rihal's route is not considered dependable until its resupply point is identified.
5. **Azmar:** Selka Haven, Seven-Mooring Harbor, and Atlas SET-05 Azmar Port are provisionally one canonical harbor with legacy aliases. Rain-Cistern Island remains a separate anchor; Oruven is placed on a larger island with an explicit freshwater requirement.

## Why these choices are coherent

- A closed inland basin explains a fluctuating lake and low-water shore without requiring an unexplained river outlet.
- Distinct lake catchments in Nirvan prevent an accidental river crossing over the highland divide.
- Independent water sources in Tharima avoid implying a permanent river through dry terrain.
- Separating Oruven from Rain-Cistern Island until the island's water budget is proven avoids placing a permanent inland settlement on a possibly unsuitable small island.
- A single canonical Azmar harbor prevents three IDs from accidentally creating three nearby ports with the same function.

## City placement status

All 30 approved city names remain in the map and in their approved registries. Their dots indicate **relative zones only**; they are not coordinates and do not establish travel distance. If a physical feature later forces a move, preserve the stable city ID and record the siting change.
