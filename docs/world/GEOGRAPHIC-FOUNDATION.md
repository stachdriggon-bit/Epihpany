# EPIPHANY — Geographic Foundation and Settlement Siting

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Integrated working geography; relative placement is accepted as the current map plan, while exact scale, coordinates, and fine coastline geometry remain open.  
**Scope:** Connect the existing coastline, relief, drainage, climate, settlement, and anchor-location records into one coherent spatial framework. This document does not replace the Atlas or the five settlement registries.

## 1. What is being fixed

The existing Atlas already establishes the broad layout: Ilmar lies west, Orath east, Tharima south, Nirvan north, and the Azmar archipelagos occupy central and outer maritime passages. The central sea is the Sea of Dawn; the Sea of Stillness lies north of it, between southern Nirvan and northern/northwestern Orath. The Strait of Passage is provisionally placed between southeastern Orath and northeastern Tharima.

This document turns those broad statements into a usable relative-placement plan. It does **not** invent a scale, coordinates, exact distances, population figures, political borders, or a final count of all continents.

## 2. Map orientation and adjacency

Use this relative layout as the default when drawing or writing travel:

- **Ilmar:** west of the Sea of Dawn. Its western coast faces the Western Outer Ocean; its eastern coast faces the central sea. The Ilan Basin occupies the central-to-eastern lowlands.
- **Orath:** east of the Sea of Dawn and north/east of Tharima. Its western slopes face the central sea; its eastern coast faces the outer ocean. The Eastern Spine runs from the north-northeast toward the south-central interior, with a curved southern branch toward the southeast.
- **Tharima:** south of the Sea of Dawn. Its northern coast faces the central sea; its northeastern reach forms the southern side of the Strait of Passage. Its southern and southwestern shores face the Southern Outer Ocean.
- **Nirvan:** north of the Sea of Stillness. Its southern coast faces that sea; its broken highland arc occupies the northwestern and northern interior. A broken fringe of nearshore islands is distinct from Azmar.
- **Azmar:** three separated archipelago groups, not one continuous chain: Inner Passages in the central sea; Harbor Islands near eastern and southeastern approaches; and Outer Azmar routes farther from the main continental shores. Keep navigable gaps between groups and leave alternate routes around any one bottleneck.

## 3. Relief and drainage commitments

### 3.1 Ilmar

- Retain the broken Western Ilmar Uplands in the western-to-southwestern interior; they are not a continuous coastal wall.
- The Ilan Basin is a broad, gently sloping lowland in the central-to-eastern part of the continent, open toward the Sea of Dawn.
- The main Ilan River begins on the eastern-facing slopes of the western uplands, descends into the basin, gathers tributaries from the Central Forests and open plains, then reaches the east-facing coast through a plausible estuary.
- Independent westward rivers drain the ocean-facing slopes. They do not join the Ilan system across the watershed.
- Place Eiraven on the navigable lower Ilan system, inland from the estuary or on a connected branch. Place Ilanford farther upstream at a viable crossing, only if the final river course supports it.
- Place Westmere on a sheltered section of the west-facing coast, not automatically at the Ilan mouth. Talwen belongs near a forest edge with usable paths; Calder Vale needs a lower upland valley and credible water power; Sorell belongs on the southern inland route toward Tharima.

### 3.2 Orath

- Keep the Eastern Spine trending north-northeast to south-central, with a curved branch toward the southeastern coast.
- The inland basin lies west of the spine and is partially enclosed by lower ridges and plateaus. Its closed-basin status remains conditional on future contours.
- Western rivers drain toward the Sea of Dawn or Sea of Stillness in separate catchments. Eastern rivers drain toward the outer ocean. They must not be connected across the spine.
- Basin inflows terminate in the low lake/wetland system unless a lower natural outlet is deliberately established.
- Veyrun requires a watered highland slope; Darsen requires a real river/overland transfer junction; Velisar belongs on a pass corridor; Ambrin belongs at the fluctuating inland lake; Qadren belongs on a dry but traversable plateau route; Lethai belongs on a suitable eastern coastal harbor.
- Do not force all six cities into one linear road or imply that the inland basin drains to the eastern sea.

### 3.3 Tharima

- Use discontinuous basin rims and uplands, not one continuous mountain ring.
- Separate the northern transitional steppe, interior arid belt, local upland refuges, and coastal drylands. Do not render the whole continent as uniform sand.
- Northern wadis descend from the steppe and basin rims; some end in interior depressions, while selected channels reach the Sea of Dawn after rain. Southern basin channels remain separate unless contours connect them.
- Rain Basin is intermittent. Springs at basin margins require a plausible groundwater source and are not evenly distributed.
- Namaris requires dependable local water; Saffra needs a safe raised market beside a seasonal wadi; Kharif needs a sustainable upland spring; Rihal needs a viable route junction and resupply; Tazir needs a coast that supports fishing and, if retained, salt production; Merzun needs clay and fuel access.
- Do not assume a permanent river links Tharima's basins or that Saffra and Kharif sit on the same watercourse.

### 3.4 Nirvan

- Retain a broken highland arc across the northwestern and northern interior, with branches approaching the coast in selected sectors.
- Concentrate fjord-like inlets where steep glacial valleys reach the sea; other coastlines should be low or moderately sloped.
- Keep a lower southern transitional belt with routes toward the Sea of Stillness.
- Northern, western, and southern river catchments remain separate unless the watershed map explicitly provides a low divide. The highland lake must have a defined outlet; the southern lake chain must spill stepwise to a river or be simplified if contours cannot support it.
- Orven Reach requires a sheltered northern harbor; Pellisar needs a lake landing and land connections; Varnholt needs accessible woodland and transport; Iskell needs a real pass corridor; Mireva needs a credible wetland/outlet; Caerun belongs in the lower southern transition zone.
- Cold does not mean every route is permanently frozen. Seasonal access varies by elevation, exposure, and time of year.

### 3.5 Azmar

- Keep Inner Passages, Harbor Islands, and Outer Azmar spatially distinct, with more than one viable maritime route through the wider region.
- Island relief varies: some islands are high and stream-bearing, some low and dry, some eroded. Do not give all islands the same ecology.
- Selka Haven needs a sheltered harbor at a genuinely useful passage junction; Tern Isle needs a practical navigation-marker site; Oruven needs enough freshwater and land for permanent inland settlement; Maret Quay needs shelter and resupply; Vey Shoals needs a credible shallow-water/sediment/current environment; Far Lantern needs a reason for remoteness and a viable local or imported water supply.
- No river connects separate islands. Every permanent settlement must have an explicit credible freshwater source or supply arrangement.

## 5. Anchor-location reconciliation

Existing anchor locations are narrative anchors, not permission to create duplicate settlements. Use the following default interpretation until a story gives a strong reason otherwise:

- **IL-LOC-01 The Three-Arch Ferry ↔ IL-CITY-04 Ilanford:** treat the Three-Arch Ferry as the named crossing/landmark within Ilanford. If the river map makes the crossing unsuitable, revise Ilanford's siting rather than inventing a second nearby crossing without purpose.
- **IL-LOC-02 The Lanternwood Edge ↔ IL-CITY-03 Talwen:** treat the Edge as a forest-edge landmark or nearby district associated with Talwen, not automatically a second town.
- **OR-LOC-01 The Switchback Gate ↔ OR-CITY-03 Velisar:** treat the Gate as the pass checkpoint/roadside cluster associated with Velisar. Keep one settlement record unless a story needs a distinct neighboring settlement.
- **OR-LOC-02 The Low-Water Shore ↔ OR-CITY-04 Ambrin:** treat the Shore as a named lakeside sector/landmark of Ambrin.
- **TH-LOC-01 The Wadi Market ↔ TH-CITY-02 Saffra:** treat the Wadi Market as the market district or historic site that gives Saffra its role.
- **TH-LOC-02 The Spring Steps ↔ TH-CITY-03 Kharif:** treat the Spring Steps as a spring-and-terrace landmark within or beside Kharif.
- **NI-LOC-01 The Last Warm Dock ↔ NI-CITY-01 Orven Reach:** treat the Dock as the harbor's best-known pier or working district.
- **NI-LOC-02 The Pine Ledger:** keep as a named woodland/trail landmark in the same broad forest-and-route region as Varnholt, not a second settlement unless the story establishes one.
- **AZ-LOC-01 The Seven-Mooring Harbor ↔ AZ-CITY-01 Selka Haven:** treat the Harbor as the anchorage or harbor district associated with Selka Haven. Reconcile its stable place record with Atlas SET-05 — Azmar Port rather than counting three separate harbors by default.
- **AZ-LOC-02 The Rain-Cistern Island ↔ AZ-CITY-03 Oruven:** treat the island as Oruven's island/location name or a nearby water-management landmark only if the island geography supports a permanent settlement. If not, keep the anchor as a separate uninhabited or seasonally used island and move Oruven to a larger island with freshwater.

These links are **continuity decisions for planning**, not claims that every anchor has already been fully mapped. Preserve all existing IDs in references. If two IDs ultimately refer to the same physical place, designate one canonical place record and list the other ID as an alias/reference; do not silently delete identifiers or duplicate full descriptions.

## 6. Route logic

1. **Ilmar internal route:** western coastal ports connect to forest-edge paths and the Ilan Basin; the river corridor links upstream crossings to downstream markets. River and road routes complement each other, rather than every town sitting on one road.
2. **Ilmar–Tharima transition:** Sorell is the southern approach market on the Ilmar side; crossing into Tharima requires a real land or sea route shown on the eventual map, not a continent-wide border line.
3. **Orath passes:** at least three broad corridors remain distinct: a northern high pass, a central lower pass connecting inhabited basins, and a southern pass toward the central-sea coast. Velisar occupies one pass corridor, not all three.
4. **Tharima routes:** settlements connect water points and seasonal markets. Route reliability varies by rainfall; avoid making every route a permanent caravan highway.
5. **Nirvan routes:** southern valleys provide the most plausible links between northern settlements and milder coastal access; high passes may be seasonal.
6. **Azmar sea routes:** Inner Passages and Harbor Islands offer multiple routes; Outer Azmar requires planned stops and supplies. Do not let one harbor or strait control every journey.
7. Travel times remain unset until the map has scale, terrain difficulty, vessel speeds, and seasonal constraints.

## 7. Map-drawing order

Create the next geographic map in layers, in this order:

1. Draw the five principal regions and the seas/strait using the adjacency in §2.
2. Draw continental outlines and the three Azmar groups; retain alternate channels through the archipelagos.
3. Draw the major ridgelines, highland arcs, basin rims, and lower transition belts.
4. Mark watershed divides before drawing any rivers.
5. Trace the Ilan system, separate Orath catchments, Tharima's episodic wadis, Nirvan's river/lake outlets, and only locally justified Azmar streams.
6. Add lakes and wetlands with an explicit inlet/outlet or closed-basin water balance.
7. Check climate and vegetation against relief, wind exposure, and water availability.
8. Place the 30 approved settlement names from §4 and reconcile anchor locations using §5.
9. Add broad travel corridors and test route continuity, avoiding implied distances until scale is selected.
10. Only then consider political borders, capitals, population, and historical routes.

## 8. Unresolved decisions that must remain open

- Map scale, coordinates, exact coastlines, elevations, distances, and travel times.
- Exact outlet status of Orath's inland basin and final positions of all lakes.
- Seasonal winds, currents, astronomical parameters, and precise climate boundaries.
- Whether additional continents or major landmasses exist beyond the current map.
- Whether every proposed settlement survives the final map; names remain approved even if a site's function or location needs revision.
- Which cities become capitals, political centers, or major historical hubs.
- Whether any anchor location eventually needs a distinct stable physical record rather than the default association in §5.

## 9. Acceptance rule

The relative layout, broad drainage logic, settlement-siting constraints, and default anchor associations in this document are the current working geographic foundation. Exact cartographic details remain provisional. Any future change must update this document and the Atlas, then check the affected settlement registry, anchor locations, routes, stories, quests, cards, and translations.
