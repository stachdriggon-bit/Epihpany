# EPIPHANY — Tharima: Candidate Cities

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Provisional naming proposals; none are established canon yet.  
**Scope:** A small first set of settlement candidates for Tharima. Names and roles should be tested in stories before they become fixed canon.

## Ground rules

- Tharima is the southern continent in the current working map. Exact coastlines, watersheds, climate boundaries, routes, and scale remain provisional.
- These are candidate settlements, not a complete gazetteer and not a requirement that every candidate must exist.
- Tharima must not be reduced to “a desert continent.” Drylands, uplands, seasonal waterways, fertile pockets, coasts, and varied communities may coexist where the physical map supports them.
- Water access matters, but not every story or settlement should revolve around scarcity.
- Before adding a place, check the shared place registry and [Anchor Locations](ANCHOR-LOCATIONS.md). Candidate overlaps are noted below to prevent accidental duplicates.
- Naming note: the current names and Arabic-script forms are provisional pronunciation/transliteration proposals, not evidence of a finalized language or cultural analogue. See [Naming and Toponymy](NAMING-AND-TOPONYMY.md) for the initial association audit and naming workflow. Keep each settlement's role while names are reviewed; do not rename them automatically.

## Candidate settlement list

### TH-CITY-01 — Namaris / ناماريس

**Working role:** An inland town with gardens and a maintained water network, supplied by a spring, well field, or managed source that must be supported by the final geography.

**Everyday life:** Gardeners, water-channel maintainers, food sellers, potters, and carriers coordinate upkeep. Neighbors may share maintenance duties even when they disagree about how the water should be distributed.

**Story potential:** A repair reveals that a channel's layout was altered in an earlier generation. The discovery explains one practical problem but does not automatically reveal a grand lost civilization.

**Placement condition:** Requires a credible water source and a catchment or groundwater system. Do not place it in the middle of an arid landscape without explaining how it survives.

**Continuity note:** Namaris is an ordinary inhabited place with a water system, not a magical oasis by default.

### TH-CITY-02 — Saffra / سفرا

**Working role:** A seasonal market on or beside a broad wadi, where traders and nearby communities gather when travel conditions permit.

**Everyday life:** Temporary stalls, livestock exchange, local produce, repair services, and shared rules for setting up and clearing the market shape the calendar.

**Story potential:** A proposed road or structure may redirect floodwater and affect people downstream. The conflict depends on evidence and competing obligations rather than a simple villain.

**Placement condition:** Requires a wadi with a plausible upstream catchment and a safe-enough raised market area. Flash floods remain possible even after long dry periods.

**Continuity note:** This may simply be the named version of anchor **TH-LOC-01 — The Wadi Market**. Keep one place record and one stable ID if so; do not treat Saffra and the anchor as separate by default.

### TH-CITY-03 — Kharif / خريف

**Working role:** An upland settlement whose terraces and gardens depend on a spring or spring complex.

**Everyday life:** Residents maintain steps, retaining walls, channels, and shared access paths. Seasonal work includes repairing damage and adjusting water-sharing arrangements.

**Story potential:** The spring's flow changes, and residents compare seasonal observations, old channel designs, and upstream land use. Different explanations can be tested without requiring a supernatural cause.

**Placement condition:** Requires suitable upland terrain and a sustainable spring source; the catchment must make geographic sense.

**Continuity note:** This may be the same location as anchor **TH-LOC-02 — The Spring Steps**. Reuse one stable place ID if the concepts align; add a separate settlement only if the story needs a distinct place.

### TH-CITY-04 — Rihal / رحال

**Working role:** A service town where two or more long-distance travel routes meet, supporting caravans and other overland traffic.

**Everyday life:** Lodging houses, animal handlers, equipment repairers, cooks, guides, and traders depend on arrivals that vary by season and political conditions. The town also has residents whose lives are not tied to travel.

**Story potential:** A route becomes less popular after a new crossing or agreement elsewhere. Local workers disagree about whether to invest in new services or diversify their livelihoods.

**Placement condition:** Requires a believable route junction and enough water, food supply, or resupply links to sustain regular traffic. Exact routes are open.

**Continuity note:** Rihal's identity is logistics and adaptation. It is not automatically the continent's largest caravan city or a political capital.

### TH-CITY-05 — Tazir / تازير

**Working role:** A coastal town combining fishing, salt production where conditions allow, and services for small vessels.

**Everyday life:** Fishers, net-menders, salt workers, boat repairers, food sellers, and families processing seasonal catches share the working shore. Storms and changing shorelines affect work differently from year to year.

**Story potential:** A new landing point improves safety for some boats but threatens a familiar work area. Residents debate access and maintenance, while older shoreline markers provide useful but incomplete evidence.

**Placement condition:** Requires a suitable coast; salt production only makes sense if local evaporation, salinity, and water access support it. If not, keep the fishing and coastal craft role and revise the salt element.

**Continuity note:** Do not assume every coastal settlement is a major port or that all of Tharima's coastline is alike.

### TH-CITY-06 — Merzun / مرزون

**Working role:** A hill-country town with pottery, fired-clay craft, and practical goods used in storage, cooking, building, or water transport.

**Everyday life:** Clay gatherers, potters, kiln workers, carriers, builders, and merchants balance fuel use, material quality, and demand. Different workshops may favor different techniques without one being universally superior.

**Story potential:** A source of clay becomes harder to use, or a new kiln design reduces fuel needs but produces unreliable batches until workers learn its limits.

**Placement condition:** Requires usable clay deposits and a plausible fuel supply. The exact terrain and resources remain open; do not assume rare minerals or extraordinary technology.

**Continuity note:** Merzun's signature is skilled material craft, not a mysterious ancient forge or a city devoted to one legendary artifact.

## Cross-city rules

1. **Stable IDs beat names.** If a name changes, retain the ID and update this record.
2. **One place, one record.** Aliases, historic names, and districts belong in the same record unless a story establishes they are separate.
3. **No duplicate city creation.** Search names, aliases, roles, and IDs before inventing a settlement. Similar functions do not automatically make places identical, but overlaps must be checked.
4. **Descriptions are not copy-paste filler.** Other files should reference the place ID and use only details needed for the scene.
5. **Changes must propagate.** If a place changes materially, update this record first, then check the Atlas, anchor locations, stories, quests, character relationships, cards, and translations.
6. **Keep uncertainty visible.** Exact water sources, coastlines, routes, political roles, and dates remain open until explicitly decided.
