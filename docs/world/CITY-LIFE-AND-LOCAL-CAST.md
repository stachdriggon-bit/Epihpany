# EPIPHANY — Selected City Life, Gathering Places, and Local Cast
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Provisional creative development pack. No district name, custom, character, or event below becomes canon by appearing here.  
**Scope:** Five city prototypes, one per mapped region, selected to test varied everyday life and story potential. They are not a complete regional survey.

## Reading status
- **Registry anchor:** Existing settlement ID and current working role only. The regional registry remains the source of truth.
- **Proposal:** New design material for possible future use. The owner may keep, change, combine, or discard it.
- **Open:** Not yet decided. No inference should be made from a gap.
- Names for proposed neighborhoods and gathering places are **working labels**, not new registered places or stable place IDs. If accepted for recurring use, reconcile them with the regional city registry and atlas before implementation.
- Character IDs below are provisional editorial IDs. These characters are drafts, not globally finalized canon, collectible cards, or established historical witnesses.
- These profiles do not establish a calendar, political system, language, religion, dynasty, formal institution, or date. They do not assume that all residents share one custom or worldview.

## 1. Eiraven / إيرافين — IL-CITY-01
**Registry anchor:** Major river-market city; possible capital candidate, not designated. Exact location, political importance, and river relationships remain provisional.

### A city experienced through different routines
**Proposed neighborhood lenses** (working labels only):
- **The Bank Stalls:** A busy trading stretch where people compare prices, delivery times, quality, and trust. Its identity comes from repeated transactions, not from a single exotic custom.
- **The Ropewalk:** A work area associated with rope, packing, load preparation, and repairs. People here judge plans by whether they can be carried out safely.
- **The Quiet Steps:** A less hurried riverside stretch used by residents who need to talk, wait, sketch, or take a break away from the busiest market traffic.

These are not confirmed geographic districts. Their exact placement and whether Eiraven needs formal named districts remain open.

### Gathering places
- **The Arrival Board** *(proposed shared notice point):* Carriers leave practical updates about delayed goods. Entries can be incomplete or outdated; the board is useful, not infallible.
- **The Open Worktable** *(proposed informal meeting spot):* People compare damaged samples, measurements, and repair ideas. It should not be treated as a guild or official tribunal.
- **The Late Cup Stall** *(proposed ordinary social stop):* A place to hear neighborhood gossip, ask for a small favor, or discover that two people have different versions of a harmless incident.

### Possible customs — not universal
- Some regular traders might repeat the key terms of a deal aloud before parting, so both sides can correct misunderstandings.
- A person returning after a long absence might be asked what has changed in their work or route, rather than immediately being asked where they have been.
- These habits may be contested, ignored, or practiced differently by newcomers and long-time residents. Do not present them as city law.

### Provisional local cast
#### IL-CHAR-P01 — Neri Vale (working name)
- **Role:** Load planner who helps small sellers prepare mixed shipments.
- **Independent goal:** Build a reputation for reliable estimates without becoming responsible for every late delivery.
- **Personality and contradiction:** Patient with beginners; impatient with people who conceal a change and expect everyone else to adapt.
- **Strengths:** Notices weight distribution, packing order, and missing details in a plan.
- **Limits:** Cannot know a shipment's true condition without inspection; tends to trust written quantities too much when the source is familiar.
- **Useful story pressure:** A shipment's contents match the list but not the buyer's expectation. Neri must decide whether to defend the paperwork or help investigate the mismatch.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Knows the loads they handled, not the motives of every trader involved.
- **Phrase:** “A good estimate still needs room for surprise.”  
  **Arabic:** «حتى التقدير الجيد يحتاج إلى هامش للمفاجآت».

#### IL-CHAR-P02 — Samet Rill (working name)
- **Role:** Stallholder who sells practical goods to residents as well as travelers.
- **Independent goal:** Keep a stable customer base while avoiding dependence on one large buyer.
- **Personality and contradiction:** Warm and observant; can mistake a familiar customer's silence for agreement.
- **Strengths:** Remembers ordinary needs and notices when a regular changes their routine.
- **Limits:** Has no special access to private information; market gossip can mislead them.
- **Useful story pressure:** Samet has promised the same limited stock to two people for different reasons and must explain the mistake honestly.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Knows conversations they personally heard and patterns they observed; does not automatically know why a person behaves differently.
- **Phrase:** “Tell me what you need it to do.”  
  **Arabic:** «أخبرني بما تحتاج إليه كي يفعله».

### Small story seeds
- A delivery notice was copied from an old message; the mistake is mundane but consequential.
- A resident wants the market to stay busy, while another needs a quiet route to remain usable.
- A tool is blamed for poor work until the group checks the material and the instructions.

### Magic, if a story chooses to use it
A specific practitioner could examine one unusual property of a cargo item, but the result should be limited and testable. Most market decisions should still depend on inspection, negotiation, craft, and trust. No city-wide magical market system is implied.

### Open questions
Capital status; exact river and bridge geography; the city's governance; whether the proposed work areas correspond to actual districts; and which routes connect Eiraven to other settlements.

## 2. Veyrun / فيرون — OR-CITY-01
**Registry anchor:** Terraced highland city known for stonework and water management. Water allocation, public works arrangements, and political status remain open.

### A city experienced through different routines
**Proposed neighborhood lenses** (working labels only):
- **The Upper Steps:** A steep residential and work area where carrying materials is part of ordinary planning. Residents may disagree about whether convenience or durability should guide repairs.
- **The Channel Row:** A stretch beside a maintained water route, where small maintenance decisions affect people farther downhill.
- **The Stone Court:** An open work area where stone samples, measurements, and unfinished pieces can be compared. It is a useful setting for public disagreement without assuming a formal court.

### Gathering places
- **The Repair Bench** *(proposed informal work point):* A place where people explain what has failed and what they have already tried.
- **The Water Ledger Table** *(proposed record-review spot):* Neighbors compare maintenance notes. It is not automatically an official archive or governing body.
- **The Shaded Landing** *(proposed resting place):* A place to rest between levels and encounter people who would not normally share the same workday.

### Possible customs — not universal
- Before a shared repair begins, some participants may point out which other households or work areas could be affected.
- A craftsperson might keep a small failed sample beside a successful one to explain a lesson to an apprentice.
- Neither habit is mandatory; some people may find the extra explanation slow or unnecessary.

### Provisional local cast
#### OR-CHAR-P01 — Dema Rusk (working name)
- **Role:** Stoneworker who inspects repairs and teaches practical measuring.
- **Independent goal:** Keep a reputation for durable work while leaving time to develop a less expensive method.
- **Personality and contradiction:** Careful and fair-minded; can dismiss a low-cost idea before testing it.
- **Strengths:** Notices stress points, uneven surfaces, and the difference between a visible crack and a deeper problem.
- **Limits:** Cannot predict every future failure; lacks full information about the water system beyond the parts personally inspected.
- **Useful story pressure:** A quick repair works for one household but redirects a problem toward another. Dema must acknowledge the trade-off and help revise the plan.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Can explain the work observed, not the intentions of whoever approved it.
- **Phrase:** “Show me where the pressure goes.”  
  **Arabic:** «أرني إلى أين ينتقل الضغط».

#### OR-CHAR-P02 — Iven Sorel (working name)
- **Role:** Resident who keeps informal notes about small maintenance problems near their route.
- **Independent goal:** Make everyday repairs easier to coordinate without becoming an unpaid official for everyone.
- **Personality and contradiction:** Helpful and precise; can treat a missing note as proof that a task was never done.
- **Strengths:** Notices recurring problems and compares observations from different days.
- **Limits:** Their notes are not comprehensive; they may not know work done privately or by people outside their circle.
- **Useful story pressure:** Two sets of notes differ. Iven must ask how each was collected rather than decide that one person is lying.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Knows their own observations and the sources named in their notes.
- **Phrase:** “Missing from my notes is not the same as never happened.”  
  **Arabic:** «غيابه عن ملاحظاتي لا يعني أنه لم يحدث».

### Small story seeds
- A channel repair is urgent, but the safest sequence temporarily inconveniences another group.
- An apprentice spots a flaw in a familiar technique and must persuade an experienced craftsperson to test it.
- A record is correct but no longer describes the current condition.

### Magic, if a story chooses to use it
One character might sense a specific stone or water behavior, with a clear range and uncertainty. This should not make construction effortless or replace measurement, maintenance, or accountability.

### Open questions
How water is allocated; who funds or performs shared repairs; whether any formal public works organization exists; the city's exact geography; and the status of any local customs.

## 3. Namaris / ناماريس — TH-CITY-01
**Registry anchor:** Inland oasis-town with gardens and a maintained water network. The water source, allocation arrangements, crops, and seasonal patterns are not finalized.

### A city experienced through different routines
**Proposed neighborhood lenses** (working labels only):
- **The Garden Lanes:** A residential-work area where plant care, household tasks, and maintenance overlap.
- **The Water Steps:** A practical access point for checking or maintaining part of the water network. Its exact physical form is open.
- **The Basket Market:** A gathering area for produce, containers, tools, and small services. It should serve residents as well as visitors.

### Gathering places
- **The Seed Exchange Mat** *(proposed informal meeting point):* Gardeners compare seeds or growing notes, with no assumption of a formal exchange institution.
- **The Maintenance Shade** *(proposed rest spot):* Workers pause, compare observations, and coordinate who will check a small problem.
- **The Evening Courtyard** *(proposed shared social space):* Residents may bring food, repair something, or talk after work. Participation and household traditions vary.

### Possible customs — not universal
- Some neighbors may announce a change to a shared task before making it, even if they believe the change is minor.
- Gardeners may describe a plant's condition using practical observations rather than a single fixed calendar date.
- These are possible habits, not evidence of a universal Thariman tradition or a finalized calendar.

### Provisional local cast
#### TH-CHAR-P01 — Luma Sadin (working name)
- **Role:** Garden grower who tests small changes in planting and care.
- **Independent goal:** Improve reliability in one garden plot without risking the household's whole season on an untested method.
- **Personality and contradiction:** Curious and generous with advice; sometimes forgets that another household may not have the same tools or time.
- **Strengths:** Observes plant condition, compares small trials, and records what changed.
- **Limits:** One garden is not proof that a method works everywhere; she cannot infer a water-system cause from plant appearance alone.
- **Useful story pressure:** A method succeeds in one plot but fails in another. Luma must learn which differences matter before recommending it widely.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Knows what she tested and observed, not the entire region's growing conditions.
- **Phrase:** “Let's change one thing at a time.”  
  **Arabic:** «لنغيّر شيئًا واحدًا في كل مرة».

#### TH-CHAR-P02 — Harun Tef (working name)
- **Role:** Maintenance worker who checks a section of the town's water network.
- **Independent goal:** Complete reliable work without being blamed for every problem in the network.
- **Personality and contradiction:** Calm under pressure; becomes defensive when people report a problem without describing what they saw.
- **Strengths:** Understands the part of the network they maintain and asks useful diagnostic questions.
- **Limits:** Does not control every section and cannot promise that a repair will solve an unrelated problem elsewhere.
- **Useful story pressure:** A complaint is real, but its suspected cause is wrong. Harun must respond respectfully while narrowing down the issue.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Knows the section they inspect and the limits of their own access.
- **Phrase:** “Where did you first notice the change?”  
  **Arabic:** «أين لاحظت التغيّر أول مرة؟»

### Small story seeds
- A leak appears before a busy day; residents must agree on a temporary plan and communicate the limits clearly.
- A seed exchange creates a disagreement over what “the same variety” actually means.
- A newcomer offers a useful suggestion but assumes every household follows the same routine.

### Magic, if a story chooses to use it
A particular person may interact with a narrow water-related phenomenon. Do not imply that the person can create unlimited fresh water, control the whole network, or replace maintenance workers.

### Open questions
Water source and rights; crop varieties; local governance; actual seasons and calendar; whether any named gathering place should become a registered location.

## 4. Pellisar / بيليسار — NI-CITY-02
**Registry anchor:** Lakeside town with seasonal boat and overland links. Exact lake geography and route conditions remain provisional.

### A city experienced through different routines
**Proposed neighborhood lenses** (working labels only):
- **The Boat Landing:** A work area for departures, arrivals, loading, and maintenance. Not every resident is a traveler or boat worker.
- **The Pack Street:** A route where goods are prepared for overland travel when water travel is unsuitable.
- **The Long Table:** A proposed shared eating or meeting place where route advice is exchanged, debated, and sometimes corrected.

### Gathering places
- **The Route Board** *(proposed informal notice point):* Records route observations with the date or condition when known. Old information remains visible but should be marked as old.
- **The Mending Corner** *(proposed repair spot):* People patch equipment and learn small practical skills while waiting.
- **The Warm Room** *(proposed community rest space):* A place to recover from travel or bad weather. It is not a formal shelter system unless a later story establishes one.

### Possible customs — not universal
- Someone giving route advice might be expected to say what kind of load or traveler the advice suits.
- A returned traveler may be asked which part of an earlier recommendation proved wrong, turning experience into a useful correction rather than shame.
- Some people may prefer familiar routes even when a new route appears faster; this is a personal choice, not a regional trait.

### Provisional local cast
#### NI-CHAR-P01 — Edda Varn (working name)
- **Role:** Boat operator who also knows several overland alternatives.
- **Independent goal:** Keep passengers safe while maintaining enough work to support their own household.
- **Personality and contradiction:** Practical and clear; can sound dismissive when a visitor asks a question she has answered many times.
- **Strengths:** Reads local conditions and explains practical trade-offs between routes.
- **Limits:** Her experience does not cover every load, season, or distant path; she can be wrong when conditions change quickly.
- **Useful story pressure:** A passenger thinks Edda is withholding a shortcut, while Edda is trying to explain that the shortcut suits a different situation.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Knows the routes she has used and recent observations, not every possible route.
- **Phrase:** “A shorter line on a map is not always a shorter journey.”  
  **Arabic:** «الخط الأقصر على الخريطة ليس دائمًا الرحلة الأقصر».

#### NI-CHAR-P02 — Pell Orik (working name)
- **Role:** Repairer who keeps equipment ready for both boat and land travel.
- **Independent goal:** Develop a reliable repair kit without carrying so many supplies that it becomes impractical.
- **Personality and contradiction:** Inventive and generous; sometimes improvises before asking the owner what the equipment must withstand.
- **Strengths:** Finds practical temporary repairs and explains what needs later attention.
- **Limits:** A temporary fix is not proof of long-term safety; has limited knowledge of equipment they have never worked on.
- **Useful story pressure:** A repair lets someone continue, but only if they understand its limits and agree to a follow-up check.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Knows the repair and materials used, not the future condition of the route.
- **Phrase:** “This will hold for now, not forever.”  
  **Arabic:** «سيصمد هذا مؤقتًا، لا إلى الأبد».

### Small story seeds
- Two route notes conflict because they describe different loads or conditions.
- A repair is adequate for a short trip but unsafe for a longer one; the traveler must revise the plan.
- A local is tired of visitors treating the town as a stop with no life beyond travel.

### Magic, if a story chooses to use it
A person might notice one unusual lake pattern, but it cannot guarantee future weather or make every route predictable. Keep route advice evidence-based and fallible.

### Open questions
Lake outlet and seasonal conditions; transport technology; the town's relation to nearby settlements; how route observations are recorded; and any overlap with anchor locations.

## 5. Tern Isle / جزيرة تيرن — AZ-CITY-02
**Registry anchor:** Small island settlement maintaining navigation markers. Island geography, marker conventions, and political connections remain open.

### A settlement experienced through different routines
**Proposed neighborhood lenses** (working labels only):
- **The Marker Yard:** A work area where parts are cleaned, checked, stored, and prepared before use. It is not necessarily a formal workshop or official department.
- **The Landing Path:** The route between the shore and the settlement's everyday spaces, where arriving visitors meet people going about ordinary work.
- **The Shared Store:** A proposed place to keep commonly needed supplies. Who owns it, what it contains, and how access is decided remain open.

### Gathering places
- **The Check Table** *(proposed work and conversation point):* People compare a marker's current condition with notes about the last inspection.
- **The Tide-Side Seats** *(proposed ordinary rest spot):* Residents talk, wait, and watch the water for practical reasons as well as pleasure.
- **The Repair Supper** *(proposed occasional shared meal, not a fixed festival):* People who helped with a difficult task may eat together. Do not turn this into an annual tradition without a separate decision.

### Possible customs — not universal
- A person returning a borrowed tool might mention what condition it is in rather than simply handing it back.
- A marker inspection note might identify the observer and the conditions, so later readers can judge its usefulness.
- These habits are possible local practices, not proof that all island communities share a single maritime culture.

### Provisional local cast
#### AZ-CHAR-P01 — Tavi Ren (working name)
- **Role:** Learner who helps inspect and maintain navigation markers.
- **Independent goal:** Earn trust by making careful observations rather than pretending to know more than they do.
- **Personality and contradiction:** Alert and enthusiastic; sometimes reports an interpretation before separating it from what was actually seen.
- **Strengths:** Notices small physical changes and is willing to recheck work.
- **Limits:** Still learning how different vessels use the markers; a visible change may have several explanations.
- **Useful story pressure:** Tavi notices a marker that looks wrong but cannot tell whether it is damaged, outdated, or unsuitable for a particular vessel.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Knows what was inspected and recorded, not the entire channel's condition.
- **Phrase:** “I saw a change; I haven't explained it yet.”  
  **Arabic:** «لاحظت تغيّرًا، لكنني لم أفسّره بعد».

#### AZ-CHAR-P02 — Maelin Vos (working name)
- **Role:** Supply keeper who balances maintenance materials against household needs.
- **Independent goal:** Keep essential supplies available without allowing one task to consume everything.
- **Personality and contradiction:** Organized and fair; can become overly cautious about releasing materials for an unfamiliar repair.
- **Strengths:** Tracks stock, notices repeated shortages, and asks what a part will be used for.
- **Limits:** Cannot predict every emergency; supply records may be incomplete and the next delivery uncertain.
- **Useful story pressure:** A repair needs a part that another household has reserved. Maelin must help both parties explain urgency and alternatives.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Knows the stock and promises recorded, not every private arrangement on the island.
- **Phrase:** “If we use it here, what will be missing elsewhere?”  
  **Arabic:** «إذا استخدمناه هنا، فما الذي سينقص في مكان آخر؟»

### Small story seeds
- A marker is intact but its meaning has been misunderstood by a visiting crew.
- A supply record is out of date because someone borrowed a tool and forgot to note its return.
- A repair crew must choose between finishing a visible task and checking a less obvious failure point.

### Magic, if a story chooses to use it
One particular marker could show a limited unusual response, but it must not be assumed magical by default. Navigation still requires practical interpretation, current conditions, and the vessel's needs.

### Open questions
The marker system; the island's exact geography; freshwater and supply arrangements; the settlement's political status; and whether any working-place label should become a registered location.

## Reusable development rules for future cities
1. Start with the city's registered role; do not overwrite it with the new cast or scene seed.
2. Add at least three different everyday rhythms: work, social life, and a need unrelated to the main regional feature.
3. A proposed district should help tell stories, not merely add a name to a map.
4. Every recurring character needs a goal independent of the player's arrival, a strength, a limitation, and a boundary on what they know.
5. Let residents disagree within the same city. A custom is never automatically universal.
6. Keep magic optional, person-specific, observable, and limited. Do not infer ability from rarity, occupation, or location.
7. Before making any profile official, reconcile accepted names, district labels, and character IDs with the source registries, character canon, anchor locations, story timeline, and Decision Log.
8. Do not treat a scene seed as an event in world history until it is actually selected, written, and registered with an appropriate status.
