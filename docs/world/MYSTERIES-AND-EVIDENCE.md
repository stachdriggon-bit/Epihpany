# EPIPHANY — Mysteries and Evidence

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Framework; the example mystery types below are possibilities, not established plot facts.

## 1. Principles

- Not every mystery connects to the same cause.
- Absence of evidence does not by itself prove a conspiracy.
- Conflicting records can arise from mistakes, politics, lost context, translation, changing geography, or deliberate deception; extraordinary explanations require evidence too.
- Experts can disagree without all being incompetent.
- Reliable conclusions should sometimes be reachable.
- Some mysteries can be solved; others partly clarified; others may remain open.
- The world's ultimate origin remains unresolved.

## 2. Evidence categories

For each clue, record:
- Evidence ID.
- Physical object, document, testimony, observation, or dataset.
- Origin, date, chain of custody, and possible bias.
- What it directly supports.
- What it does not establish.
- Reliability and limitations.
- Competing interpretations.
- Who in-world has access to it.
- Spoiler level and related stories.

## 3. Mystery record template

- Mystery ID and working title.
- Central question.
- What is established.
- Evidence supporting each interpretation.
- Contradictory evidence.
- Current hypotheses, clearly labeled as hypotheses.
- What would strengthen or falsify each hypothesis.
- What is solved, partly clarified, disputed, or intentionally open.
- Related characters, locations, events, and cards.
- Lore presentation and spoiler rules.

## 4. Possible mystery seeds — not canon

- Historical records disagree about the same event.
- A route disappears from later records.
- Similar architectural symbols appear in distant regions, with no established shared origin.
- A documented phenomenon has disputed causes.
- A victorious power's history conflicts with evidence from the conquered society.
- An archaeological layer appears older than current estimates predict.

These are prompts only. They need not all be used, share an explanation, or be resolved.

## 5. Lore archive format

Present discoveries in readable chapters with provenance and context. Separate established facts, reliable evidence, interpretations, folklore, contradictions, and open questions. Protect story spoilers and avoid presenting every theory as equally supported when the evidence is not equal.
