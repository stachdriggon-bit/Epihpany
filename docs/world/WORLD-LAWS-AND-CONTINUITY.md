# EPIPHANY — World Laws and Continuity Charter
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Foundational synthesis of accepted principles and open design boundaries. This document organizes current rules; it does not settle unanswered mechanics.  
**Purpose:** Keep future geography, magic, creatures, societies, histories, stories, and game systems coherent without making every mystery share one explanation.

## 1. Status labels are part of the worldbuilding
Every significant claim must be treated as one of the following:
- **Accepted principle/fact:** explicitly approved as a stable project rule or source-supported fact.
- **Provisional:** a useful working model that may change.
- **In-world claim:** something a person, document, institution, or tradition says; it may be true, mistaken, incomplete, or self-serving.
- **Disputed:** supported by conflicting evidence or accounts.
- **Open:** not decided. A blank is not permission to invent a hidden answer.
- **Superseded:** retained for decision history but no longer current.

A scene may contain a character's false belief without changing objective canon. Conversely, author-facing notes must not present an in-world belief as fact merely because it appears in dialogue.

## 2. Natural-world consistency
The setting has recognizable physical geography and ordinary environmental processes. Wonder exists within that world; it does not excuse every inconsistency.

- Rivers follow plausible elevation and watershed logic. A river cannot cross between islands; each island settlement needs a credible freshwater arrangement.
- Climate, elevation, coastlines, water balance, vegetation, travel, and settlement placement must support one another. Do not infer exact distances, travel times, currents, or weather from a schematic map.
- Resources are finite and unevenly distributed. A harbor does not guarantee fresh water; a road does not guarantee safe passage; a fertile valley does not guarantee fair access to food.
- Environmental effects may be seasonal, gradual, abrupt, local, or broad, but each claim needs a plausible mechanism or an explicit status as unusual/unexplained.
- A magical effect may complicate ordinary physical expectations only within its defined observed scope. Do not use one local exception to rewrite the world's general natural rules.

**Current boundary:** These are consistency principles, not a complete physics textbook. The exact laws and exceptions of the setting have not all been specified.

## 3. Causality and consequences
- Actions can have consequences beyond the immediate scene, but consequences should travel through a plausible mechanism: people, records, trade, migration, institutions, ecology, routes, or a documented phenomenon.
- Similar appearances do not prove shared cause. Two strange events may be unrelated, and one event may have several competing explanations.
- A mystery does not automatically imply a conspiracy, sentient world, lost super-civilization, or single hidden force.
- Problems should not be solved by an ability, tool, or institution whose relevant limits were never established. If a new capability is introduced, show its scope and constraints before relying on it to resolve a major conflict.
- Consequences need not always be catastrophic. A small change can alter a schedule, craft practice, relationship, local custom, or record.

## 4. Magic and unusual phenomena
**Accepted direction:** EPIPHANY is a fantasy setting. Magic and wonder may shape daily life, craft, travel, ecology, and community decisions. Some characters can use, channel, study, or interact with magic; not everyone can.

**Rules already accepted:**
- Magical access is character-specific and must be established. Do not infer it from a character's rarity, importance, profession, ancestry, or location.
- A phenomenon's observable effect, scope, limits, conditions, evidence, risks, and everyday consequences should be recorded before it becomes recurring canon.
- Phenomena vary in distribution, scale, danger, and degree of understanding. There are no universal magical zones and no required shared explanation.
- Local acceptance of a phenomenon does not establish its cause, age, wider distribution, or connection to another event.
- An unexplained effect is a description of current knowledge, not a mechanism.
- Ordinary skill, observation, maintenance, negotiation, and error remain meaningful in a magical world.

**Still open:** universal or multiple sources, access methods, training, costs, schools or traditions, interactions between abilities, anti-magic or nullification, inheritance, creature capabilities, and any deeper metaphysical structure. Do not silently decide these while writing an isolated scene.

See [Magic and Unusual Phenomena](MAGIC-AND-PHENOMENA.md), [Magical Phenomena Catalog](MAGICAL-PHENOMENA-CATALOG.md), and [Decision Log](DECISIONS-LOG.md).

## 5. Knowledge, evidence, and memory
- Distinguish what happened from what was observed, what was recorded, what was inferred, and what was later remembered.
- Records are made for purposes. Two accurate records may disagree because they measure different things, cover different periods, or use different definitions.
- Witnesses can be sincere and mistaken. Experts can be competent and still have incomplete evidence.
- Preserve provenance where possible: who recorded a claim, when, under what conditions, and with what limitations.
- Do not make all evidence unreliable. Some questions should be answerable through careful comparison, testing, or corroboration.
- A phenomenon may change; a measurement may change; knowledge may change; or the evidence may be insufficient to distinguish them. Stories should not collapse these possibilities prematurely.

## 6. Time and historical continuity
- The world's ultimate origin remains unresolved. The oldest surviving record is not necessarily the beginning.
- The current map covers four named continents and the Azmar archipelagos as principal mapped regions; it does not prove that no other landmasses exist.
- There is no required single civilization from which all peoples descend and no mandatory sequence of ever-greater empires.
- Local, connected, and broad world history are different scales. World-scale changes should be rarer than local and regional developments.
- Keep event time, recording time, character knowledge time, and player discovery time separate.
- Use exact dates only when supported. Relative order is not a calendar date; release order is not in-world chronology.
- A culture's name, border, technology, or identity can change over time. Do not assume present-day terms were used in every era.

Detailed calendar conventions, era names, age calculations, and chronology remain open until they are deliberately designed. Use [Timeline](TIMELINE.md) and [Event Registry](EVENT-REGISTRY.md).

## 7. Life, creatures, and non-human beings
The setting's full biology and creature catalogue remain open. Until a creature or species is defined:
- Do not assume every unusual animal is magical, intelligent, hostile, tameable, or connected to a mystery.
- Do not assume that all non-human beings share one origin, language, society, lifespan, or relationship with humans.
- If a creature has a consequential ability, establish its observable behavior, limits, needs, risks, and ecological or social consequences.
- Avoid introducing a creature solely as an unlimited solution to transport, healing, communication, labor, or combat.
- Distinguish folklore about a creature from direct observation and reliable study.

These are safeguards, not a decision that all creatures must obey ordinary biology without exceptions. Define each exception intentionally.

## 8. Technology, craft, and capability
No single global technology level or complete technological chronology has been fixed. For each story period and region:
- Establish what tools, materials, energy sources, transport, communication, medical practices, and record-keeping are actually available there.
- Do not assume one invention spreads everywhere immediately. Knowledge moves through people, trade, institutions, migration, conflict, or other identifiable channels.
- A tool's existence does not mean every person can afford, maintain, repair, or safely use it.
- Magic should not automatically replace craft, infrastructure, skilled labor, or logistics.
- Avoid modern conveniences under different names unless the setting's technology or magic supports their function.

A later technology register should record capability and availability by place and period rather than impose a single label on the entire world.

## 9. Societies, cultures, and authority
- Geography influences societies but does not determine a single culture or personality.
- A region contains internal differences, disagreements, minorities, newcomers, and changing identities. Do not write all residents as sharing one custom or belief.
- Cultural exchange does not prove common ancestry. Similar practices may arise independently or be adapted for different reasons.
- Political borders need not match cultural regions. Defeat does not erase a people's legacy; losing influence does not necessarily mean a state disappears.
- Do not invent a universal religion, language, legal code, political system, calendar, or institution unless explicitly chosen.
- An institution should have a defined purpose, scope, resources, limits, and relationship to the people it affects. A title or name alone does not prove it controls a whole region.

Specific legal systems, forms of government, family structures, belief systems, and rights remain open for deliberate development.

## 10. Character and story fairness
- Characters have independent motives, knowledge limits, strengths, and flaws; they are not omniscient tools for delivering lore.
- Rarity, power, social status, and narrative importance are separate dimensions.
- Decisions should respond to information the character could reasonably have at the time.
- Do not reveal the correct answer in the question prompt or treat a reasonable interpretation as foolish solely because the player has not seen hidden information.
- Consequences and branching choices should follow the established situation, not an arbitrary hidden judgment.
- A recurring rule must be applied consistently, or a meaningful reason for the exception must be shown.

## 11. The unresolved-question rule
Before adding a major world rule, ask:
1. Is this already accepted somewhere?
2. Is it supported by an existing registry or story?
3. If not, is it clearly marked as a proposal or in-world belief?
4. Does it accidentally answer another open question?
5. Does it create new powers or exceptions that would change older stories?
6. What would a careful resident actually be able to observe or know?
7. Which dependent records need updating if the idea is accepted?

Do not resolve an open question by implication, repetition, artwork, a character's confident statement, or a convenient user-interface label.

## 12. Recommended order for completing the foundations
This order is a planning recommendation, not new canon:
1. **Physical world:** finish coastlines, relief, water, climate, biomes, and plausible routes.
2. **General rule boundaries:** record the ordinary natural assumptions and the types of exception the setting permits.
3. **Magic model:** decide access, practice, limits, costs or trade-offs if any, interactions, and what remains impossible—without forcing every phenomenon into one source.
4. **Life and creatures:** establish ecological principles and selected creature records.
5. **Time and technology:** define calendar needs and capability by period and region.
6. **Societies:** develop languages, institutions, law, beliefs, and political structures only where needed and with internal diversity.
7. **History and evidence:** connect selected events through supported causal paths.
8. **Implementation:** only then decide which accepted material belongs in Stories, World, character cards, collections, and gameplay.
