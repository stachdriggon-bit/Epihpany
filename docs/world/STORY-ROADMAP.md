# EPIPHANY — Story Roadmap and Lore Integration

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Editorial roadmap; story pitches below are proposals, not canon.  
**Purpose:** Connect the existing English-learning story library to the shared world without damaging useful learning content or silently inventing history.

## 1. Core decision

**Keep the current learning stories. Add a clearly identified world-story collection, then selectively connect or adapt existing stories only when the result is genuinely better.**

The current story library is primarily made of everyday, contemporary, workplace, school, and reasoning scenarios. These are useful for English practice, but they should not all be retroactively declared to have happened in EPIPHANY. Some can remain setting-neutral forever. The world should expand the learning experience, not force every exercise into one fictional continuity.

This roadmap does not authorize changing accepted lore, turning a provisional location into fixed canon, or treating a character/card description as objective history.

## 2. The story layers

Every lore-linked story should track three separate clocks:

1. **Event time:** when the events actually happen within the story world.
2. **In-world knowledge time:** when a character, community, institution, or later historian learns or records each relevant fact.
3. **Player discovery time:** when the learner encounters the story, clue, card, or Lore entry in the app.

The order in which stories are released does not have to match their in-world chronology. Before linking stories, check character age and presence, place names at the time, technology and knowledge available, and whether an event has already happened from each character's point of view.

Each story record should carry:
- Story ID and working title.
- CEFR level and learning goals.
- Event-time marker: date/range, relative period, or explicitly uncertain.
- Knowledge-time marker: what is known in-world at this point and by whom.
- Discovery-time/release position: when the learner sees it, including any prerequisite or spoiler warning.
- Region and stable place IDs, where applicable.
- Characters and their knowledge boundaries.
- What is established, what is evidence, what is interpretation, and what remains open.
- Related stories, World locations/quests, cards, and Lore entries.
- Comprehension questions whose answers are supported by the actual story text.

## 3. What to do with the current library

### Keep as learning-first stories

Keep existing stories as standalone or setting-neutral unless a review identifies a natural, continuity-safe connection. Their educational value does not depend on world lore.

Examples of content that should not be forcibly retconned:
- Everyday A1/A2 stories about keys, picnics, umbrellas, lunchboxes, libraries, hotels, deliveries, and gardens.
- Modern workplace and analytical scenarios involving software migrations, dashboards, automation proposals, public-service metrics, and contemporary policy.
- Stories whose central learning goal is communication, evidence, inference, or decision-making but whose present-day details do not fit the chosen world period.

### Use as thematic companions, not automatic canon

These existing stories have themes that could help prepare learners for later lore stories, but they are **not** thereby events in the world:
- exp-c1-archive-room — conflicting historical records and cautious interpretation.
- exp-c1-restoration — environmental observations and uneven recovery.
- exp-c2-evidence and exp-c2-policy-language — evidence quality and limits of claims.
- b1-quiet-lab — testing an assumption against observations.
- b2-data-gap and c2-missing-counterfactual — comparison and causal reasoning.

They can be linked as optional “skills that help with this mystery” only if the UI makes clear that they are learning companions rather than in-world historical records.

### When to revise an existing story

Revise an existing story only if all of the following are true:
1. It has a clear, useful connection to an established place, character, event, or phenomenon.
2. The change improves the story itself, not merely its lore label.
3. The new version remains suitable for its CEFR level and learning goal.
4. The location, technology, character knowledge, and chronology pass continuity review.
5. Its questions, answers, explanations, Arabic translations, and vocabulary are updated and rechecked together.

Otherwise, preserve it.

## 4. First proposed world-story arc

### Working title: The Two Records of Ambrin

**Status:** Pitch only — not approved canon.  
**Suggested level:** B1, with optional Arabic support.  
**Region:** Orath, eastern continent (current working map).  
**Candidate place:** Ambrin / أمبرين — OR-CITY-04, currently described in the settlement registry as an inland-shore town shaped by a fluctuating lake. The settlement's exact placement and details remain provisional; check the anchor-location registry before use and merge records if it is the same place as an existing anchor.  
**Time:** Leave the exact date and universal era open. Use a relative marker only after the timeline context is chosen.  
**New characters:** Do not reuse or imply an existing character. Create stable character records and approve their motivations, relationships, and knowledge limits before drafting the final story.

#### Core premise

Two local records describe the lake's level differently. People initially disagree about what the mismatch means. The characters compare when, where, and how the observations were made, then gather a fresh observation before recommending a practical response. The story may resolve the immediate question without proving a grand theory about the lake or the world's origins.

This premise grows from Ambrin's provisional settlement concept and the world's evidence rules. It does **not** establish that the lake is supernatural, that a conspiracy exists, or that the records reveal an ancient civilization.

#### Six-episode outline

1. **The Mismatch** — A discrepancy between two records affects a practical local decision. Learners identify the problem and distinguish a report from a conclusion.
2. **Who Wrote It?** — The characters trace the records' origins and learn what each record was intended to measure. The witnesses have different access and possible limitations, not automatically dishonest motives.
3. **The Shoreline** — A visit to the relevant observation points reveals that location and timing matter. The story avoids treating one visit as conclusive proof.
4. **A Different Explanation** — The characters compare plausible explanations, including ordinary variation and measurement differences. Unusual explanations remain hypotheses unless evidence supports them.
5. **One More Observation** — They collect or locate a comparable observation and state what it can and cannot establish.
6. **A Careful Decision** — The community chooses a proportionate practical action. The immediate issue reaches a satisfying outcome; any larger question remains open only if the evidence genuinely leaves it open.

Each episode should have a small, satisfying narrative turn, distinct English-learning goals, and comprehension questions that test what the episode actually establishes. Do not use the same “mystery question” six times. Avoid cliffhangers that withhold basic information solely to stretch the series.

#### Continuity and evidence ledger for this arc

Before drafting:
- Check OR-CITY-04 against ANCHOR-LOCATIONS.md and the Atlas; do not create a duplicate place.
- Decide whether the lake and its observation points are supported by the current map draft; do not infer scale, exact distances, or final hydrology from concept art.
- Define each record's author, purpose, date or relative date, measurement method, and limitations.
- Separate direct observations from witness recollections and later interpretations.
- Record which facts each character knows in each episode.
- Do not connect this event to a world-scale mystery unless independent evidence justifies that connection.
- Add character records before the story is treated as final canon.
- Track Arabic translations and question/answer order alongside the English source.

## 5. A gradual release plan

### Phase A — Prepare
1. Keep the existing story library intact.
2. Add story metadata for lore-linked stories only; do not require all old stories to receive lore metadata immediately.
3. Confirm the Ambrin/anchor relationship and the relevant map constraints.
4. Create the proposed story's character records and evidence ledger.

### Phase B — Pilot
1. Draft episode one at B1 and review it for story quality, lore continuity, and language level.
2. Draft all six episodes only after the pilot premise and character motivations work.
3. Audit every comprehension question against the text; verify answer indices, Arabic options, explanations, vocabulary, and translation-toggle behavior.
4. Add the story to the app only when the complete episode set and its metadata are ready. Do not publish placeholder lore or unfinished links.

### Phase C — Connect systems
1. Add a World location/quest only if it offers a real exploration interaction, not merely a story link.
2. Add cards only for characters, objects, places, or evidence with a useful identity and stable record.
3. Let the Lore archive distinguish established facts, physical evidence, interpretations, folklore, disputed accounts, and open questions.
4. Link follow-up stories only after their chronology and character knowledge have been checked.

### Phase D — Expand selectively
After the pilot, choose the next story from a different kind of everyday life or regional setting. Do not make every story a mystery, every location ancient, or every phenomenon connected. Keep local histories more common than world-scale events.

## 7. Acceptance checklist for every lore-linked story

- [ ] Its premise fits the approved world principles and does not resolve the world's ultimate origin.
- [ ] Every place reuses the correct stable ID; no duplicate settlement or anchor is introduced.
- [ ] Event time, in-world knowledge time, and player discovery time are distinct.
- [ ] Character motives, relationships, presence, and knowledge match their records.
- [ ] Evidence is separated from interpretation, folklore, and disputed claims.
- [ ] No phenomenon is declared supernatural or world-changing without support.
- [ ] The story works as a story, not just as an encyclopedia entry.
- [ ] CEFR level and learning goals are clear.
- [ ] Every question and explanation is justified by the story; answers do not leak into the prompt.
- [ ] English and Arabic prompts, options, answer indices, explanations, and vocabulary stay aligned.
- [ ] Links to World, cards, and Lore are meaningful and spoiler-aware.
- [ ] The status of every new lore claim is recorded as accepted, provisional, disputed, or open.
