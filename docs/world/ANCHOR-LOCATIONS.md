# EPIPHANY — Anchor Locations

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Provisional story-design concepts, not fixed canon.  
**Scope:** Two candidate anchor locations for each of the five principal regions. These are deliberately small in number: enough to test regional identity through stories, without naming every city or writing a complete gazetteer.

## How to use this document

Each location has a recognizable signature, an everyday life, a source of tension, and story potential across more than one era. These concepts do not establish a full civilization, dynasty, timeline, or final map position. Names may be revised. A story may also reveal that a location needs to change.

The time labels below are relative examples, not a finalized global chronology:
- **Earlier:** a story set before a later-known change.
- **Middle:** a period in which the place has an established role.
- **Later:** a story set after change has accumulated.
These labels do not imply that every region shares the same historical phases.

## Ilmar / إيلمار

### IL-LOC-01 — The Three-Arch Ferry
**Type:** River crossing and market stop  
**Signature:** A broad river, three old stone arches, ferry platforms for larger loads, and a market that grows on the banks on crossing days.

**Everyday life:** Ferrymen, mill workers, farmers, carriers, and traders depend on the river's changing level. People know which bank is muddy after rain and which boatman will wait for a late traveler.

**Tension:** The bridge is old and expensive to maintain. One community wants tolls to fund repairs; another argues that the crossing should remain free because it predates the current market.

**Across eras**
- **Earlier:** The arches are newly built, and the ferry operators resent losing part of their livelihood.
- **Middle:** The crossing becomes a dependable meeting place where news, rumors, and languages travel.
- **Later:** A new road diverts most traffic. The market shrinks, but the crossing becomes valuable to people seeking a quieter route.

**Story hooks:** A lost object passes through several hands; two witnesses remember the same event differently; a character must choose between a fast crossing and keeping a promise to the ferryman.

**Continuity anchors:** The river must be navigable or crossable at this point; seasonal floods affect access; any major bridge damage should persist until repaired.

### IL-LOC-02 — The Lanternwood Edge
**Type:** Forest settlement and woodland boundary  
**Signature:** A settlement built where cultivated land meets a large forest, with lanterns hung along a maintained path—not because the forest is inherently evil, but because fog and branching trails make it easy to lose one's way.

**Everyday life:** Woodcutters, herbalists, charcoal makers, guides, children who know local paths, and residents arguing over where the forest boundary should be.

**Tension:** A familiar route has become harder to follow. Some blame a change in the forest, others point to neglected markers, new growth, or an old path that was never mapped well.

**Across eras**
- **Earlier:** The settlement expands and marks a path into newly used woodland.
- **Middle:** The lantern route becomes a local tradition and a test of responsibility for young guides.
- **Later:** The forest has reclaimed abandoned plots. The old lantern posts are clues to former homes, not proof of a supernatural disaster.

**Story hooks:** A guide's confidence exceeds their knowledge; a family disagrees about leaving; a strange trail may have a natural, historical, or still-uncertain explanation.

**Continuity anchors:** Forest extent can change over time; avoid making every unusual event in the woods part of one mystery.

## Orath / أوراث

### OR-LOC-01 — The Switchback Gate
**Type:** Mountain pass settlement  
**Signature:** A road of broad switchbacks, a weather station maintained by local travelers, and a cluster of inns, workshops, and storage buildings at the last dependable stop before the climb.

**Everyday life:** Porters, mule or pack-animal handlers, repairers, innkeepers, guides, and officials who try to regulate traffic without knowing every condition on the mountain.

**Tension:** A new route promises to shorten the journey but may be unsafe in certain seasons. Guides disagree about the evidence; neither side needs to be foolish or dishonest.

**Across eras**
- **Earlier:** The pass is known mainly to local communities and a few traders.
- **Middle:** Regular traffic supports a settlement and makes the gate a place where news and techniques mix.
- **Later:** A different route draws away trade. The old gate survives as a specialist crossing and a repository of travel records.

**Story hooks:** A guide refuses a profitable journey; two maps show different safe seasons; a traveler discovers that an old warning was based on a specific event, not a universal rule.

**Continuity anchors:** Travel time depends on season and route condition; information about a distant event arrives only through plausible travelers or records.

### OR-LOC-02 — The Low-Water Shore
**Type:** Inland lake settlement  
**Signature:** A town built around a shallow, fluctuating lake, with old landing steps that sometimes end above the waterline and plots exposed during dry years.

**Everyday life:** Fishers when conditions allow, salt or mineral workers if the local geology supports it, boat repairers, traders, and households that remember different lake levels.

**Tension:** The lake has receded. One group believes this is an ordinary dry cycle; another argues that recent records show a meaningful long-term shift. Evidence exists, but the records cover different time spans and were kept for different purposes.

**Across eras**
- **Earlier:** The settlement forms around a more reliable shoreline.
- **Middle:** The lake's variation becomes part of local planning and folk memory.
- **Later:** Old landing steps and household records help reconstruct how the shore changed, though they do not answer every question.

**Story hooks:** A newly exposed structure is mistaken for proof of a legend; a record keeper discovers that two calendars were compared incorrectly; a family debates whether to move.

**Continuity anchors:** The lake's water balance must remain plausible; do not assume it is a world-defining anomaly simply because it fluctuates.

## Tharima / ثاريما

### TH-LOC-01 — The Wadi Market
**Type:** Seasonal river-channel market  
**Signature:** A market arranged above the normal channel of a broad wadi, with routes and temporary stalls adapted to brief periods of flow.

**Everyday life:** Carriers, farmers from nearby cultivable patches, livestock traders, water managers, craftspeople, and visitors who must learn local rules before setting up a stall.

**Tension:** A proposed shortcut or new structure may alter how water reaches downstream users. The dispute is about evidence, obligations, and trust—not simply a contest between good and bad people.

**Across eras**
- **Earlier:** A small seasonal gathering grows around a dependable travel window.
- **Middle:** The market becomes a regional meeting place whose dates depend on weather and local agreements.
- **Later:** A new road reduces some traffic, while the market remains important for local exchange and annual gatherings.

**Story hooks:** A message arrives during a brief flood; a traveler mistakes an empty channel for a safe road; a character must settle a dispute using incomplete records.

**Continuity anchors:** A wadi can be dry for long periods and still flood; any permanent settlement needs water sources beyond wishful thinking.

### TH-LOC-02 — The Spring Steps
**Type:** Upland settlement and spring-fed terraces  
**Signature:** A series of terraces below a spring or spring complex, connected by narrow steps and shared water channels.

**Everyday life:** Gardeners, repairers of channels, families who maintain water-sharing rules, and travelers who stop to rest before crossing the uplands.

**Tension:** The spring's flow has weakened or shifted. Residents disagree whether the cause is seasonal variation, damage to the catchment, a change in use upstream, or something they do not yet understand.

**Across eras**
- **Earlier:** The first terraces are built around the known water source.
- **Middle:** Water-sharing customs and maintenance duties become central to community life.
- **Later:** Some terraces are abandoned or repurposed; old channel designs preserve evidence of earlier conditions.

**Story hooks:** A young caretaker finds an overlooked leak; an inherited water agreement no longer fits the population; a mystery remains open until enough observations accumulate.

**Continuity anchors:** The spring must have a plausible recharge area; do not treat all of Tharima as uniformly dry or all its uplands as water-rich.

## Nirvan / نيرفان

### NI-LOC-01 — The Last Warm Dock
**Type:** Northern coastal harbor  
**Signature:** A sheltered dock where ships make final repairs before exposed northern waters, with a public board of weather notes that is practical rather than perfectly predictive.

**Everyday life:** Fishers, shipwrights, cooks, families who provision journeys, and visitors waiting for a safe departure window.

**Tension:** A season's weather differs from local expectations. Experienced sailors disagree because their knowledge comes from different routes and decades, not because one side is automatically incompetent.

**Across eras**
- **Earlier:** The dock is a small local refuge used by a handful of crews.
- **Middle:** Repeated voyages make it a trusted staging point for northern travel.
- **Later:** Improved routes and vessels reduce its role, but local records help travelers understand older conditions.

**Story hooks:** A ship arrives with an unfamiliar logbook; a repairer recognizes a flaw that others overlook; a departure delayed by weather creates an unexpected friendship or conflict.

**Continuity anchors:** The coast is cold but not permanently frozen; travel conditions vary seasonally, and the harbor's shelter has a geographic reason.

### NI-LOC-02 — The Pine Ledger
**Type:** Forest community and record house  
**Signature:** A settlement in a forest belt where families keep practical records of timber use, storms, animal movements, and boundary agreements on durable wooden tablets and paper where available.

**Everyday life:** Foresters, builders, record keepers, craftspeople, and residents who disagree about how much of the forest can be used without harming future livelihoods.

**Tension:** A set of old records appears to contradict current boundaries. The difference may reflect a changed forest edge, an old surveying convention, or a past agreement that was never universally accepted.

**Across eras**
- **Earlier:** Records are kept to coordinate shared work and avoid disputes.
- **Middle:** The ledger becomes an important local archive, though not an infallible one.
- **Later:** Researchers use it to reconstruct changes in land use, while some entries remain ambiguous.

**Story hooks:** A missing page matters to two families; a character discovers that a record is accurate but describes a different boundary system; a later story revisits a place whose forest has changed.

**Continuity anchors:** Keep the forest within plausible temperature and moisture ranges; records preserve viewpoints and conventions as well as facts.

## Azmar / أزمار

### AZ-LOC-01 — The Seven-Mooring Harbor
**Type:** Island harbor and passage junction  
**Signature:** A compact harbor with seven traditional mooring places, each associated with a different wind or vessel type; the number is a local convention, not a magical law.

**Everyday life:** Pilots, ferry crews, merchants, repairers, translators, and households accustomed to news arriving from several directions.

**Tension:** A channel marker is missing or misplaced. Several crews report different hazards because they sailed at different tides, drafts, or weather conditions.

**Across eras**
- **Earlier:** The moorings are informal and known mostly to local pilots.
- **Middle:** The harbor becomes a reliable transfer point between island groups.
- **Later:** Larger vessels use another route, but the harbor remains essential for smaller boats and local connections.

**Story hooks:** A supposedly obvious safe passage proves unsafe for a different vessel; an old pilot's notes resolve part of a dispute; a character has to decide whether to trust an official chart or local experience.

**Continuity anchors:** Navigation depends on tide, depth, vessel, and weather; harbor activity requires plausible freshwater and supplies.

### AZ-LOC-02 — The Rain-Cistern Island
**Type:** Small island with limited freshwater  
**Signature:** Roofs and stone catchments feed communal cisterns; the island's silhouette is defined by a high ridge and a sheltered landing rather than a giant monument.

**Everyday life:** Cistern keepers, gardeners, boat repairers, visiting crews, and families who know the limits of the island's stored water.

**Tension:** A damaged cistern threatens the next dry spell. The island must choose how to ration water and whether to rely on a visiting vessel whose arrival is uncertain.

**Across eras**
- **Earlier:** The cistern system is built after residents learn that a nearby harbor does not guarantee drinking water.
- **Middle:** Maintenance becomes a shared duty and a source of occasional resentment.
- **Later:** A new water source or trade route changes the settlement, but the old cisterns remain evidence of earlier constraints.

**Story hooks:** A missing maintenance record; a disagreement about who has priority; a traveler discovers that a modest engineering solution matters more than a rumored treasure.

**Continuity anchors:** Freshwater supply must match rainfall, storage, population, and trade; island ecosystems and livelihoods vary across Azmar.

## Cross-era continuity: a lightweight rule

For any story using one of these locations, record only:
- location ID;
- period or relative date (or “uncertain”);
- which version/state of the location appears;
- characters present and their life stage;
- one or two changes that must carry into later stories.

A location can evolve through ordinary causes: trade shifts, maintenance, migration, changing family fortunes, environmental variation, rebuilding, or new knowledge. Not every change needs a mystery or a catastrophe.

## Selection rule

These ten concepts are candidates, not a demand to use all of them. Promote a location into established canon when a story, World area, character, or card benefits from it. If an idea feels generic when written into a scene, revise it through the lives and choices of its inhabitants rather than adding more geography.

## Next step

Test a small number of these locations with short story premises in different eras. Only then decide which deserve stable names, detailed maps, recurring characters, or deeper historical links.
