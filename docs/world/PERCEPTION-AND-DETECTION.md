# Perception and Detection — Exploratory Note

**Status:** Exploratory note only. This is not canon and does not establish a formal sorcery branch, a universal progression, or any character's ability. The former branch-first framework has been superseded; use [Foundational Sorcery Model](MAGIC-FOUNDATIONS.md) as the current working foundation. Keep all proposals here provisional unless explicitly approved.

## Purpose

Explore whether **Perception and Detection** is a useful description for some abilities, without assuming it is an official branch or fixed operation family. Build only the useful foundation; return to edge cases when a story or character requires them.

## Current working model

Possible operations to keep in view:

- **Presence sensing:** Detect that a magical trace or presence exists without necessarily knowing its exact location, nature, or source.
- **Locating:** Narrow down or identify the location of a detected trace or target. A current hypothesis treats this as a more advanced use of sensing, not necessarily a separate operation.
- **Nature analysis:** Infer the type or nature of a magical effect without necessarily knowing who created it, how it works in full, or being able to cancel or alter it. Currently treated as a possible advanced stage of sensing.
- **Detecting concealed targets:** Detection may depend on the concealment method and on the interaction between the concealing sorcerer's power and the perceiving sorcerer's ability. No outcome is guaranteed by power alone.
- **Detecting targets without an obvious magical trace:** This may be an extension of the same broad domain, depending on the perceiver's power and the nature of the target. It is not established that every perceiver can do this.
- **Reading a target's condition or details:** Potentially a more advanced level than detecting the target itself. Detecting a person does not automatically reveal identity, awareness, injury, possessions, or other details.

These are candidate examples, not mandatory sequential stages or a fixed taxonomy. A sorcerer may be able to perform some and not others; this remains open.

## Boundaries to preserve

- Perceiving or detecting something does **not** automatically grant the ability to control, remove, cancel, overcome, or otherwise affect it.
- Detecting a target does not necessarily reveal its identity, condition, intentions, possessions, or history.
- Detecting a magical trace does not necessarily identify its source or reconstruct the event that produced it.
- Failure to detect something does not by itself prove that the concealment is stronger; the perceiver's ability, restrictions, the target, conditions, and the detection method may matter.
- Strength is a contextual factor, not a guarantee of success. Outcomes may depend on the interaction between both sorcerers, the method used, and the target.
- Do not assume every individual restriction maps directly to this domain or automatically explains a failed detection.

## Open questions — do not resolve in advance

- Is Perception and Detection a distinct domain, a branch, a set of operations distributed among existing branches, or a mixed model?
- Are locating and nature analysis advanced uses of sensing, or can they be independent capabilities?
- What kinds of concealment can be detected, and under what conditions?
- Can a sorcerer detect a target that leaves no detectable magical signal?
- How much information can be learned about a detected target?
- Can traces support limited inferences about past events, or would that require a different ability? This has not yet been tested or accepted.

## Test record summary

1. **Magical trace present, exact location/nature/source unknown:** test interpretation — presence sensing as a distinct operation.
2. **Trace located behind a wall, nature/function unknown:** test interpretation — locating as a more advanced stage of sensing.
3. **Trace identified as a concealment spell, caster unknown and spell not countered:** test interpretation — nature analysis as a more advanced stage.
4. **Person magically concealed behind a wall and detected without being seen or identified:** test interpretation — detectability depends on concealment type and the relative capabilities of both sorcerers.
5. **Person behind a wall with no obvious magical trace:** test interpretation — direct target detection may extend the same domain, depending on the perceiver and target.
6. **Person located precisely, but their condition and possessions remain unknown:** test interpretation — reading condition/details may be more advanced than detecting the target.

All six are hypothetical test cases, not canonical events or confirmed mechanics. The possible inference of past events from magical residue remains an untested question, not a seventh accepted operation.

## Working method

Keep this note concise and expand it only when a character, scene, or consistency problem makes a detail necessary. When testing a case, record the scenario, provisional interpretation, observation, and unresolved question separately. Do not convert a hypothesis into canon without explicit approval.
