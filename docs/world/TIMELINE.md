# EPIPHANY — Timeline Framework

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Framework only; no complete world chronology has been established.

## 0. Source records

Use [Master Event Registry](EVENT-REGISTRY.md) as the stable event index and [Local History Register](LOCAL-HISTORIES.md) to develop the history of each place. This framework is not itself a complete timeline; event records remain the source for status, evidence, place IDs, and time confidence. The six Low-Water Shore pilot entries are provisional story-linked records, not proof of a dated historical era.

## 1. Chronology principles

- The world's ultimate beginning remains unknown.
- The earliest surviving records do not prove the beginning of the world or civilization.
- Different regions may enter comparable historical phases at different times.
- Archaeological layers, oral traditions, written records, and later interpretations have different reliability.
- Dates should be exact only when the evidence supports exactness; otherwise use ranges or relative ordering.
- Do not make every regional event part of a single global catastrophe or master mystery.

## 2. Provisional broad periods

These are organizing labels, not finalized universal eras.

1. **Scattered Times / الأزمنة المتفرقة:** communities and local traditions before extensive reliable records; evidence may be fragmentary.
2. **First Cities / المدن الأولى:** urban centers, specialized labor, and varied record-keeping emerge in some regions.
3. **Interwoven Routes / الطرق المتشابكة:** trade, migration, diplomacy, warfare, and knowledge transfer connect more regions.
4. **Age of Rebalancing / زمن إعادة التوازن:** regional power shifts caused by varied combinations of politics, economics, migration, conflict, and environmental change. This is not necessarily one synchronized world event.
5. **Age of Contested Exploration / عصر الاستكشاف المتنازع عليه:** a possible current-period label for renewed exploration, reopened routes, and disputes over new evidence. Its status and exact scope remain open.

Do not assume these periods begin or end simultaneously across the world.

## 3. Event record template

For each major event, record:
- Event ID and working name.
- Date or date range, with confidence.
- Location and geographic context.
- Direct causes and contributing conditions.
- Participants and their motives.
- Immediate consequences and longer-term effects.
- Evidence and its provenance.
- Competing accounts or interpretations.
- Status: accepted, provisional, disputed, or in-world claim.
- Stories, characters, places, cards, and routes affected.

## 4. Event categories

Possible categories include trade-route openings, water or environmental crises, historical corrections, state fragmentation or federation, migration, war, technological or knowledge transfer, and changes in documented phenomena.

These are categories, not events already established as canon.

## 5. Continuity check

Before adding an event, check whether its travel times, climate, resources, political borders, technologies, and information routes fit the relevant period. If an old source contradicts a modern account, preserve the discrepancy and evaluate the evidence rather than silently rewriting either source.


## 6. Stories across different times

EPIPHANY stories may take place in different periods, and their release order does not have to match in-world chronology. Each story should carry a lightweight time marker: a known date or range when useful, a relative period when exact dating is unsupported, or an explicitly uncertain placement. Before linking stories, check only the continuity that matters: character age and life stage, who is alive or present, political and place names in that period, available technology and knowledge, and whether an event has already happened from that character's perspective. Earlier-set stories may reveal context for later stories, but must not accidentally contradict established events. Preserve mysteries and conflicting historical accounts where intentional. Do not force every story into one master plot or complete a detailed chronology before stories need it.
