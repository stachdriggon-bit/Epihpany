# EPIPHANY — Continental and Regional Identities

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Creative direction / provisional. These are story-generating concepts, not fixed canon.  
**Purpose:** Give each major region a recognizable feel without overbuilding geography, cultures, or history before stories need them.

## Design rule

A continent is not a single culture, climate, personality, or plot. Each should contain contrasts: busy and quiet places, old and new communities, ordinary concerns and unusual discoveries. The concepts below are starting points, not restrictions. A region can host comedy, romance, mystery, conflict, everyday life, or adventure; it should not be trapped in one genre.

Names and broad placements are provisional:
- **Ilmar / إيلمار** — western continent.
- **Orath / أوراث** — eastern continent.
- **Tharima / ثاريما** — southern continent.
- **Nirvan / نيرفان** — northern continent.
- **Azmar / أزمار** — scattered archipelagos and maritime passages.

## 1. Ilmar / إيلمار — Crossroads of familiar lives

**First impression:** Temperate coasts, broad lowlands, wooded uplands, river routes, and settlements linked by roads and waterways.

**Possible regional contrasts**
- **Western Coast:** Working harbors, fishing towns, ship repair, sailors' superstitions, and families whose lives follow the tides.
- **Central Forest Belt:** Villages, old paths, forest work, hidden clearings, local customs, and disagreements about how the woods should be used.
- **Ilan Lowlands:** Farms, river crossings, markets, mills, flood memories, and competition over access to fertile land.
- **Open Plains:** Long journeys, seasonal gatherings, roadside inns, animal herding, and communities spread far apart.
- **Southern Transition:** Drier landscapes, mixed livelihoods, and routes connecting different ecological zones.

**Story opportunities:** A missing parcel that matters to two rival families; a ferry keeper who knows everyone's secrets; a forest guide who refuses to explain one old trail; a local festival whose rules have changed across generations.

**Avoid reducing Ilmar to:** A generic peaceful kingdom, or the default home of every important character.

## 2. Orath / أوراث — Passages, heights, and contested knowledge

**First impression:** A varied continent shaped by highlands, mountain passes, western-facing valleys, an inland basin, and an exposed eastern coast.

**Possible regional contrasts**
- **Western Shores:** Port towns, translators, traders, and travelers carrying ideas as well as goods.
- **Great Highlands:** Isolated valleys beside well-traveled passes; distinct local practices rather than one uniform mountain culture.
- **Eastern Spine:** Difficult crossings, high settlements, weather-dependent journeys, and people whose livelihoods depend on knowing the terrain.
- **Inland Basin:** Communities adapting to a lake whose level may fluctuate; practical arguments over water, records, and the meaning of environmental change.
- **Eastern Valleys and Coast:** Exposed shores, river mouths, workshops, and settlements looking outward across the ocean.

**Story opportunities:** Two maps that disagree for understandable reasons; a guide who knows a safer route but has personal reasons not to reveal it; a lake-side community divided over whether to move; a discovery that changes the accepted history of a pass.

**Avoid reducing Orath to:** A continent of mysterious scholars, mountains, or one all-explaining ancient secret.

## 3. Tharima / ثاريما — Water, distance, and adaptation

**First impression:** Dry interiors, semi-arid northern transitions, broken uplands, seasonal channels, basins, and coasts where water is precious but local conditions vary.

**Possible regional contrasts**
- **Northern Steppe:** Herding, farming where feasible, market stops, and communities accustomed to changing seasons.
- **Interior Drylands:** Long distances, specialized knowledge of routes, and varied ways of making a living—not empty land.
- **Basin Communities:** Settlements whose plans depend on uncertain rainfall, local water agreements, and memories of unusually wet or dry years.
- **Upland Refuges:** Cooler pockets, springs where recharge supports them, and routes between ecological zones.
- **Coastal Belt:** Harbors, salt or coastal trades, maritime connections, and sharp differences between neighboring stretches of coast.
- **Wadi Corridors:** Brief floods, fertile patches, travel windows, and disputes over how to maintain shared channels.

**Story opportunities:** A guide must decide whether to take the short route through a dry channel; a community preserves an old water-sharing agreement; an apparent miracle has several plausible explanations; a traveler learns that local hospitality comes with obligations.

**Avoid reducing Tharima to:** Endless sand, a single desert culture, or a place where every plot is about scarcity.

## 4. Nirvan / نيرفان — Seasonal knowledge and sheltered lives

**First impression:** Cold northern coasts, broken highlands, forests at suitable elevations, glacial valleys in selected areas, and milder southern routes.

**Possible regional contrasts**
- **Northern Coasts:** Fishing, weather-reading, close-knit settlements, and journeys planned around sea conditions.
- **Highlands:** Seasonal travel, isolated communities, and practical expertise in terrain and weather.
- **Forest Belt:** Timber work, hunting or gathering where appropriate, local crafts, and disputes over resources.
- **Southern Routes:** Cross-regional trade, inns, translators, and communities that act as meeting points between different ways of life.
- **Fjord and Maritime Valleys:** Sheltered harbors in some places, difficult access in others; not every northern coast is a fjord.

**Story opportunities:** A seasonal route opens earlier than expected; a family maintains a warning system nobody outside the valley understands; a visitor mistakes quietness for hostility; an old expedition's records are reinterpreted by a new generation.

**Avoid reducing Nirvan to:** Permanent snow, grim people, or a uniformly isolated northern realm.

## 5. Azmar / أزمار — Islands with different horizons

Azmar is a collection of island groups, not one homogeneous island nation. Freshwater, soils, exposure, and access vary from island to island.

- **Inner Passages:** Busy crossings, pilots, ferries, local rules, and competing claims about safe navigation.
- **Harbor Islands:** Repair yards, markets, multilingual households, visiting crews, and news arriving from several directions.
- **Remote Islands:** Distinct local traditions, limited resources, unusual natural histories, and occasional contact rather than automatic isolation.
- **Windward and Leeward Shores:** Different weather and livelihoods on opposite sides of the same island.
- **Low Islands and High Islands:** Different risks, water sources, crops, building practices, and travel needs.

**Story opportunities:** A sailor who recognizes a supposedly uninhabited island; a dispute over who maintains a channel marker; a community whose most valuable resource is knowledge rather than a rare object; a character caught between island loyalties and a wider maritime network.

**Avoid reducing Azmar to:** A single pirate culture, a chain of tropical paradises, or a convenient place to hide every mystery.

## 6. Make places memorable without over-documenting them

When a story needs a specific location, define only the details that affect the experience:

1. **Signature:** One or two immediately recognizable features.
2. **Everyday life:** What people do, need, trade, celebrate, or worry about.
3. **Tension:** A local disagreement, constraint, change, or unanswered question.
4. **Connection:** Why someone from elsewhere might arrive, leave, or care.
5. **Time marker:** When the story happens, at whatever precision is justified.
6. **Continuity note:** Only facts that later stories must respect.

Not every location needs a mystery, anomaly, ancient ruin, or world-changing event. Some places should simply feel lived in.

## 7. Stories across eras

- Story release order and in-world chronology may differ.
- A place may look different across eras because of ordinary change: growth, neglect, migration, rebuilding, changing routes, or environmental shifts.
- A character may return at another life stage, but age, relationships, knowledge, and consequences must remain coherent.
- Later stories may reveal missing context without declaring every earlier account false.
- Use a precise date only when useful and supportable; otherwise use a period, a relative marker, or “date uncertain.”
- Do not invent a complete timeline merely to justify a single scene.

## 8. When to promote an idea into canon

Keep an idea provisional until it is used by a story, game area, character, or meaningful continuity link. Before locking it in, ask:
- Does it make this place more distinct?
- Does it create a useful story or play opportunity?
- Does it conflict with an established fact?
- Is the detail worth preserving, or can it remain flexible?

## 9. Next practical step

Choose a small set of **anchor locations**—one or two per continent at most—to test these identities through actual story concepts. Do not name every city or define every culture yet. Let the places earn further detail through use.
