# EPIPHANY — Communities and Local Institutions

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Design framework; the roles below are grounded in existing anchor tensions but are not finalized institutions, named organizations, laws, or political systems.  
**Purpose:** Give local history believable human mechanisms. Communities change through maintenance, agreements, livelihoods, access, records, and competing needs before they are connected by regional powers or world-scale events.

## 1. Core principles

- Start with a practical need already present in a location concept. Do not create an institution merely to make the setting look complex.
- Distinguish a **community** (people who share a place, livelihood, risk, or obligation) from an **institution** (a recurring rule, role, meeting, record, or procedure used to coordinate them).
- Institutions can be informal, temporary, family-based, shared across several groups, or contested. Not every place needs a formal government office.
- Give every rule a purpose, a cost, someone responsible for maintaining it, and a way it can fail.
- Avoid reducing a region to one culture or a whole people to a single occupation.
- Do not name a guild, council, dynasty, religion, law code, or political state until a story or worldbuilding decision needs it.
- Local institutions may have different customs even when they solve similar problems. Similar function does not prove common origin.
- Record who is included, who is excluded, who pays, who benefits, and whose evidence is trusted.
- A local dispute need not have a villain. Conflicts can arise from scarcity, different time horizons, unequal risk, inherited agreements, or incomplete information.
- These are story-design prompts. They do not establish a historical event until a dated or relative event record is created and its status is clear.

## 3. Location-grounded institutional seeds

Each entry below is a **functional seed**, not a claim that a formal organization already exists. Keep the name descriptive until the setting needs a proper name.

### INST-IL-01 — Crossing maintenance and toll arrangement

- **Status:** Proposed from the anchor concept
- **Place ID:** IL-LOC-01 — The Three-Arch Ferry
- **Practical need:** Maintain a bridge and ferry facilities while preserving access for people who depend on the crossing.
- **Participants:** Bridge repairers, ferrymen, mill workers, farmers, carriers, traders, and people who cross without selling goods.
- **Possible responsibilities:** Inspect the arches, schedule repairs, close unsafe access after floods, coordinate ferry service, and account for collected tolls if tolls are adopted.
- **Unresolved design choice:** One community wants tolls to pay for maintenance; another argues that the crossing should remain free because it predates the current market. Neither policy is yet the accepted outcome.
- **Failure mode:** Repairs are delayed, the toll burden falls unevenly, or a route change leaves some residents paying for infrastructure they rarely use.
- **Historical change seed:** Ferry operators lose some work when the bridge is built; later, a new road diverts traffic and changes the maintenance bargain.
- **Open questions:** Who owns or maintains the crossing, whether tolls are legal or customary, how flood closures are decided, and whether IL-CITY-04 Ilanford is this same place.
- **Source:** `ANCHOR-LOCATIONS.md`; `CITIES-ILMAR.md`; `LOCAL-HISTORIES.md`.

### INST-OR-01 — Route safety notes and seasonal guidance

- **Status:** Proposed from the anchor concept
- **Place ID:** OR-LOC-01 — The Switchback Gate
- **Practical need:** Share route conditions when weather and terrain make a shorter path potentially unsafe.
- **Participants:** Guides, porters, pack-animal handlers, repairers, innkeepers, officials, and travelers.
- **Possible responsibilities:** Record weather observations, note damaged sections, identify seasonal hazards, and distinguish direct observations from second-hand reports.
- **Unresolved design choice:** The weather station is maintained by local travelers in the anchor concept, but the record does not specify a formal office or central authority.
- **Failure mode:** A warning becomes outdated, a report from one route is generalized to another, or officials regulate traffic using incomplete local knowledge.
- **Historical change seed:** Local route knowledge becomes more organized as regular traffic grows; after a competing route gains importance, records remain useful to specialist travelers.
- **Open questions:** Who may post a warning, how observations are dated, how disputes are reviewed, and which route later competes with the pass.
- **Source:** `ANCHOR-LOCATIONS.md`; `LOCAL-HISTORIES.md`; `GEOGRAPHIC-FOUNDATION.md`.

### INST-TH-01 — Wadi-market water and access agreement

- **Status:** Proposed from the anchor concept
- **Place ID:** TH-LOC-01 — The Wadi Market
- **Practical need:** Coordinate market access and protect downstream users when a shortcut or new structure could change water flow.
- **Participants:** Market sellers, carriers, nearby farmers, livestock traders, water managers, craftspeople, and downstream users.
- **Possible responsibilities:** Announce safe market windows, explain temporary access rules, hear downstream concerns, and record the reason for a structural change.
- **Unresolved design choice:** The market's dates depend on weather and local agreements, but the decision-makers and enforcement method are not established.
- **Failure mode:** A dry channel is mistaken for a safe route, a structure shifts water without consultation, or visiting traders misunderstand local obligations.
- **Historical change seed:** A dependable travel window supports a small gathering; a later road reduces some traffic without eliminating local exchange.
- **Open questions:** Water rights, catchment boundaries, emergency procedures during a flash flood, and whether the market is governed by custom or a named settlement.
- **Source:** `ANCHOR-LOCATIONS.md`; `LOCAL-HISTORIES.md`; `GEOGRAPHIC-FOUNDATION.md`.

### INST-TH-02 — Spring maintenance and water-sharing routine

- **Status:** Proposed from the anchor concept
- **Place ID:** TH-LOC-02 — The Spring Steps
- **Practical need:** Share a limited spring supply and maintain terraces and channels.
- **Participants:** Gardeners, channel repairers, households, caretakers, and downstream users.
- **Possible responsibilities:** Set a maintenance rota, identify leaks, agree on temporary restrictions, and record when water is unusually low.
- **Unresolved design choice:** An inherited agreement may no longer fit the population, but its terms and who can revise it remain open.
- **Failure mode:** Duties fall on the same households repeatedly, a leak is blamed on the wrong cause, or old allocation rules ignore a changed water supply.
- **Historical change seed:** Early terrace construction establishes shared work; later population and flow changes test whether the old arrangement still works.
- **Open questions:** Source recharge, seasonal flow, who can change the agreement, and how newcomers gain access.
- **Source:** `ANCHOR-LOCATIONS.md`; `LOCAL-HISTORIES.md`; `GEOGRAPHIC-FOUNDATION.md`.

### INST-NI-01 — Harbor weather board and departure practice

- **Status:** Proposed from the anchor concept
- **Place ID:** NI-LOC-01 — The Last Warm Dock
- **Practical need:** Help crews compare weather observations before departing into more exposed northern waters.
- **Participants:** Fishers, shipwrights, provisioning families, ship crews, and travelers.
- **Possible responsibilities:** Date observations, distinguish conditions seen locally from reports brought by a vessel, and preserve older voyage notes.
- **Unresolved design choice:** The public board is practical, not perfectly predictive; no formal harbor authority or forecasting system is established.
- **Failure mode:** An old note is treated as a guarantee, or experience from one route is applied to a different season or coastline.
- **Historical change seed:** Repeated voyages increase the dock's importance; improved vessels and routes later reduce its role.
- **Open questions:** Who maintains the board, how reports are checked, and what safety decisions remain with each captain.
- **Source:** `ANCHOR-LOCATIONS.md`; `LOCAL-HISTORIES.md`; `GEOGRAPHIC-FOUNDATION.md`.

### INST-NI-02 — Shared forest records and boundary review

- **Status:** Proposed from the anchor concept
- **Place ID:** NI-LOC-02 — The Pine Ledger
- **Practical need:** Coordinate timber use, storm records, animal observations, and agreements over changing forest boundaries.
- **Participants:** Foresters, builders, craftspeople, record keepers, families, and later researchers.
- **Possible responsibilities:** Preserve entries, explain the surveying conventions used, record amendments without erasing earlier versions, and distinguish an agreed boundary from a universally accepted one.
- **Unresolved design choice:** The archive becomes important locally, but its access rules and authority are open.
- **Failure mode:** A correct entry is misread outside its original convention, a missing page is treated as proof of wrongdoing, or one family's records are mistaken for a neutral view.
- **Historical change seed:** Practical records begin as coordination tools; later researchers use them to reconstruct land use while some entries remain ambiguous.
- **Open questions:** Who can add or copy records, how disputes are recorded, and what materials and writing systems fit each period.
- **Source:** `ANCHOR-LOCATIONS.md`; `LOCAL-HISTORIES.md`.

### INST-AZ-01 — Pilot knowledge and channel-marker upkeep

- **Status:** Proposed from the anchor concept
- **Place ID:** AZ-LOC-01 — The Seven-Mooring Harbor
- **Practical need:** Keep a passage usable for different vessel drafts, tides, and weather conditions.
- **Participants:** Local pilots, ferry crews, merchants, repairers, translators, and visiting crews.
- **Possible responsibilities:** Maintain channel markers, record the conditions under which a route is safe, and compare local pilot notes with official charts.
- **Unresolved design choice:** Seven moorings are a local convention, not a magical law. No formal pilot guild or island-wide authority is established.
- **Failure mode:** A marker is misplaced, or one crew's experience is treated as universally applicable to every vessel.
- **Historical change seed:** Informal local knowledge supports the early harbor; later traffic between island groups makes it a transfer point; larger vessels eventually shift to another route.
- **Open questions:** Marker maintenance, freshwater and supply arrangements, authority over channel notices, and the actual island groups connected.
- **Source:** `ANCHOR-LOCATIONS.md`; `LOCAL-HISTORIES.md`; `GEOGRAPHIC-FOUNDATION.md`.

### INST-AZ-02 — Cistern maintenance and water rationing

- **Status:** Proposed from the anchor concept
- **Place ID:** AZ-LOC-02 — The Rain-Cistern Island
- **Practical need:** Maintain shared rainwater storage and allocate water through a dry spell.
- **Participants:** Cistern keepers, gardeners, boat repairers, households, and visiting crews.
- **Possible responsibilities:** Inspect catchments, record repairs, estimate stored water, set transparent rationing priorities, and plan for a delayed supply vessel.
- **Unresolved design choice:** The island has shared cisterns in the concept, but the ownership, rationing rules, and emergency authority remain open.
- **Failure mode:** A damaged catchment goes unnoticed, storage estimates are wrong, or visiting crews and residents disagree about priorities.
- **Historical change seed:** Residents build storage after learning that harbor access does not guarantee drinking water; a later water source or trade route may alter the arrangement.
- **Open questions:** Rainfall, cistern capacity, population, dry-season duration, and how rationing decisions can be appealed.
- **Source:** `ANCHOR-LOCATIONS.md`; `LOCAL-HISTORIES.md`; `GEOGRAPHIC-FOUNDATION.md`.

## 4. Connections between communities: evidence required

The following are possible mechanisms for future regional history, not established links:

- **Trade:** Require a viable route, travel time, goods that can be transported, and a reason both sides participate.
- **Migration:** Record the pressure or opportunity, the people moving, the route, and how receiving communities respond.
- **Shared technical practice:** Show how knowledge travels—apprenticeship, copied records, a traveling craftsperson, captured equipment, or deliberate teaching. Similar inventions alone do not prove contact.
- **Political influence:** Establish what a distant authority can realistically reach, tax, protect, or enforce. Do not let borders on a map imply effective control everywhere.
- **Conflict:** Define the disputed resource or obligation, each side's goals, the available alternatives, and consequences for ordinary people. Avoid making war the default engine of history.
- **Environmental change:** Distinguish a local observation from a regional trend; require comparable records before asserting a common cause.
- **Shared records:** Track who copied a document, when it traveled, and whether later readers understood its original units and conventions.

Before creating a cross-regional event, add the event to `EVENT-REGISTRY.md`, reference the involved location IDs, and state the evidence and travel/communication mechanism. If the connection is only a hypothesis, mark it as such.

## 5. Next development order

1. Check anchor-to-city overlaps in each regional registry; retain stable IDs and do not duplicate a place.
2. Choose two or three local institutions for actual stories, rather than activating every seed at once.
3. Draft a small, concrete event for each chosen institution and add a stable event ID only after the event has source text.
4. Test the event's relative date, character knowledge, geographic plausibility, and later consequences.
5. Connect two regions only after a documented route, migration, shared record, or other mechanism exists.
6. Keep local histories and the world timeline synchronized by references, not copied event descriptions.
