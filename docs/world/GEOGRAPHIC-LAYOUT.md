# EPIPHANY — Relative Geographic Layout Worksheet

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Working coordinate framework, not a finished map.  
**Purpose:** Give future coastline, relief, drainage, and climate sketches a shared spatial reference without inventing exact distances.

## 1. How to use this worksheet

Use a simple north-up grid with 12 columns (A–L) and 8 rows (1–8). Each cell represents a relative planning area, **not a fixed number of kilometres**. A feature may span several cells. Cell assignments are approximate anchors only; coastline shapes and exact positions remain provisional.

- North is at the top; west is left; east is right.
- Do not treat cell boundaries as real borders or geographic lines.
- This grid is a planning aid for relative placement, not a projection and not a scale map.
- A feature should be moved if a later physical consistency check exposes a contradiction. Record substantial moves in the decision log.
- No civilization, political boundary, city, or historical event is canonized by this worksheet.

## 5. Climate overlays to test

These are qualitative patterns, not finalized wind arrows or rainfall contours:

- **Coolest overall:** Nirvan, especially higher northern/northwestern relief. Southern lowlands can be milder.
- **Temperate tendency:** Ilmar, with wetter coastal/upland pockets and a gradual drier transition in some inland/southern areas.
- **Strong local variation:** Orath, where elevation, exposure, and rain shadow can change over short distances.
- **Driest interior tendency:** Tharima, with a semi-arid northern transition and localized upland/wadi refuges.
- **Maritime variation:** Azmar, where windward/leeward exposure, island elevation, and currents control rainfall and freshwater.
- **Do not finalize** wind direction, currents, snowline, or biome borders until the latitude and seasonal model is chosen.

## 6. Physical consistency checklist

Before treating any draft map as coherent, verify:

1. Coastlines form plausible connected landmasses and navigable channels.
2. The central sea remains between the intended coasts; the Strait of Passage remains a real passage unless the concept is explicitly revised.
3. Every river starts on the correct side of a watershed and flows downhill.
4. Rivers do not cross high ridges without an explicitly designed gorge or geological explanation.
5. The Ilan River's estuary is on Ilmar's east-facing coast, while western rivers drain the opposite side of the divide.
6. The Orath inland basin has no outlet in the current closed-basin draft; revisit this if contours show a lower saddle.
7. Lakes have plausible catchments and an outlet or a stated closed-basin water balance.
8. Tharima's seasonal wadis do not accidentally become a permanent continent-wide river.
9. Nirvan's lakes, snowmelt, and seasonal routes agree with its relief and future climate model.
10. Rain shadows and biomes transition plausibly; no dense wet forest appears in a dry region without a local moisture source.
11. Island freshwater availability is recorded before any settlement is proposed.
12. Do not place cities, borders, or historical routes at this stage.

## 7. Unresolved decisions

- Exact outline and scale of every continent.
- Precise bay, cape, island, and strait geometry.
- Latitude grid, axial tilt, seasonal circulation, prevailing winds, and currents.
- Relative elevations and contour lines.
- Whether the Orath inland basin remains closed after contouring.
- Exact river courses, lake extents, and water balances.
- Biome boundaries and local freshwater reliability.

## 9. First coastline silhouette — topology test only

This is a **cell-level silhouette**, not a drawn coastline. Each letter marks the dominant content of a cell; actual coasts curve through cells and continents span multiple cells. The purpose is to test adjacency and broad arrangement before drawing detailed bays.

### What this silhouette tests

- Nirvan occupies the northern band; its southern margin approaches the Sea of Stillness.
- Ilmar occupies the west and opens onto both western outer waters and the central sea.
- Orath occupies the east; its western coast faces the central sea, while its eastern coast faces outer waters.
- Tharima occupies the south and faces the central sea to the north and outer waters farther south.
- Nearer Azmar groups sit within the central maritime region; remote groups remain scattered farther south/east.
- The Strait of Passage is a **candidate corridor** near Orath's southeastern reach and Tharima's northeastern side. The schematic does not yet prove its exact width or whether every neighbouring channel connects as intended.

### Problems intentionally left visible

1. The grid is too coarse to encode peninsulas, bays, islands, or the exact Sea of Stillness connection.
2. The exact meeting point of Nirvan, the northern waters, and Orath's northwest remains open.
3. The proposed Strait of Passage must be redrawn at finer resolution so that land does not accidentally seal it.
4. The southwestern Ilmar cape and the separate western/central coast shapes need more than one cell to represent faithfully.
5. This silhouette does not specify mountains, rivers, borders, settlements, or history.
