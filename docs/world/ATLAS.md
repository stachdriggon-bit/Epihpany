# EPIPHANY — Atlas and Geographic Register

**Version:** 0.3  
**Status:** Provisional geographic master plan; relative relief layout defined, not yet a scaled final map.

## 1. Geographic design rules

- Rivers follow downhill drainage and do not cross high ridgelines without a clearly established explanation.
- Large rivers require plausible sources and drainage areas.
- Inland closed basins may end in lakes, wetlands, salt flats, or dry lake beds rather than reaching the sea.
- Mountain rain shadows and elevation affect climate, vegetation, water, and routes.
- Deserts are not culturally or ecologically uniform; forests do not imply one uniform culture.
- Natural routes influence history but do not determine it completely.
- Distances, scale, exact coastlines, and precise river courses must not be inferred from concept art.

## 2. Major land regions

All names and arrangements below are **provisional working proposals**.

### Ilmar (إيلمار) — west
Temperate plains, river systems, forests, estuaries, and coastal ports. Internal regions may differ in rainfall and settlement patterns.

- **I-01 — Ilan Basin / حوض إيلان:** river plains and potential old agricultural settlements.
- **I-02 — Central Forests / الغابات الوسطى:** forest regions with varied local communities.
- **I-03 — Open Plains / السهول المفتوحة:** broad grassland and agricultural possibilities.
- **I-04 — Western Coast / الساحل الغربي:** estuaries, ports, and maritime connections.
- **M-05 — Western Ilmar Uplands / مرتفعات إيلمار الغربية:** lower and discontinuous uplands, possible river sources.

### Orath (أوراث) — east
Mountain chains, highlands, plateaus, inland basins, passes, and scattered mineral resources.

- **O-01 — Great Highlands / المرتفعات الكبرى:** main upland and mountain zone.
- **O-02 — Inland Basins / الأحواض الداخلية:** drainage areas whose outlet status remains to be decided.
- **O-03 — Passes / الممرات:** potential overland crossings.
- **O-04 — Mineral Zones / مناطق المعادن:** scattered resource areas; deposits and their historical role are not yet specified.
- **M-01 — Eastern Spine / العمود الشرقي:** proposed long mountain belt, with branches and traversable passes.
- **M-02 — Orath Inland Basin Rim / حواف حوض أوراث:** uplands surrounding a possible closed or partly closed basin.

### Sahir (ساهِر) — south
Arid and semi-arid areas, steppes, seasonal wadis, and water basins.

- **S-01 — Arid Belt / النطاق الجاف**
- **S-02 — Water Basins / الأحواض المائية:** not assumed to be identical or mutually connected.
- **S-03 — Seasonal Wadis / الأودية الموسمية**
- **S-04 — Transitional Steppes / السهوب الانتقالية**
- **M-04 — Basin Rims / حواف الأحواض:** discontinuous uplands guiding episodic runoff.

### Nirvan (نيرفان) — north
Cold forests, highlands, lakes, and irregular coasts. Not all of the north is assumed to be permanently ice-covered.

- **N-01 — Northern Forests / الغابات الشمالية**
- **N-02 — Cold Highlands / المرتفعات الباردة**
- **N-03 — Coasts and Fjords / السواحل والخلجان المتعرجة**
- **N-04 — Transitional Belt / النطاق الانتقالي**
- **M-03 — Nirvan Highlands / مرتفعات نيرفان:** proposed uplands connected to lakes, forests, and rivers.

### Azmar (أزمار) — archipelagos
Island groups, natural harbors, straits, and maritime passages. Azmar is not assumed to be one contiguous landmass or one unified culture.

- **A-01 — Inner Passages / الممرات الداخلية**
- **A-02 — Natural-Harbor Islands / جزر المرافئ الطبيعية**
- **A-03 — Remote Islands / الجزر البعيدة**

## 3. Relative layout proposal

Working arrangement only:
- Nirvan lies to the north.
- Ilmar lies to the west.
- Orath lies to the east.
- Sahir lies to the south.
- Azmar consists of archipelagos distributed around central maritime passages, with some groups farther away.
- A named “Distant Ocean” is a working label for waters beyond part of the known geographic area. The name does not establish the ocean's edge or the world's extent.

The world may contain additional land beyond these five principal regions.

## 4. Seas and passages

Provisional names:
- **S-SEA-01 — Sea of Dawn / بحر الفجر:** proposed principal maritime area near Ilmar and some Azmar routes.
- **S-SEA-02 — Sea of Stillness / بحر السكون:** proposed northern or northeastern sea. Its name does not mean it is always calm.
- **S-STR-01 — Strait of Passage / مضيق العبور:** proposed strategic narrow passage; exact location and alternative routes remain open.
- **S-OCEAN-01 — Distant Ocean / المحيط البعيد:** working name for outer waters not yet comprehensively charted in the known records.

## 5. Water systems

- **R-001 — Ilan River / نهر إيلان:** proposed to rise in western inland uplands, cross the Ilan Basin, and reach a coastal estuary. Exact source, tributaries, course, and mouth remain provisional.
- **R-002 — Orath River Network / شبكة أنهار أوراث:** several rivers descend from highlands; some may reach coasts, while others drain into inland basins.
- **R-003 — Sahir Wadi Network / أودية ساهِر:** seasonal channels fed by irregular rainfall and upland runoff.
- **R-004 — Nirvan Rivers and Lakes / أنهار وبحيرات نيرفان:** water systems influenced by northern uplands, local precipitation, and cold-season conditions.

Do not assign exact lengths or precise tributary counts until map scale and topography are established.

## 6. Climate framework

- Temperate and relatively humid areas in parts of Ilmar and some coasts.
- Mountain climates and rain-shadow effects in Orath and other uplands.
- Arid and semi-arid zones in Sahir and some inland basins.
- Cold forests, cold uplands, and seasonally constrained routes in Nirvan.
- Varied maritime climates across Azmar, depending on latitude, currents, and elevation.
- Transition belts between biomes where geography supports them.

Global wind directions, currents, exact temperature bands, and precise biome borders remain open.

## 7. Candidate settlement sites — not finalized cities

- **SET-01 — Ilan Confluence:** possible river confluence or crossing in Ilmar.
- **SET-02 — Pass City:** possible settlement controlling a usable Orath pass.
- **SET-03 — Basin Settlement:** possible settlement near a reliable water source in Sahir.
- **SET-04 — Nirvan Harbor:** possible protected northern harbor.
- **SET-05 — Azmar Port:** possible port at a junction of maritime passages.

These are site concepts, not canonized city names or established historical events.

## 8. Route register

- **P-01 — Western Passage:** possible link between Ilmar's coast and nearby islands.
- **P-02 — Strait Route:** maritime route through the proposed Strait of Passage.
- **P-03 — Orath Passes:** land routes between inland basins and coasts or neighboring regions.
- **P-04 — Sahir Basin Routes:** routes linking water sources, settlements, and trade.
- **P-05 — Northern Routes:** routes whose usability may vary seasonally.
- **P-06 — Outer Azmar Routes:** routes linking remote island groups to regional navigation.

These are route categories, not yet surveyed roads or guaranteed historical trade routes.

## 9. Coastline topology — working proposal v0.2

This section establishes a coherent relative arrangement of coasts and island groups so later relief, drainage, and climate layers have a stable base. It is a design proposal, not a claim that exact distances or coordinates have been measured. Do not let concept art silently override it.

### 9.1 Central sea and outer waters

- **Central Sea / بحر الفجر (S-SEA-01):** a broad, irregular sea between eastern Ilmar, western Orath, northern Sahir, and the nearer Azmar island groups. It has several bays and island passages rather than one perfectly open circular basin.
- **Sea of Stillness / بحر السكون (S-SEA-02):** lies north of the central sea, between southern Nirvan and northern/northwestern Orath. A set of channels and island gaps connects it to the central sea; the name is cultural, not a promise of calm weather.
- **Western Outer Ocean / المحيط الغربي البعيد:** open water off Ilmar's western coast. This is a working regional label, not a claim about the world's edge.
- **Southern Outer Ocean / المحيط الجنوبي البعيد:** open water beyond the southern and southwestern Sahir coast, with routes that can reach outer Azmar groups.
- **Strait of Passage / مضيق العبور (S-STR-01):** provisionally placed on the southeastern side of the central sea, between the southern reach of Orath and the northeastern coast of Sahir, opening toward the eastern/southeastern outer waters. Its navigability and strategic importance depend on final widths, currents, and alternate routes.

### 9.2 Ilmar coast — west

- **West-facing coast:** predominantly open and exposed, with alternating rocky headlands and stretches of lower coast. Do not make the entire coast one uninterrupted cliff or one uninterrupted beach.
- **Northwest reach:** more indented coast with sheltered coves and a few offshore islets; suitable for small harbors but not automatically for a major capital.
- **Western-central reach:** a broader coastal plain interrupted by low headlands. At least one river mouth may form an estuary here, provided its drainage basin is confirmed later.
- **Southwestern reach:** a prominent peninsula or hooked cape is proposed, with smaller islands offshore. It creates a natural change in sailing routes without closing off the western ocean.
- **East-facing coast:** opens onto the central sea. Include one broad bay and several smaller inlets; keep room for river mouths from the interior, but do not place the Ilan estuary until the river's final course is fixed.
- **Open decision:** exact bay count, names, peninsula dimensions, and location of the Ilan mouth.

### 9.3 Nirvan coast — north

- **Northern edge:** deeply irregular in selected sectors, with fjord-like inlets where highlands approach the sea; other stretches remain lower and more continuous.
- **Southern coast:** faces the Sea of Stillness and includes several protected bays and island-studded channels.
- **Western shoulder:** a transition coast connecting cold northern waters to the open waters northwest of Ilmar. Avoid assuming that all routes remain ice-free year-round.
- **Eastern shoulder:** more exposed, with fewer sheltered anchorages where the uplands approach the coast.
- **Coastal islands:** a broken fringe of nearshore islands is proposed, distinct from the main Azmar archipelagos.
- **Open decision:** exact fjord locations, seasonal ice extent, and whether any major island is connected to the mainland by a shallow shelf.

### 9.4 Orath coast — east

- **Western coast:** faces the central sea and is interrupted by foothills, river mouths, and a few deep bays. Mountain ridges should approach the coast only in selected sections, leaving lowland corridors elsewhere.
- **Northwestern coast:** forms part of the boundary with the Sea of Stillness, with narrow channels and exposed capes.
- **Eastern coast:** faces the outer ocean and is comparatively exposed. It alternates between steep headlands and broader coastal pockets rather than mirroring the western coast.
- **Southeastern reach:** narrows toward the proposed Strait of Passage; the final land shape must preserve a navigable channel unless a later decision deliberately changes the strait concept.
- **Open decision:** exact number and placement of coastal river outlets, and whether the east coast has a large gulf.

### 9.5 Sahir coast — south

- **Northern coast:** faces the central sea. It includes several shallow gulfs and stretches of low coast where seasonal rivers may reach the sea, but not every wadi should become a permanent river mouth.
- **Northeastern reach:** forms the southern side of the proposed Strait of Passage.
- **Southern coast:** faces the southern outer ocean, with long arid stretches broken by occasional natural harbors and coastal highlands.
- **Southwestern reach:** a low coastal shelf with offshore islands is proposed; reefs or shoals may influence navigation only after currents and sea depth are designed.
- **Open decision:** whether one major southern gulf exists and which seasonal waterways reach the sea.

### 9.6 Azmar archipelagos

For map consistency, divide Azmar into three spatial groups rather than treating it as one continuous island belt:

- **A-01 — Inner Passages:** the nearer islands in the central sea, arranged in broken chains that create several routes and several bottlenecks. No single island should block all movement between the surrounding continents.
- **A-02 — Harbor Islands:** a middle group near the eastern and southeastern central-sea approaches, with naturally sheltered coves on some islands and exposed coasts on others.
- **A-03 — Remote Islands:** smaller, more scattered groups farther south and southeast in the outer waters. They need not all be inhabited or mapped with equal certainty.

The archipelagos should not form a perfect arc or evenly spaced stepping stones. Leave open-water gaps and alternate routes so maritime history can vary by period and technology.

### 9.7 Coastline consistency rules

1. Bays must have a plausible opening to a sea; a narrow opening can create a strait or lagoon but must be intentional.
2. River mouths must align with drainage basins established on the relief map.
3. Estuaries and deltas require appropriate sediment, tidal, and river conditions; they are not interchangeable decorative symbols.
4. Natural harbors should be sheltered by headlands, islands, or bay geometry, while exposed ports need another reason to thrive.
5. Fjords require steep coastal relief and glacially shaped valleys in the setting's physical history; use them selectively.
6. Island chains may influence navigation but must leave plausible channels, distances, and alternate routes.
7. Coastline detail should vary by region; avoid making every shore equally jagged.
8. Do not finalize political borders or major cities from coastline alone.

## 10. Relief framework — working proposal v0.3

This is the next layer above the coastline topology. It fixes the relative placement and orientation of major relief features so river drainage can be designed coherently. Heights and distances remain relative rather than numeric.

### 10.1 Ilmar — western continent

- **M-05 Western Ilmar Uplands:** a broken upland belt in the western-to-southwestern interior, not a continuous wall along the coast. It rises inland of parts of the west coast and gradually lowers toward the central plains.
- **I-01 Ilan Basin:** a broad, gently sloping lowland in the central-to-eastern part of Ilmar, open toward the east-facing coast of the central sea. Reserve a plausible route for the Ilan River from uplands through this basin to an estuary on the central-sea coast; exact course and mouth remain undecided.
- **I-02 Central Forests:** rolling uplands and low hills between the western uplands and the main river lowlands; not a solid forest wall.
- **I-03 Open Plains:** broad low-relief country east and southeast of the central forests, broken by tributary valleys and isolated hills.
- **I-04 Western Coast:** coastal plains are wider in the west-central reach and narrower where headlands approach the sea.
- **Drainage rule:** rivers rising on the western uplands can flow west to the outer ocean or east toward the Ilan Basin, depending on which side of the local watershed they begin. The watershed must be drawn before assigning tributaries.

### 10.2 Orath — eastern continent

- **M-01 Eastern Spine:** the principal mountain belt trends from the north-northeast toward the south-central interior, with a curved southern branch trending toward the southeastern coast. It is not a perfectly straight north-south wall.
- **O-01 Great Highlands:** a broad elevated region around and west of the spine, containing secondary ridges and valleys.
- **M-02 Basin Rim:** lower mountain arcs and plateaus partially enclose an inland basin west of the spine. The basin is provisionally closed or weakly connected; its outlet status must be decided from a contour-level sketch, not assumed.
- **O-03 Passes:** reserve at least three distinct types of crossing: a northern high pass, a central lower pass connecting inhabited basins, and a southern pass leading toward the central-sea coast. These are candidate corridors, not named roads.
- **O-04 Mineral Zones:** reserve several separated upland pockets rather than one continuous mineral belt. Exact deposits remain open.
- **Drainage rule:** western slopes may feed rivers toward the central sea; eastern slopes may feed rivers toward the outer ocean. Streams inside the inland basin drain to its lowest point unless a lower natural outlet is explicitly designed.

### 10.3 Sahir — southern continent

- **M-04 Basin Rims:** discontinuous uplands in the north-central interior and along selected basin edges. They guide runoff without forming an unbroken ring around the continent.
- **S-01 Arid Belt:** broad dry interior zones, with local variation near coasts, highlands, and seasonal watercourses.
- **S-02 Water Basins:** separate low basins with different water balances; do not connect them by permanent rivers unless the elevation profile supports it.
- **S-03 Seasonal Wadis:** main channels descend from basin rims toward low basins or the central/southern coasts. Some terminate inland; only selected channels reach the sea.
- **S-04 Transitional Steppes:** reserve a band between the wetter northern margins and the drier interior, with width varying by relief and rainfall.
- **Drainage rule:** seasonal floods may spread across low plains, but the main channel still needs a plausible downhill route. Do not use a wadi as a permanent river without a reliable source.

### 10.4 Nirvan — northern continent

- **M-03 Nirvan Highlands:** a broken mountain and highland arc across the northwestern and northern interior, with branches approaching the coast in selected sectors.
- **N-02 Cold Highlands:** the highest and coldest zones occur around the main uplands, not uniformly across the whole continent.
- **N-01 Northern Forests:** occupy lower and middle elevations where temperature and moisture allow; forest cover changes with latitude, altitude, and exposure.
- **N-03 Coasts and Fjords:** fjord-like inlets are concentrated where steep glacial valleys meet the sea. Other coast sectors remain low or moderately sloped.
- **N-04 Transitional Belt:** a southern belt of lower terrain provides routes toward the Sea of Stillness and neighboring regions.
- **Drainage rule:** most major rivers descend from the highlands toward the northern, western, or southern coasts or into local lakes. Each lake needs an inlet/outlet or an explicitly closed-basin status.

### 10.5 Azmar — archipelagos

- **A-01 Inner Passages:** the islands occupy broken chains in the central sea, with several navigable gaps. Some islands may be volcanic or steep, but that is not assumed for all.
- **A-02 Harbor Islands:** a scattered group nearer the southeastern approaches to the central sea; sheltered harbors depend on island shape and prevailing seas.
- **A-03 Remote Islands:** more dispersed islands south and southeast of the main maritime region, with variable rainfall and freshwater availability.
- Island relief is local: some islands are mountainous, others low and coral-like, and others old eroded land. Do not assign a single geology or ecosystem to all of Azmar.

### 10.6 Watershed and contour rules

1. Draw ridgelines and basin rims first; place headwaters on the correct side of each divide.
2. Rivers descend toward the nearest lower outlet unless a closed basin or unusual geological explanation is deliberately established.
3. A river may cross a mountain chain only through a plausible gorge, antecedent course, or other explicitly designed geological feature; default designs should route around high ridges.
4. A lake must have a plausible catchment and either an outlet, evaporation-dominated closed basin, or other recorded water balance.
5. Mountain rain shadows should influence downstream dryness but must not create instant biome boundaries.
6. Coastal plains widen where relief retreats inland and narrow where highlands approach the coast.
7. Passes should align with lower saddles between ridges; they cannot be placed arbitrarily through the highest peaks.
8. Keep the relief map internally consistent before adding borders, cities, or historical routes.

## 11. Open geographic decisions

1. Draw a consistent outline with a provisional scale and orientation grid.
2. Refine exact coastlines, bay geometry, and island positions.
3. Assign relative heights and contour lines to the relief framework.
4. Decide whether the Orath inland basin has a natural outlet.
5. Trace each major river and lake from watershed to outlet.
6. Fix the exact width and navigation constraints of straits and channels.
7. Determine winds, currents, rainfall, and detailed biome borders.
8. Place candidate settlements and routes only after the physical layers are checked.
9. Add political borders and historical routes in later map layers.

## 12. Map layers to create

World overview; physical/topographic map; rivers and drainage basins; climate and biomes; political borders by period; historical routes and migrations; trade and maritime routes; phenomena and evidence; regional/city maps; story and player-discovery maps.

Illustrative images are mood references only and must never override this register.
