# EPIPHANY — Atlas and Geographic Register

**Version:** 0.3  
**Status:** Integrated working geographic foundation; relative layout and settlement-siting constraints are established, but this is not yet a scaled final map.

## 1. Geographic design rules

- Rivers follow downhill drainage and do not cross high ridgelines without a clearly established explanation.
- Large rivers require plausible sources and drainage areas.
- Inland closed basins may end in lakes, wetlands, salt flats, or dry lake beds rather than reaching the sea.
- Mountain rain shadows and elevation affect climate, vegetation, water, and routes.
- Deserts are not culturally or ecologically uniform; forests do not imply one uniform culture.
- Natural routes influence history but do not determine it completely.
- Distances, scale, exact coastlines, and precise river courses must not be inferred from concept art.

## 2. Geographic classification: four named continents and Azmar archipelagos

**Current working inventory:** four named continents — Ilmar, Orath, Tharima, and Nirvan — plus the Azmar archipelagos. Azmar is a geographic island-region, not currently counted as a continent. This inventory describes the five principal regions mapped so far; it does **not** establish that the entire world contains exactly four continents. Additional continents or major landmasses beyond the current known map remain an open possibility.

All names and arrangements below are **provisional working proposals**. The classification above is the current reference for organizing this draft, not a claim that the world's full extent has been mapped.

### Ilmar (إيلمار) — west
Temperate plains, river systems, forests, estuaries, and coastal ports. Internal regions may differ in rainfall and settlement patterns.

- **I-01 — Ilan Basin / حوض إيلان:** river plains and potential old agricultural settlements.
- **I-02 — Central Forests / الغابات الوسطى:** forest regions with varied local communities.
- **I-03 — Open Plains / السهول المفتوحة:** broad grassland and agricultural possibilities.
- **I-04 — Western Coast / الساحل الغربي:** estuaries, ports, and maritime connections.
- **M-05 — Western Ilmar Uplands / مرتفعات إيلمار الغربية:** lower and discontinuous uplands, possible river sources.

### Orath (أوراث) — east
Mountain chains, highlands, plateaus, inland basins, passes, and scattered mineral resources.

- **O-01 — Great Highlands / المرتفعات الكبرى:** main upland and mountain zone.
- **O-02 — Inland Basins / الأحواض الداخلية:** drainage areas whose outlet status remains to be decided.
- **O-03 — Passes / الممرات:** potential overland crossings.
- **O-04 — Mineral Zones / مناطق المعادن:** scattered resource areas; deposits and their historical role are not yet specified.
- **M-01 — Eastern Spine / العمود الشرقي:** proposed long mountain belt, with branches and traversable passes.
- **M-02 — Orath Inland Basin Rim / حواف حوض أوراث:** uplands surrounding a possible closed or partly closed basin.

### Tharima (ثاريما) — south
Arid and semi-arid areas, steppes, seasonal wadis, and water basins.

- **S-01 — Arid Belt / النطاق الجاف**
- **S-02 — Water Basins / الأحواض المائية:** not assumed to be identical or mutually connected.
- **S-03 — Seasonal Wadis / الأودية الموسمية**
- **S-04 — Transitional Steppes / السهوب الانتقالية**
- **M-04 — Basin Rims / حواف الأحواض:** discontinuous uplands guiding episodic runoff.

### Nirvan (نيرفان) — north
Cold forests, highlands, lakes, and irregular coasts. Not all of the north is assumed to be permanently ice-covered.

- **N-01 — Northern Forests / الغابات الشمالية**
- **N-02 — Cold Highlands / المرتفعات الباردة**
- **N-03 — Coasts and Fjords / السواحل والخلجان المتعرجة**
- **N-04 — Transitional Belt / النطاق الانتقالي**
- **M-03 — Nirvan Highlands / مرتفعات نيرفان:** proposed uplands connected to lakes, forests, and rivers.

### Azmar (أزمار) — archipelagos
Island groups, natural harbors, straits, and maritime passages. Azmar is not assumed to be one contiguous landmass or one unified culture.

- **A-01 — Inner Passages / الممرات الداخلية**
- **A-02 — Natural-Harbor Islands / جزر المرافئ الطبيعية**
- **A-03 — Remote Islands / الجزر البعيدة**

## 3. Relative layout proposal

Working arrangement only:
- Nirvan lies to the north.
- Ilmar lies to the west.
- Orath lies to the east.
- Tharima lies to the south.
- Azmar consists of archipelagos distributed around central maritime passages, with some groups farther away.
- A named “Distant Ocean” is a working label for waters beyond part of the known geographic area. The name does not establish the ocean's edge or the world's extent.

The world may contain additional continents, major landmasses, or island groups beyond these five principal regions. The total number of continents in the entire world remains open.

## 4. Seas and passages

Provisional names:
- **S-SEA-01 — Sea of Dawn / بحر الفجر:** proposed principal maritime area near Ilmar and some Azmar routes.
- **S-SEA-02 — Sea of Stillness / بحر السكون:** proposed northern or northeastern sea. Its name does not mean it is always calm.
- **S-STR-01 — Strait of Passage / مضيق العبور:** proposed strategic narrow passage; exact location and alternative routes remain open.
- **S-OCEAN-01 — Distant Ocean / المحيط البعيد:** working name for outer waters not yet comprehensively charted in the known records.

## 5. Water systems

- **R-001 — Ilan River / نهر إيلان:** proposed to rise in western inland uplands, cross the Ilan Basin, and reach a coastal estuary. Exact source, tributaries, course, and mouth remain provisional.
- **R-002 — Orath River Network / شبكة أنهار أوراث:** several rivers descend from highlands; some may reach coasts, while others drain into inland basins.
- **R-003 — Tharima Wadi Network / أودية ثاريما:** seasonal channels fed by irregular rainfall and upland runoff.
- **R-004 — Nirvan Rivers and Lakes / أنهار وبحيرات نيرفان:** water systems influenced by northern uplands, local precipitation, and cold-season conditions.

Do not assign exact lengths or precise tributary counts until map scale and topography are established.

## 6. Climate framework

- Temperate and relatively humid areas in parts of Ilmar and some coasts.
- Mountain climates and rain-shadow effects in Orath and other uplands.
- Arid and semi-arid zones in Tharima and some inland basins.
- Cold forests, cold uplands, and seasonally constrained routes in Nirvan.
- Varied maritime climates across Azmar, depending on latitude, currents, and elevation.
- Transition belts between biomes where geography supports them.

Global wind directions, currents, exact temperature bands, and precise biome borders remain open.

## 9. Coastline topology — working proposal v0.2

This section establishes a coherent relative arrangement of coasts and island groups so later relief, drainage, and climate layers have a stable base. It is a design proposal, not a claim that exact distances or coordinates have been measured. Do not let concept art silently override it.

### 9.1 Central sea and outer waters

- **Central Sea / بحر الفجر (S-SEA-01):** a broad, irregular sea between eastern Ilmar, western Orath, northern Tharima, and the nearer Azmar island groups. It has several bays and island passages rather than one perfectly open circular basin.
- **Sea of Stillness / بحر السكون (S-SEA-02):** lies north of the central sea, between southern Nirvan and northern/northwestern Orath. A set of channels and island gaps connects it to the central sea; the name is cultural, not a promise of calm weather.
- **Western Outer Ocean / المحيط الغربي البعيد:** open water off Ilmar's western coast. This is a working regional label, not a claim about the world's edge.
- **Southern Outer Ocean / المحيط الجنوبي البعيد:** open water beyond the southern and southwestern Tharima coast, with routes that can reach outer Azmar groups.
- **Strait of Passage / مضيق العبور (S-STR-01):** provisionally placed on the southeastern side of the central sea, between the southern reach of Orath and the northeastern coast of Tharima, opening toward the eastern/southeastern outer waters. Its navigability and strategic importance depend on final widths, currents, and alternate routes.

### 9.2 Ilmar coast — west

- **West-facing coast:** predominantly open and exposed, with alternating rocky headlands and stretches of lower coast. Do not make the entire coast one uninterrupted cliff or one uninterrupted beach.
- **Northwest reach:** more indented coast with sheltered coves and a few offshore islets; suitable for small harbors but not automatically for a major capital.
- **Western-central reach:** a broader coastal plain interrupted by low headlands. At least one river mouth may form an estuary here, provided its drainage basin is confirmed later.
- **Southwestern reach:** a prominent peninsula or hooked cape is proposed, with smaller islands offshore. It creates a natural change in sailing routes without closing off the western ocean.
- **East-facing coast:** opens onto the central sea. Include one broad bay and several smaller inlets; keep room for river mouths from the interior, but do not place the Ilan estuary until the river's final course is fixed.
- **Open decision:** exact bay count, names, peninsula dimensions, and location of the Ilan mouth.

### 9.3 Nirvan coast — north

- **Northern edge:** deeply irregular in selected sectors, with fjord-like inlets where highlands approach the sea; other stretches remain lower and more continuous.
- **Southern coast:** faces the Sea of Stillness and includes several protected bays and island-studded channels.
- **Western shoulder:** a transition coast connecting cold northern waters to the open waters northwest of Ilmar. Avoid assuming that all routes remain ice-free year-round.
- **Eastern shoulder:** more exposed, with fewer sheltered anchorages where the uplands approach the coast.
- **Coastal islands:** a broken fringe of nearshore islands is proposed, distinct from the main Azmar archipelagos.
- **Open decision:** exact fjord locations, seasonal ice extent, and whether any major island is connected to the mainland by a shallow shelf.

### 9.4 Orath coast — east

- **Western coast:** faces the central sea and is interrupted by foothills, river mouths, and a few deep bays. Mountain ridges should approach the coast only in selected sections, leaving lowland corridors elsewhere.
- **Northwestern coast:** forms part of the boundary with the Sea of Stillness, with narrow channels and exposed capes.
- **Eastern coast:** faces the outer ocean and is comparatively exposed. It alternates between steep headlands and broader coastal pockets rather than mirroring the western coast.
- **Southeastern reach:** narrows toward the proposed Strait of Passage; the final land shape must preserve a navigable channel unless a later decision deliberately changes the strait concept.
- **Open decision:** exact number and placement of coastal river outlets, and whether the east coast has a large gulf.

### 9.5 Tharima coast — south

- **Northern coast:** faces the central sea. It includes several shallow gulfs and stretches of low coast where seasonal rivers may reach the sea, but not every wadi should become a permanent river mouth.
- **Northeastern reach:** forms the southern side of the proposed Strait of Passage.
- **Southern coast:** faces the southern outer ocean, with long arid stretches broken by occasional natural harbors and coastal highlands.
- **Southwestern reach:** a low coastal shelf with offshore islands is proposed; reefs or shoals may influence navigation only after currents and sea depth are designed.
- **Open decision:** whether one major southern gulf exists and which seasonal waterways reach the sea.

### 9.6 Azmar archipelagos

For map consistency, divide Azmar into three spatial groups rather than treating it as one continuous island belt:

- **A-01 — Inner Passages:** the nearer islands in the central sea, arranged in broken chains that create several routes and several bottlenecks. No single island should block all movement between the surrounding continents.
- **A-02 — Harbor Islands:** a middle group near the eastern and southeastern central-sea approaches, with naturally sheltered coves on some islands and exposed coasts on others.
- **A-03 — Remote Islands:** smaller, more scattered groups farther south and southeast in the outer waters. They need not all be inhabited or mapped with equal certainty.

The archipelagos should not form a perfect arc or evenly spaced stepping stones. Leave open-water gaps and alternate routes so maritime history can vary by period and technology.

### 9.7 Coastline consistency rules

1. Bays must have a plausible opening to a sea; a narrow opening can create a strait or lagoon but must be intentional.
2. River mouths must align with drainage basins established on the relief map.
3. Estuaries and deltas require appropriate sediment, tidal, and river conditions; they are not interchangeable decorative symbols.
4. Natural harbors should be sheltered by headlands, islands, or bay geometry, while exposed ports need another reason to thrive.
5. Fjords require steep coastal relief and glacially shaped valleys in the setting's physical history; use them selectively.
6. Island chains may influence navigation but must leave plausible channels, distances, and alternate routes.
7. Coastline detail should vary by region; avoid making every shore equally jagged.
8. Do not finalize political borders or major cities from coastline alone.

## 10. Relief framework — working proposal v0.3

This is the next layer above the coastline topology. It fixes the relative placement and orientation of major relief features so river drainage can be designed coherently. Heights and distances remain relative rather than numeric.

### 10.1 Ilmar — western continent

- **M-05 Western Ilmar Uplands:** a broken upland belt in the western-to-southwestern interior, not a continuous wall along the coast. It rises inland of parts of the west coast and gradually lowers toward the central plains.
- **I-01 Ilan Basin:** a broad, gently sloping lowland in the central-to-eastern part of Ilmar, open toward the east-facing coast of the central sea. Reserve a plausible route for the Ilan River from uplands through this basin to an estuary on the central-sea coast; exact course and mouth remain undecided.
- **I-02 Central Forests:** rolling uplands and low hills between the western uplands and the main river lowlands; not a solid forest wall.
- **I-03 Open Plains:** broad low-relief country east and southeast of the central forests, broken by tributary valleys and isolated hills.
- **I-04 Western Coast:** coastal plains are wider in the west-central reach and narrower where headlands approach the sea.
- **Drainage rule:** rivers rising on the western uplands can flow west to the outer ocean or east toward the Ilan Basin, depending on which side of the local watershed they begin. The watershed must be drawn before assigning tributaries.

### 10.2 Orath — eastern continent

- **M-01 Eastern Spine:** the principal mountain belt trends from the north-northeast toward the south-central interior, with a curved southern branch trending toward the southeastern coast. It is not a perfectly straight north-south wall.
- **O-01 Great Highlands:** a broad elevated region around and west of the spine, containing secondary ridges and valleys.
- **M-02 Basin Rim:** lower mountain arcs and plateaus partially enclose an inland basin west of the spine. The basin is provisionally closed or weakly connected; its outlet status must be decided from a contour-level sketch, not assumed.
- **O-03 Passes:** reserve at least three distinct types of crossing: a northern high pass, a central lower pass connecting inhabited basins, and a southern pass leading toward the central-sea coast. These are candidate corridors, not named roads.
- **O-04 Mineral Zones:** reserve several separated upland pockets rather than one continuous mineral belt. Exact deposits remain open.
- **Drainage rule:** western slopes may feed rivers toward the central sea; eastern slopes may feed rivers toward the outer ocean. Streams inside the inland basin drain to its lowest point unless a lower natural outlet is explicitly designed.

### 10.3 Tharima — southern continent

- **M-04 Basin Rims:** discontinuous uplands in the north-central interior and along selected basin edges. They guide runoff without forming an unbroken ring around the continent.
- **S-01 Arid Belt:** broad dry interior zones, with local variation near coasts, highlands, and seasonal watercourses.
- **S-02 Water Basins:** separate low basins with different water balances; do not connect them by permanent rivers unless the elevation profile supports it.
- **S-03 Seasonal Wadis:** main channels descend from basin rims toward low basins or the central/southern coasts. Some terminate inland; only selected channels reach the sea.
- **S-04 Transitional Steppes:** reserve a band between the wetter northern margins and the drier interior, with width varying by relief and rainfall.
- **Drainage rule:** seasonal floods may spread across low plains, but the main channel still needs a plausible downhill route. Do not use a wadi as a permanent river without a reliable source.

### 10.4 Nirvan — northern continent

- **M-03 Nirvan Highlands:** a broken mountain and highland arc across the northwestern and northern interior, with branches approaching the coast in selected sectors.
- **N-02 Cold Highlands:** the highest and coldest zones occur around the main uplands, not uniformly across the whole continent.
- **N-01 Northern Forests:** occupy lower and middle elevations where temperature and moisture allow; forest cover changes with latitude, altitude, and exposure.
- **N-03 Coasts and Fjords:** fjord-like inlets are concentrated where steep glacial valleys meet the sea. Other coast sectors remain low or moderately sloped.
- **N-04 Transitional Belt:** a southern belt of lower terrain provides routes toward the Sea of Stillness and neighboring regions.
- **Drainage rule:** most major rivers descend from the highlands toward the northern, western, or southern coasts or into local lakes. Each lake needs an inlet/outlet or an explicitly closed-basin status.

### 10.5 Azmar — archipelagos

- **A-01 Inner Passages:** the islands occupy broken chains in the central sea, with several navigable gaps. Some islands may be volcanic or steep, but that is not assumed for all.
- **A-02 Harbor Islands:** a scattered group nearer the southeastern approaches to the central sea; sheltered harbors depend on island shape and prevailing seas.
- **A-03 Remote Islands:** more dispersed islands south and southeast of the main maritime region, with variable rainfall and freshwater availability.
- Island relief is local: some islands are mountainous, others low and coral-like, and others old eroded land. Do not assign a single geology or ecosystem to all of Azmar.

### 10.6 Watershed and contour rules

1. Draw ridgelines and basin rims first; place headwaters on the correct side of each divide.
2. Rivers descend toward the nearest lower outlet unless a closed basin or unusual geological explanation is deliberately established.
3. A river may cross a mountain chain only through a plausible gorge, antecedent course, or other explicitly designed geological feature; default designs should route around high ridges.
4. A lake must have a plausible catchment and either an outlet, evaporation-dominated closed basin, or other recorded water balance.
5. Mountain rain shadows should influence downstream dryness but must not create instant biome boundaries.
6. Coastal plains widen where relief retreats inland and narrow where highlands approach the coast.
7. Passes should align with lower saddles between ridges; they cannot be placed arbitrarily through the highest peaks.
8. Keep the relief map internally consistent before adding borders, cities, or historical routes.

## 11. Open geographic decisions

1. Draw a consistent outline with a provisional scale and orientation grid.
2. Refine exact coastlines, bay geometry, and island positions.
3. Assign relative heights and contour lines to the relief framework.
4. Decide whether the Orath inland basin has a natural outlet.
5. Trace each major river and lake from watershed to outlet.
6. Fix the exact width and navigation constraints of straits and channels.
7. Determine winds, currents, rainfall, and detailed biome borders.
8. Place candidate settlements and routes only after the physical layers are checked.
9. Add political borders and historical routes in later map layers.

## 13. Drainage network — working proposal v0.4

This section proposes river corridors and lake roles from the relief framework. It does not establish measured lengths, discharge, exact tributary counts, or final map coordinates. The route descriptions are topological: they define which side of a watershed the water occupies and where it ultimately drains.

### 13.1 Ilmar — the Ilan system and western rivers

- **R-001 — Ilan River / نهر إيلان:** headwaters begin on the eastern-facing slopes of the Western Ilmar Uplands. The main river descends into the Ilan Basin, gathers tributaries from the central rolling hills, then bends toward the east-facing coast and enters the Sea of Dawn through an estuary. Keep its lower course broad and gently sloping; the estuary is a proposed feature, not yet a fixed point on the coastline.
- **R-005 — Westward Rivers / أنهار الساحل الغربي:** several independent, shorter rivers descend from the ocean-facing side of the Western Ilmar Uplands to the Western Outer Ocean. Their catchments are separated from the Ilan system by the main watershed. Do not depict them as tributaries of Ilan.
- **R-006 — Forest Tributaries / روافد الغابات الوسطى:** smaller streams rise in the Central Forests and rolling hills and join Ilan or its basin tributaries. Some headwater streams may be seasonal in drier southern reaches; do not assume every forest stream is navigable.
- **R-007 — Plain Rivers / أنهار السهول المفتوحة:** low-gradient tributaries cross the Open Plains and join the lower Ilan system. Reserve room for floodplains, abandoned channels, and wetlands near the lower river, but only where the terrain and sediment supply support them.
- **Lake L-001 — Forest Lake / بحيرة الغابة:** a modest upland lake in a hollow among the Central Forests, fed by local streams and draining through a small outlet into a tributary of Ilan. This is a proposed lake, not a vast inland sea.
- **Lake L-002 — Lower Ilan Wetlands / مستنقعات إيلان السفلى:** a seasonal wetland/floodplain complex near the lower river rather than a separate deep lake. Its size varies with floods and seasons.

**Consistency check:** the Ilan source must sit on the basin-facing side of the upland divide; the estuary must lie on Ilmar's east-facing coast. If a later coastline sketch makes this route impossible, revise the route or divide explicitly rather than forcing a river through a ridge.

### 13.2 Orath — outward rivers and an inland closed basin

For the current draft, use a **provisional closed-basin model** for the inland basin west of the Eastern Spine. This gives Orath a distinct hydrological region and avoids inventing an outlet through the Basin Rim before the contour layout supports one. This is a design choice, not a claim that all inland basins in Orath are closed.

- **R-002A — Western Orath Rivers / أنهار أوراث الغربية:** rivers descend from the western slopes of the Great Highlands and lower passes toward the Sea of Dawn or Sea of Stillness. Separate catchments reach different coastal bays; do not combine every western river into one giant trunk.
- **R-002B — Eastern Orath Rivers / أنهار أوراث الشرقية:** rivers descend from the Eastern Spine's eastern slopes toward the exposed eastern coast. The steepest headwaters can have shorter, faster courses; longer rivers need broader valleys and tributaries. Their mouths do not connect to the western rivers.
- **R-008 — Basin Inflows / روافد الحوض الداخلي:** intermittent streams and at least one more reliable upland-fed river descend into the enclosed basin west of the spine. They terminate in a lowland lake/wetland system rather than crossing the Basin Rim.
- **Lake L-003 — Orath Inner Lake / بحيرة أوراث الداخلية:** a shallow, evaporation-sensitive lake in the lowest part of the closed basin. Its shoreline expands and retreats over long wet/dry cycles; in drier periods, exposed mudflats or salt flats may border it. The basin should not be a permanently huge freshwater lake unless later climate and inflow calculations justify that.
- **Pass drainage:** valleys through the northern, central, and southern candidate passes carry local streams toward whichever basin or coast is downhill. A pass is not automatically a river crossing between opposite sides of the main spine.

**Consistency check:** no stream may leave the closed basin in the draft map. If a contour sketch later reveals a lower saddle to the sea, the basin must be reclassified as open and its lake/outlet redesigned.

### 13.3 Tharima — episodic water, not a hidden continent-wide river

- **R-003A — Northern Wadis / أودية ثاريما الشمالية:** seasonal channels descend from the transitional steppe and northern basin rims into low interior basins. Their flow is brief and flood-prone; they do not form a permanent connected river network.
- **R-003B — Coastal Wadis / أودية الساحل:** selected wadis reach the northern coast and the Sea of Dawn after major rain events. Others lose flow to infiltration, evaporation, or inland depressions before reaching the sea.
- **R-003C — Southern Basin Channels / قنوات الأحواض الجنوبية:** short, separate channels drain local uplands into closed depressions. Keep these catchments independent unless the relative elevations explicitly connect them.
- **Lake L-004 — Rain Basin / بحيرة المطر:** a shallow, intermittent lake in one interior depression, appearing or expanding after exceptional seasonal rains and shrinking markedly in dry periods. Its presence is not a guarantee of permanent freshwater.
- **Waterhole series W-001 — Basin Springs / عيون الأحواض:** a small set of proposed springs at selected basin margins where groundwater can emerge. Their reliability is local and must be justified by aquifer recharge; do not scatter permanent springs uniformly across the arid belt.

**Consistency check:** draw the wadi's downhill path even if its visible channel disappears into sand or a depression. A dry channel may reach the coast, but it must not be treated as a perennial river without a reliable year-round source.

### 13.4 Nirvan — highland rivers and lake outlets

- **R-004A — Northern Rivers / أنهار نيرفان الشمالية:** streams rise in the highland arc and descend toward northern fjorded or open-coast sectors. Steep upper reaches feed broader lower valleys where relief permits.
- **R-004B — Western Rivers / أنهار نيرفان الغربية:** separate catchments descend from western slopes toward the northwest coast. They remain independent from rivers flowing south to the Sea of Stillness unless a low divide is deliberately drawn.
- **R-004C — Southern Rivers / أنهار نيرفان الجنوبية:** longer rivers descend through the lower southern belt toward protected bays on the Sea of Stillness. These valleys can become natural overland corridors, but their banks may be seasonally difficult in cold conditions.
- **Lake L-005 — Highland Lake / بحيرة المرتفعات:** a glacially shaped lake in a highland hollow, fed by snowmelt and local precipitation, with a defined outlet joining one northern or southern river. Do not leave it as an isolated blue symbol with no water balance.
- **Lake L-006 — Southern Lake Chain / سلسلة البحيرات الجنوبية:** a small chain of connected lakes in a lower valley, where each lake spills into the next and the final outlet joins a southern river. If later relief cannot support this sequence, keep only the best-supported lake and remove the rest.
- **Seasonal conditions:** cold-season freezing and snowmelt can alter flow timing, but do not assume every river is frozen all year or that all routes are impassable.

**Consistency check:** any fjord-side river must descend from a real inland catchment; not every fjord needs a major river, and not every river mouth must sit inside a fjord.

### 13.5 Azmar — small catchments and freshwater limits

- **A-01 Inner Passages:** larger inhabited islands may support short streams if their relief and rainfall justify them; low islands may instead rely on rainwater storage, springs, or imports.
- **A-02 Harbor Islands:** sheltered coves do not imply abundant freshwater. Keep freshwater sources separate from harbor suitability.
- **A-03 Remote Islands:** some islands have seasonal streams or small freshwater lenses; others are dry and sparsely inhabited. A permanent settlement on a dry island requires a recorded water solution.
- No single river system connects the archipelagos. Each island has its own catchment, and the sea separates drainage networks.

### 13.6 Network-wide rules and unresolved checks

1. Each main river has a source region, watershed side, destination, and plausible gradient before its tributaries are named.
2. Rivers flowing to opposite coasts remain separate unless an explicit low divide or unusual geological history is documented.
3. The Orath closed basin is provisional; its status depends on future contours and must be revisited before the map is considered final.
4. Lakes L-001 to L-006 are proposed geographic features, not all equally large or permanent. Their water balances must be revisited with climate.
5. Tharima's seasonal channels should vary in length and reliability; some end inland and some reach the sea.
6. Nirvan's snowmelt and lake outlets must match elevation and seasonal climate.
7. Small islands need not have permanent rivers. Freshwater availability is a constraint on settlement, not a decorative detail.
8. No precise lengths, volumes, navigability, or population capacity are assigned until a scale and climate model exist.

## 14. Climate and natural regions — working proposal v0.5

**Status:** coherent first-pass climate model; not a final meteorological simulation. The map scale, exact latitude, axial tilt, prevailing wind belts, ocean currents, and mountain heights remain unset. The model below establishes relative patterns and causal links without pretending that exact climate boundaries are known.

### 14.1 Climate-design assumptions

- Treat Nirvan as the generally coolest major region and Tharima as the generally driest interior region, but neither is uniform.
- Latitude is only one control. Elevation, exposure to moist winds, distance from the sea, ocean currents, and seasonal circulation can alter local conditions.
- Use gradual transitions between humid forest, open woodland, grassland, steppe, and desert. Sharp local boundaries need a physical cause such as a ridge, coast, or abrupt change in elevation.
- Rain shadows are strongest near the downwind side of major highlands and weaken or change with distance; do not turn every leeward valley into a desert.
- Coastal climates can be mild but are not automatically wet. Exposed shores, cold currents, and seasonal winds can create cool or dry coasts.
- This is a working model for a world with familiar physical geography. Unusual phenomena may locally affect weather or ecosystems later, but they are not used here to explain every climate pattern.

### 14.2 Relative temperature belts

- **Nirvan:** coldest overall, with cold highlands in the northern/northwestern arc, cool forests at suitable lower elevations, and a milder southern transitional belt. Seasonal snow and ice are plausible in high areas; permanent ice is not assumed across the entire region.
- **Ilmar:** broadly temperate, with a wetter western/northwestern margin in exposed places and more seasonally dry areas farther inland or toward the south. The Ilan Basin is potentially fertile where river water and rainfall overlap.
- **Orath:** highly varied because of the Eastern Spine, Great Highlands, and inland basin. Elevation creates cool highlands; windward slopes can be wet, while selected leeward areas and the enclosed basin can be drier.
- **Tharima:** warmest and driest overall in its interior, with semi-arid transition zones, locally wetter northern margins, and coastal variations. Some highlands are cooler than surrounding lowlands.
- **Azmar:** maritime moderation varies by latitude, island size, relief, and currents. Windward islands may be wet while nearby low or leeward islands can be seasonal or dry.

These are relative tendencies, not fixed temperature numbers or a claim that every point follows the regional average.

### 14.3 Working wind and moisture model

Until a latitude grid and circulation model are drawn, use the following as **testable design assumptions**, not immutable canon:

1. Moisture-bearing winds reach parts of Ilmar from the western ocean, supporting wetter coastal pockets and upland-facing slopes. Their exact prevailing direction and seasonal strength remain open.
2. Some western and northwestern slopes in Ilmar receive more rain than interior lowlands, while the Western Ilmar Uplands produce a modest-to-strong rain-shadow gradient toward selected inland and southeastern areas.
3. Orath's western slopes can receive moisture from the central seas, but the Eastern Spine and associated highlands reduce or redirect moisture into the interior and eastern slopes. Do not assume the entire east coast is dry: local exposure and currents remain decisive.
4. Tharima receives irregular seasonal rain. The northern transitional belt catches more of the available moisture than the deeper interior; isolated storms can create destructive floods without sustaining permanent rivers.
5. Nirvan's highlands intercept moisture and snow. The southern belt may receive maritime moisture from the Sea of Stillness, while exposed northern/eastern coasts can be windy and cold without necessarily being the wettest.
6. Azmar's small islands respond strongly to wind direction and local relief. Windward slopes may be lush; leeward sides can be noticeably drier over short distances.

**Open variable:** whether the world has an Earth-like axial tilt and seasonal cycle. This draft uses familiar seasons as a convenient provisional model but does not define astronomical parameters.

### 14.4 Natural-region map units

These labels identify broad physical/ecological regions, not cultures or political territories.

#### Ilmar

- **BIO-IL-01 — Western Coastal Temperate Belt:** mixed woodland, grassland, and coastal wetlands where rainfall, soil, and shelter permit. Exposure varies along the coast.
- **BIO-IL-02 — Western Upland Mosaic:** cooler uplands, wooded slopes, open ridges, and headwater valleys. Not all uplands are densely forested.
- **BIO-IL-03 — Central Forest Mosaic:** forest blocks, clearings, wet hollows, and rolling hills. Forest extent varies with elevation and moisture.
- **BIO-IL-04 — Ilan River Lowlands:** floodplains, tributary valleys, wetlands, and fertile terraces near reliable water. Flood risk and soil drainage vary by subregion.
- **BIO-IL-05 — Open Plains:** grasslands and mixed open woodland, with local farming potential where soils and rainfall allow. Do not assume all plains are naturally treeless.
- **BIO-IL-06 — Southern/Drier Transition:** a gradual shift toward open woodland and steppe where rainfall declines; exact boundary remains open.

#### Orath

- **BIO-OR-01 — Western Windward Slopes:** locally moist foothills and valleys facing the central seas; vegetation ranges from forest to open woodland with elevation.
- **BIO-OR-02 — Great Highland Mosaic:** cool upland forests, grassland, rocky ridges, and alpine-like zones only at sufficiently high elevations.
- **BIO-OR-03 — Eastern Spine High Country:** cooler ridges and headwaters with strong local exposure effects; snow persistence depends on height and season.
- **BIO-OR-04 — Inland Basin Drylands:** steppe, scrub, seasonal wetlands, and possible salt flats around the provisional closed lake. The exact balance depends on inflow versus evaporation.
- **BIO-OR-05 — Eastern Leeward Valleys:** patchy dry woodland and steppe in rain-shadowed areas, with wetter corridors near rivers and exposed slopes.
- **BIO-OR-06 — Southern Coastal Transition:** varied foothills and coastal plains near the strait; maritime influence may soften dryness locally.

#### Tharima

- **BIO-SA-01 — Northern Semi-arid Steppe:** seasonal grass, shrubs, and scattered trees near more reliable drainage. Green-up may be brief and irregular.
- **BIO-SA-02 — Interior Arid Belt:** sparse scrub, bare ground, and dry channels. This is not one uninterrupted sand sea; rocky deserts and gravel plains are possible.
- **BIO-SA-03 — Basin-floor Mosaic:** salt-tolerant vegetation, temporary wetlands after rain, exposed mudflats, and dry lake beds where the water balance supports them.
- **BIO-SA-04 — Upland Refuges:** cooler slopes and localized vegetation around higher terrain, springs, and shaded valleys.
- **BIO-SA-05 — Coastal Arid Belt:** dry coastal plains with locally moderated temperatures; fog or coastal moisture is possible only if winds and currents support it.
- **BIO-SA-06 — Wadi Corridors:** narrow bands of vegetation along channels that receive episodic runoff. Their green appearance is seasonal, not guaranteed year-round.

#### Nirvan

- **BIO-NI-01 — Northern Cold-Coast Mosaic:** cold-tolerant woodland, heath, tundra-like vegetation, or bare rock according to latitude and exposure; do not apply one biome to the entire northern shore.
- **BIO-NI-02 — Highland Cold Zones:** rocky uplands, cold grassland, persistent snow patches, and possible glaciers only at sufficient elevation and under the chosen climate.
- **BIO-NI-03 — Boreal Forest Belt:** coniferous or mixed cold forest at suitable lower and middle elevations, broken by lakes, wetlands, and exposed ground.
- **BIO-NI-04 — Southern Transitional Forests:** more varied woodland and grassland in the lower southern belt, with milder seasonal conditions than the highlands.
- **BIO-NI-05 — Fjord and Maritime Valleys:** localized wet valleys where topography and maritime exposure concentrate moisture. Fjords alone do not guarantee dense forest.

#### Azmar

- **BIO-AZ-01 — Humid Windward Islands:** forested slopes and short streams on islands receiving reliable maritime rainfall.
- **BIO-AZ-02 — Seasonal Leeward Islands:** scrub, open woodland, and seasonal watercourses where relief creates a rain shadow.
- **BIO-AZ-03 — Low Dry Islands:** limited surface freshwater, salt-tolerant plants, and dependence on stored rainwater or groundwater where present.
- **BIO-AZ-04 — Cool Exposed Islands:** wind-shaped vegetation and limited sheltered growing areas on exposed or higher-latitude islands.

### 14.5 Climate-to-water reconciliation

- **Ilan River:** its headwaters need enough rainfall or upland storage to sustain the main channel. The lower floodplain may flood seasonally; do not assign a huge delta until sediment and coastal conditions support it.
- **Ilmar western rivers:** shorter and more variable than Ilan in the current model; their flow depends on which side of the upland divide they drain and local exposure.
- **Orath western rivers:** their catchments should be wetter on selected windward slopes, with flow becoming more seasonal toward drier interiors.
- **Orath closed basin and Lake L-003:** water level depends on inflow versus evaporation. A persistent large freshwater lake is not assumed; a shallow fluctuating lake, wetland, or salt-flat phase is more plausible for a dry basin.
- **Tharima wadis:** flood pulses may be powerful but short. Some channels terminate in depressions, and only selected ones reach the coast.
- **Nirvan lakes and rivers:** snowmelt can create a seasonal high-flow period. Exact freeze duration and river permanence remain open until latitude and elevation are chosen.
- **Azmar freshwater:** a settlement site must have a credible source: rainfall storage, a spring, groundwater, a stream, or regular supply. Island size alone does not guarantee water.

### 14.6 Map quality checks

1. Do not place a dense rainforest in a dry rain shadow without a local moisture source.
2. Do not place a broad permanent river in a dry basin without reliable inflow or groundwater.
3. Do not place permanent glaciers at low elevations without a deliberately unusual climate.
4. Do not assume every coast is humid or every highland is forested.
5. Keep biome boundaries gradual except where relief or other strong physical causes justify sharper transitions.
6. Use the closed-basin lake as a climate-sensitive feature, not a fixed permanent blue patch.
7. Revisit every proposed river and lake after the wind, rainfall, and biome map is drafted.
8. Do not yet infer cities, borders, civilizations, or historical events from the biome labels.

- Prevailing winds by season and the strength/direction of ocean currents.

- Whether Orath's closed basin remains closed after contours are drafted.
- Exact biome boundaries and the reliability of springs in Tharima.
