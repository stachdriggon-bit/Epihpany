# EPIPHANY — Foundational Sorcery Model

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Provisional foundation for development; not a finalized explanation of every magical ability.  
**Purpose:** Record the shared principles for sorcerers without forcing abilities into fixed branches or deciding character-specific mechanics before characters are designed.

## 1. Core approach

EPIPHANY does not currently use a finalized universal taxonomy of sorcery branches. Build the system from individual abilities and their actual behavior first. Any future labels or groupings are optional organizational tools, not rules that determine what magic can or cannot do.

Do not invent a rule merely to fill an open question. Keep the foundation concise and return to a specific detail when a character, story, or continuity problem makes it necessary.

## 2. Becoming a sorcerer

The current working model is:

- When a person becomes a sorcerer, their **basic ability** takes shape.
- A **restriction symbol** appears on their hand and remains throughout their life.
- The sorcerer becomes instinctively aware of the general nature of their basic ability, much as a person naturally knows they have a hand and how to move it.
- The sorcerer also knows their restriction within the world's established restriction-symbol system. The symbol identifies its known category; it does not reveal every practical consequence or teach the sorcerer how to manage it.
- This immediate awareness does not provide complete knowledge of every application, extension, or interaction. Those require experience, practice, and discovery.
- No ritual, extra stage, or additional transformation condition is established here.

The reason a particular person receives a particular ability is unknown. Do not assume it can be predicted from personality, interests, ancestry, or another trait. An underlying reason may exist, but it has not been established.

## 3. Basic abilities and their growth

- Each sorcerer has a basic ability that defines their starting point.
- Basic abilities may resemble one another. Similarity does not require identical extensions, restrictions, methods, or mastery.
- An ability may grow broader as its user's skill develops. This is an important difference between an ordinary sorcerer and a highly skilled one.
- Growth can involve better execution, more creative applications, or discovering possibilities that were not obvious at first.
- Some extensions may be logical deductions from the ability; others may be discovered through experimentation and deeper understanding. Not every ability needs to develop in the same way.
- Greater skill does not make every imaginable effect possible. Abilities have limits, even though no single numerical ceiling or universal formula has been established.
- Do not assume that broader ability necessarily means greater destructive force; precision, versatility, reliability, and clever application can also distinguish a powerful sorcerer.

## 4. Ability, restriction, skill, and natural limits

Keep these concepts distinct:

- **Basic ability:** the nature of what the sorcerer can do.
- **Individual restriction:** a separate constraint whose effect depends on the type of restriction. Two sorcerers with similar basic abilities may have different restrictions.
- **Skill and mastery:** how well the sorcerer understands, controls, develops, and applies their ability.
- **Natural limits:** things the ability cannot accomplish, even with high skill; the exact boundary may not be obvious in advance.
- **Knowledge:** what the sorcerer has observed, inferred, learned, or misunderstood.

A skilled sorcerer may invent clever ways to use an ability while respecting the restriction. This is adaptation, not removal or breach of the restriction. It does not follow that every restriction can be bypassed through ingenuity.

The effect of a restriction depends on its type. Do not assume all restrictions limit the same aspect of an ability or that one rule describes every restriction.

## 5. Understanding another sorcerer's ability

There is no universal rule that lets a sorcerer fully analyze another ability by looking at it.

- Observing a sorcerer may reveal the general nature of their basic ability, but does not reveal all its possible applications, limits, or hidden aspects.
- Further understanding may come from observation, inference, experience, or other means, and may remain incomplete or mistaken.
- Some abilities—such as certain perception or analysis abilities—may reveal information that ordinary observation cannot. What they can reveal depends on the specific ability and the interaction involved.
- Strength alone does not guarantee complete understanding. There is no absolute rule covering every ability or encounter.

## 6. Exceptional abilities

Abilities such as copying, perception, or analysis can raise questions about what is copied or perceived, how long an effect lasts, what can be controlled, and how restrictions interact. These details are **not** to be answered by a universal rule in advance.

Resolve such mechanics when designing the relevant character, based on the nature of that character's ability and the ability it interacts with. Preserve internal consistency within each case, but do not force all exceptional abilities into one template.

## 7. No fixed branch taxonomy for now

The former seven-branch catalogue was a provisional experiment and is superseded. Do not use it as the current law of magic or force a character's ability into one of its categories.

Start with the actual ability: what it does, what its natural limits are, what its restriction changes, what the user knows, and how skill affects its use. Consider broad labels only later, if they genuinely help organize established examples without constraining them.

The exploratory note [Perception and Detection](PERCEPTION-AND-DETECTION.md) remains a set of character-design questions, not an official branch.

## 8. Open questions deliberately left open

- Whether magic uses a distinct energy resource, and how that might relate to using an ability.
- The broader source or sources of magic, if any.
- Whether and how abilities can be learned, borrowed, copied, inherited, or otherwise acquired beyond the basic transformation model above.
- The detailed mechanics of any particular ability or restriction.
- Whether historical sorcery worked differently before the emergence of restrictions.
- Any deeper connection between individual restrictions and cosmic laws.

Do not answer these questions by implication. In particular, the relationship between magical-energy depletion and lifespan loss remains distinct from the question of what fuels ordinary ability use.

## 9. Character-first workflow

When creating a sorcerer, define only what is needed:

1. Basic ability and what its general nature lets the sorcerer do.
2. Natural limits that matter to the character or story.
3. Individual restriction and its specific effect.
4. Skill, current mastery, and known applications.
5. What the character knows versus what remains undiscovered.
6. Relevant interactions with other characters' abilities, if needed.

Keep proposals provisional until explicitly approved. Do not design every edge case before a character or story needs it.
