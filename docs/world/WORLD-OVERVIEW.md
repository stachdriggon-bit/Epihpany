# EPIPHANY — Unified World Overview

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Author-facing overview of the current working setting. It combines existing references; it does not promote provisional geography or story ideas to final canon.  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-28

> **How to read this document:** “Current rule” means an established worldbuilding principle. “Working geography” means the current map plan, which can still change. “Open” means no answer should be invented or implied. For detailed facts, use the linked source registry rather than duplicating its record here.

## 1. The identity of the world

EPIPHANY is a geographically recognizable world of landmasses, seas, mountains, rivers, forests, drylands, islands, settlements, routes, and ordinary social life. Unusual phenomena exist within that natural and social world; they do not replace it.

The setting is intended to support stories at different scales: intimate personal choices, local community problems, connected regional histories, and occasional wider mysteries. Work, family, craft, trade, affection, humor, ambition, politics, conflict, and learning matter as much as extraordinary events.

The world's ultimate origin is unresolved. The oldest surviving record is not necessarily the world's first event. No single civilization is assumed to be the origin of all peoples, and there is no required master explanation connecting every mystery.

## 2. The known map — current working geography

The present map plan organizes five principal mapped regions: four named continents and one archipelago region. It is schematic and not to scale.

| Region | Current relative position | Working physical character | Status boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ilmar / إيلمار** | West | Broadly temperate; river basin, forests, plains, uplands, estuaries, and coasts | Regional pattern and river plan are provisional; local variation matters |
| **Orath / أوراث** | East, north/east of Tharima | Highlands, mountain passes, inland basins, mineral zones, and coasts | Relief and basin model are provisional; lake and settlement links must be checked |
| **Tharima / ثاريما** | South | Arid and semi-arid interiors, transitional steppes, seasonal wadis, and distinct water basins | Do not assume all channels connect or all communities share one culture |
| **Nirvan / نيرفان** | North | Cold forests, highlands, lakes, irregular coasts, and locally difficult routes | Not all of the north is permanently frozen; exact climate and drainage remain provisional |
| **Azmar / أزمار** | Separated archipelagos around central and outer maritime passages | Island groups, harbors, straits, navigation routes, and varied freshwater conditions | An island region, not currently classified as a continent or one unified culture |

This is the inventory mapped so far, **not** proof that the whole world contains exactly four continents. Other landmasses may exist beyond the current map.

### Seas and routes

The working geographic plan names the **Sea of Dawn** as the central maritime area; the **Sea of Stillness** lies farther north between southern Nirvan and northern/northwestern Orath; and the **Strait of Passage** is provisionally placed between southeastern Orath and northeastern Tharima. The **Distant Ocean** is a working label for outer waters, not a claim that the world's edge is known.

These relationships are useful for planning adjacency only. Exact coastlines, scale, coordinates, distances, travel times, currents, winds, and the final shape of routes are not fixed. Never infer them from a schematic drawing alone.

### Natural systems

- Rivers must flow downhill and follow plausible watersheds.
- The Ilan River is provisionally planned to cross the Ilan Basin and reach the Sea of Dawn; its detailed course and estuary depend on the final relief and coastline.
- Orath's inland basin currently uses a provisional closed-basin model with a shallow, climate-sensitive lake. Reopen this if the contour plan supports a natural outlet.
- Tharima's wadi systems are mostly seasonal and may be partly disconnected.
- Nirvan's rivers and lakes need explicit catchments, outlets, or closed-basin status.
- Azmar settlements require a plausible freshwater source; a good harbor does not guarantee fresh water.

These are geographic constraints and working proposals, not a complete physical atlas. See [Atlas](ATLAS.md) and [Geographic Foundation](GEOGRAPHIC-FOUNDATION.md).

## 3. Settlements and people

The five regional settlement registries contain **30 approved settlement names and stable IDs**. Approval of a name does not automatically finalize its precise location, role, population, political status, cultural identity, or connection to an anchor location. Use the registries as the source of truth for each named candidate.

Current regional society documents are design prompts, not finalized cultures or states. Useful directions include river and water management in parts of Ilmar; highland routes and passage systems in Orath; seasonal water infrastructure and caravan logistics in Tharima; coastal, forest, and seasonal navigation communities in Nirvan; and navigation, repair, trade, and mediation across Azmar. These themes must not become stereotypes: every region contains internal differences, rivalries, minorities, and changing identities.

No real-world language or cultural analogue should be assigned to a region just because of climate, terrain, or the sound of a few names. Languages, institutions, religions, state borders, succession systems, and named civilizations remain open until specifically developed and recorded.

## 4. Magic, unusual phenomena, and evidence

**EPIPHANY is a fantasy world.** Magic and wonder should sometimes shape daily life, craft, travel, ecology, and community decisions; they should not be limited to decorative effects or occasional world-ending threats. A magical setting can include familiar, reliable practices as well as rare, disputed, or poorly understood events.

The current references establish this fantasy direction but do not yet define a complete magic system. Who can use magic, its source, costs, methods, limits, and the full range of non-human creatures remain open. Do not invent universal mechanics while drafting a local scene. Use [Magic and Unusual Phenomena](MAGIC-AND-PHENOMENA.md) for the phenomenon template and continuity safeguards; its suggested hooks are design prompts, not established lore.

Phenomena can be well understood, partly understood, documented but unexplained, disputed, or uncertain because evidence is weak. Their distribution, scale, danger, and causes can differ from place to place. There is no universal division between “normal” and “magical” zones.

When a strange event occurs, distinguish at least four possibilities:
1. The world itself changed.
2. People's knowledge or interpretation changed.
3. The evidence is incomplete or was gathered for another purpose.
4. Multiple explanations remain plausible.

Do not make every phenomenon dangerous, world-changing, or connected to the same hidden cause. Experts may be mistaken without being uniformly incompetent, and reliable conclusions should sometimes be possible.

For every major clue, preserve its source, context, limits, competing interpretations, and who can access it. A legend, official report, character belief, or card description is not automatically objective truth.

## 5. History and chronology

World history should be developed at three scales:

- **Local history:** households, settlements, institutions, community disputes, and environmental change.
- **Connected history:** trade, migration, warfare, diplomacy, shared infrastructure, and movement of knowledge across regions.
- **Broad world history:** rare large-scale changes and questions about the nature of the world.

A distant event should affect a place only through a plausible route or mechanism: travel, trade, migration, institutions, politics, environment, or knowledge exchange. Not every region shares the same eras or changes at the same pace.

The Timeline's five labels — **Scattered Times**, **First Cities**, **Interwoven Routes**, **Age of Rebalancing**, and **Age of Contested Exploration** — remain organizing proposals, not finalized universal eras. Do not assign calendar years until the dating conventions and evidence justify them.

For each story or event, keep three clocks separate:
- **Event time:** when it happens in-world.
- **Knowledge time:** when a character or community learns or records something.
- **Player discovery time:** when the learner encounters it in the app.

The release order of stories need not match their in-world chronology. Use exact dates only when evidence supports that precision; otherwise use a range, relative order within a known sequence, or an explicitly unknown date.

## 6. Story and character continuity

The English-learning story library should remain useful on its own. Do not retroactively place every everyday, workplace, or reasoning story into EPIPHANY. Add a world link only when the connection is clear, useful, and continuity-safe.

A new side story may fit the established setting without becoming a major historical fact. Label it as a derived story for production purposes; its new events do not automatically revise global canon. If a story is meant to change history, record that decision and update affected source records deliberately.

Character records should distinguish motivation, strengths, limits, relationships, knowledge, and unresolved threads. A character must not know information they could not plausibly have learned. Card rarity is separate from narrative importance, and card text is not automatically an in-world source.

### Current lore-linked pilot

**The Two Records at the Low-Water Shore** is a six-episode B1-target pilot. Its provisional characters are **Tarin Vell (OR-CHAR-001)**, a boat repairer, and **Sella Orin (OR-CHAR-002)**, a local record keeper. Their different methods help investigate records created for different purposes.

The draft establishes a local investigation into changing shoreline evidence and the limits of two records. It does **not** establish a conspiracy, supernatural lake, ancient civilization, world-scale mystery, or definitive long-term cause. The pilot's event date and global era remain unknown; the episode order is only a local story sequence.

See [Story Roadmap](STORY-ROADMAP.md), [pilot character records](stories/LOW-WATER-SHORE-CHARACTERS.md), and [event registry](EVENT-REGISTRY.md).

## 7. Place-identity reconciliation: Ambrin and the Low-Water Shore

This is a deliberately explicit continuity rule until further evidence or an owner decision resolves the identity.

- **OR-CITY-04 — Ambrin / أمبرين** is the approved name of a settlement candidate whose working role is an inland-shore town by a fluctuating lake.
- **OR-LOC-02 — The Low-Water Shore** is the provisional anchor and the sole location reference used by the six-episode pilot.
- Their descriptions strongly overlap, but the existing material does not conclusively establish whether they are the same named settlement, an anchor description later to be given the Ambrin name, or related but distinct records.
- For current story, archive, map, and route work, use **OR-LOC-02 only** for the pilot. Do not show both as separate towns, create separate routes, or duplicate the same events under both IDs.
- Preserve both IDs in their source registries for now. Do not delete or merge either record until the identity decision is supported and recorded in the Decision Log.

**Current resolution:** the duplicate-place risk is resolved operationally; the in-world identity remains open. This is not a claim that the two names are definitely identical.

## 9. Rules for future additions

Before adding a new fact, event, character, culture, or location:

1. Search the source registries for a matching ID, name, alias, or description.
2. Identify whether the proposed detail is established, provisional, an in-world claim, disputed, or open.
3. Separate direct evidence from interpretation and from an author's idea.
4. Check geography, water, routes, travel, technology, chronology, and character knowledge where relevant.
5. Check whether an existing place or event would be duplicated.
6. Record consequential owner decisions in [Decision Log](DECISIONS-LOG.md); mark superseded choices rather than erasing their history.
7. Update source registries first, then dependent maps, stories, archive records, cards, and translations.
8. Keep unresolved questions visible. Do not fill a gap just because a timeline or collection interface would look cleaner with a date.
