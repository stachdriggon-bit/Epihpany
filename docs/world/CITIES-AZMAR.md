# EPIPHANY — Azmar: Candidate Island Settlements

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Provisional place and naming proposals; none are established canon yet.  
**Scope:** A small registry for settlement candidates across the Azmar archipelagos. Azmar is a mapped island-region, not currently classified as a continent.

## Ground rules

- Azmar consists of island groups and maritime passages; it is not assumed to be one contiguous landmass or one unified culture.
- Island climates, resources, travel times, and political relationships depend on latitude, currents, elevation, and the final map. Do not make every island tropical, isolated, or dependent on fishing.
- These are candidate settlements, not a complete list and not a claim that all islands are inhabited.
- Names are placeholders. They do not establish a shared Azmar language or real-world cultural analogue. See [Naming and Toponymy](NAMING-AND-TOPONYMY.md).
- Check [Anchor Locations](ANCHOR-LOCATIONS.md) and the [Atlas](ATLAS.md) before adding a harbor, island, or named passage. Compare candidates with the possible Azmar Port in the Atlas.

## Candidate settlement list

### AZ-CITY-01 — Selka Haven / سيلكا هيفن

**Working role:** A busy harbor serving as a transfer point between several island passages and longer sea routes.

**Everyday life:** Pilots, dock crews, food sellers, repairers, interpreters, and merchants coordinate arrivals that may be delayed by weather or navigation conditions. Not everyone in town works at the harbor.

**Story potential:** A new navigation channel saves time for some vessels but reduces traffic through a smaller neighboring harbor. Residents debate whether to cooperate, adapt, or seek compensation.

**Placement condition:** Requires a naturally usable harbor and multiple plausible sea routes. The map must support the claimed junction rather than merely placing several routes beside one another.

**Continuity note:** This may correspond to the Atlas concept SET-05 — Azmar Port. Keep one record and one stable ID if the concepts prove to be the same.

### AZ-CITY-02 — Tern Isle / جزيرة تيرن

**Working role:** A small island community responsible for maintaining navigation markers, warning signs, or a sheltered landing used by nearby routes.

**Everyday life:** Marker keepers, fishers or other local workers as the ecology allows, repairers, and families who host travelers during delays. Maintenance may be shared across neighboring islands.

**Story potential:** A marker is missing or misaligned. The cause may be damage, poor records, disagreement over responsibility, or deliberate interference; evidence must distinguish these possibilities.

**Placement condition:** Requires an island positioned near a route where a marker or safe landing has practical value.

**Continuity note:** Do not make the community important only because a mystery happens there. Its routine work should matter even in ordinary seasons.

### AZ-CITY-03 — Oruven / أوروفن

**Working role:** An inland settlement on a sufficiently large island, supported by a freshwater source and local agriculture or another viable supply system.

**Everyday life:** Growers, water-system maintainers, craftspeople, carriers, and boat crews connect the interior with coastal landing places. Goods moving inland may differ from goods traded at the shore.

**Story potential:** A new road to the coast changes which neighborhood benefits from trade. The disagreement reveals how transport links redistribute opportunity without requiring a grand conspiracy.

**Placement condition:** Requires enough land, freshwater, and suitable terrain to support a permanent inland settlement. Do not assume every island has a river or dependable spring.

**Continuity note:** This candidate helps avoid portraying Azmar as nothing but docks and boats.

### AZ-CITY-04 — Maret Quay / رصيف ماريت

**Working role:** A practical repair and resupply stop for short maritime routes, smaller craft, and crews waiting for suitable conditions.

**Everyday life:** Boat repairers, sail and rope workers, cooks, supply sellers, and families balance local needs against the uncertain arrival of vessels.

**Story potential:** A shortage of a common repair material forces crews to choose between waiting, improvising, or taking a longer route. Each option carries different risks and costs.

**Placement condition:** Requires a sheltered landing and a plausible supply chain for food, water, and repair materials.

**Continuity note:** Its role is dependable service, not necessarily wealth, political power, or a large fleet.

### AZ-CITY-05 — Vey Shoals / ضفاف ڤي

**Working role:** A settlement whose livelihood depends on navigating a shallow coastal area where channels and shoals can shift or become exposed.

**Everyday life:** Local pilots, fishers or shellfish gatherers where appropriate, boat builders, and mapmakers pass on practical knowledge that may be incomplete or out of date.

**Story potential:** Two charts disagree about a safe passage. Investigation shows that one chart records a different season, vessel depth, or year rather than simply being wrong.

**Placement condition:** Requires a credible shallow-water environment shaped by sediment, tides, currents, or other established local processes.

**Continuity note:** Avoid using “shifting waters” as a magical explanation unless the story separately establishes evidence for an unusual phenomenon.

### AZ-CITY-06 — Far Lantern / الفانوس البعيد

**Working role:** A remote island settlement with irregular connections to larger ports and a locally adapted way of managing supplies and communication.

**Everyday life:** Residents maintain essential equipment, exchange goods when vessels arrive, and make decisions based on what can realistically be stored or repaired locally. The settlement may have both permanent residents and seasonal visitors.

**Story potential:** A delayed vessel creates a practical shortage, prompting residents to decide what to share, substitute, or conserve. A message from outside arrives with old information and must be checked before action.

**Placement condition:** Requires a plausible reason for relative remoteness and enough local resources or reliable supply arrangements to sustain inhabitants.

**Continuity note:** Remoteness does not imply ignorance, cultural stagnation, or a secret civilization.

## Cross-settlement continuity rules

1. Stable IDs remain even if names change.
2. Aliases, local names, exonyms, and historical names belong in the same record unless they identify genuinely different places.
3. Search IDs, names, aliases, and functions before adding a harbor, island town, or passage settlement.
4. Other documents reference stable IDs and add only scene-specific information.
5. When a place changes, update this registry first, then check the Atlas, anchors, routes, stories, quests, characters, cards, and translations.
6. Exact island positions, currents, distances, political ties, and travel times remain open until the physical map supports them.
