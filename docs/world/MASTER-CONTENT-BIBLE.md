# EPIPHANY — Master Content Bible

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Foundational working reference  
**Scope:** Shared setting for stories, World exploration, characters, cards, and the in-app Lore archive.

## 0. Shared place registry and continuity

Place descriptions must be stored once in the appropriate regional city/settlement registry and reused by reference. Before inventing or writing about a city, town, landmark, harbor, or named district, search existing stable IDs, names, aliases, and descriptions to avoid duplicates. Keep stable IDs when names change; store aliases and historical names in the same record unless there is a clear in-world reason for separate places. Other files should reference the place ID and add only scene-specific information rather than copying the full description. When a place changes, update its source record and check dependent atlas entries, anchor locations, stories, quests, characters, cards, and translations.

Naming proposals should follow [Naming and Toponymy](NAMING-AND-TOPONYMY.md). Do not infer a region's language or cultural analogue from its climate, terrain, or early name proposals. Keep current names provisional until they have been checked for pronunciation, distinction, unintended real-world associations, transliteration, and continuity.

## 1. Core identity

For a concise synthesis of the current setting and the boundaries between established principles, provisional geography, and open questions, see [Unified World Overview](WORLD-OVERVIEW.md). The overview summarizes this bible and the source registries; it does not override them or promote provisional material to canon.

EPIPHANY takes place in a world that feels geographically recognizable: continents, seas, mountains, rivers, forests, deserts, islands, cities, trade routes, and ordinary human concerns. Unusual phenomena exist within this world rather than replacing its natural and social foundations.

The world should support long-form, layered storytelling without copying another intellectual property.

For geographic terminology, distinguish continents from archipelagos and other mapped regions. The current working map names four continents — Ilmar, Orath, Tharima, and Nirvan — plus the Azmar archipelagos. This is the current mapped inventory, **not** a declaration that the entire world contains exactly four continents; the world's full extent remains open.

## 2. Canon principles

- The world's ultimate origin is unknown, including to the author-facing world bible. The earliest surviving record is not necessarily the beginning of the world.
- Myths, legends, official histories, and archaeological interpretations are not automatically true.
- Civilizations have mixed origins. Some develop independently; others exchange ideas, migrate, trade, conquer, or lose contact. There is no required universal first civilization from which all others descend.
- History is not a single sequence of ever-greater empires. States can rise through geography, politics, economics, knowledge, environment, or unusual phenomena; they can also decline for ordinary reasons.
- A conquered society may leave a stronger legacy than its conqueror. A surviving state may lose influence without disappearing.
- Not every story, character, card, or mystery must connect to one central plot.
- Everyday life, humor, affection, ambition, work, trade, politics, conflict, and family matters are as real as extraordinary events.
- Not every unusual phenomenon is dangerous, world-changing, or connected to another phenomenon.
- Experts may be mistaken, but they should not be written as uniformly incompetent merely to preserve a mystery.
- Some mysteries should be solved, some partly clarified, and some left open. Unresolved questions must not all be secretly explained by one master answer.

## 3. The three historical scales

1. **Local histories:** settlements, families, institutions, regional conflicts, and local environmental change.
2. **Connected histories:** trade, migration, warfare, alliances, cultural exchange, and movement of knowledge across regions.
3. **Broad world history:** rare world-scale changes and questions about the world's nature. This scale should remain less common than local and regional history.

Events spread through plausible causal paths. A distant event should affect a region only when a route, institution, migration, environment, political relationship, or other mechanism connects them.

Use the [Master Event Registry](EVENT-REGISTRY.md) for stable event IDs and source/status tracking, the [Local History Register](LOCAL-HISTORIES.md) for place-by-place development, and [Communities and Local Institutions](COMMUNITIES-INSTITUTIONS.md) for proposed social mechanisms. These are linked working references: they do not turn provisional story scenes into accepted historical canon.

## 4. Phenomena and evidence

EPIPHANY is a fantasy setting: magic and wonder should meaningfully shape some parts of daily life, craft, travel, ecology, and community decisions, not appear only as decorative effects. The detailed mechanics of magic, who can use it, its costs, and the catalogue of creatures remain open until deliberately designed. Use [Magic and Unusual Phenomena](MAGIC-AND-PHENOMENA.md) as the working framework; its location-specific hooks are prompts, not canon facts.

Phenomena may be:
- well understood and practically managed;
- partly understood;
- documented but unexplained;
- disputed because records or witnesses conflict;
- uncertain because the evidence is weak.

Their distribution varies by region. There is no strict division between “normal” and “magical” zones. Changes may be sudden, gradual, or slow processes that cross a threshold abruptly. Most effects should be small or medium; world-redefining events should be rare.

Sometimes the world changes; sometimes knowledge changes; sometimes the available evidence cannot yet distinguish between them. Still, reliable conclusions must be possible in some cases.

## 5. Canon and knowledge layers

For every significant claim, distinguish:
- objective reference facts accepted by the project;
- reliable in-world evidence;
- interpretations or hypotheses;
- folklore, propaganda, mistakes, and disputed accounts;
- intentionally open questions.

A Lore entry should identify what is known, the source of the knowledge, what remains uncertain, and whether the entry contains spoilers.

## 6. Maps and cards

Maps are mutually consistent reference layers: world, physical/topographic, climate/biomes, political, historical, trade/routes, phenomena, regional/city, and story-exploration maps. Illustrative artwork is not authoritative geography.

Cards represent established or intentionally mysterious parts of the setting; they do not dictate world history. Character-card rarity is separate from narrative importance. Collecting should be enjoyable without real-money purchases. Cards may be earned through exploration, stories, achievements, or other in-app play.

## 7. Workflow

Use [Magic and Unusual Phenomena](MAGIC-AND-PHENOMENA.md) to design magical effects with clear observable behavior, scope, limits, evidence, and consequences before making them recurring canon. For region-grounded daily-life proposals, use [Regional Life and Cultural Patterns](REGIONAL-LIFE-AND-CULTURAL-PATTERNS.md); for settlement-level prompts across the 30 approved candidate names, use [City Culture Profiles](CITY-CULTURE-PROFILES.md); for five deeper prototypes with provisional local casts, use [Selected City Life and Local Cast](CITY-LIFE-AND-LOCAL-CAST.md). These are proposal packs, not accepted canon by default.

Build in this order where practical:
1. physical geography and map relationships;
2. climate, water systems, and natural routes;
3. regional settlements and cultures;
4. local and connected histories;
5. characters, institutions, factions, and events;
6. mysteries, evidence, and links among stories;
7. implementation in Stories, World, cards, and Lore.

This order is a guide, not a ban on creative discoveries. New story ideas may reveal a missing piece, but changes must be reconciled with the reference.
