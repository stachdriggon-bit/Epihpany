# EPIPHANY — Naming and Toponymy

**Version:** 0.2  
**Status:** Provisional framework; no regional naming system is finalized.  
**Scope:** Place names, personal names, language contact, transliteration, aliases, and naming continuity across the world.

## 1. Purpose

Names should feel as though they developed through generations of use by people who live in the world. They should not be random fantasy syllables, nor should a continent automatically imitate one real-world language or culture.

This document is a working method, not a complete conlang specification. It does not establish the world's languages, ethnicities, or cultural analogues.

## 2. Principles

1. **No default real-world analogue.** A region must not be assigned an Arabic, Persian, Scandinavian, or other real-world naming identity simply because of its climate, geography, or first batch of names.
2. **Accepted names stay canon until explicitly changed.** The six names in each of the five current settlement registries (30 names total across Ilmar, Orath, Tharima, Nirvan, and the Azmar archipelagos) are approved by the project owner. Their IDs and approved spellings are stable. Settlement existence, precise placement, and links to anchor locations may still be reconciled without silently renaming a place.
3. **Geography does not dictate language.** Climate and terrain can shape settlement patterns, but they do not determine what names sound like.
4. **Regions are not linguistically uniform.** A place may have a local name, an older name, a trade-language name, an outsider's name, and a modern administrative spelling.
5. **Variation needs causes.** Naming differences can emerge from migration, trade, conquest, neighboring languages, religious or scholarly traditions, sound change, and imperfect record-keeping. Do not add every layer to every place; use only what the story needs.
6. **Names should be pronounceable and distinguishable.** Important names should be reasonably easy to read aloud, not too similar to nearby names, and stable enough for players to remember.
7. **Sound is not proof of etymology.** A name that resembles a real word may create an unintended association even when no such origin was intended. Check both written forms and likely pronunciation.
8. **Do not encode spoilers accidentally.** Names may preserve history, but they should not disclose the answer to a mystery unless that is intentional and supported by the evidence.

## 3. Naming layers to consider

A place may have one or more of these layers, as needed:

- **Local endonym:** the name used by people from the place.
- **Exonym:** a name used by outsiders or neighboring communities.
- **Historical name:** a former name retained in records, ruins, songs, or family memory.
- **Trade name:** a shortened or adapted form used by travelers and merchants.
- **Administrative form:** a standardized spelling used by a state, mapmaker, or institution.
- **Translated or transliterated form:** a form used when a name is represented in another language or writing system.

These are options, not mandatory lore. Keep aliases and historical forms in the existing place registry under the same stable ID unless there is a clear reason they refer to different places.

## 4. Regional sound patterns: how to develop them

Do not lock every region into a single formula yet. Instead, build a small, testable pattern from several names that belong to a plausible community or historical layer.

For each proposed pattern, record:
- common sound combinations and syllable shapes;
- sounds or clusters that are rare or absent;
- typical word endings, if any;
- how names change when borrowed by neighboring speakers;
- pronunciation and a consistent romanization approach;
- known exceptions, such as old names or imported names.

A regional pattern should be a tendency, not a rule that every person, city, river, and mountain must obey. Avoid designing a pattern from only one or two names. Do not make every region's names match its climate stereotype.

## 5. Workflow for creating a name

1. **Define the place first:** its role, geography, likely inhabitants, and the story purpose it serves.
2. **Choose the naming context:** who named it, approximately when, and who uses the name now. Leave these unknown if the story does not need them.
3. **Generate several candidates:** test different sound shapes rather than committing to the first attractive name.
4. **Audit associations:** check the name in English and Arabic script/transliteration for obvious words, strong real-world associations, awkward meanings, and unintended jokes. Other languages can be checked when relevant.
5. **Check pronunciation and distinction:** make sure readers can reasonably pronounce it and that it is not easily confused with another established name.
6. **Search the registries:** compare stable IDs, names, aliases, and descriptions before adding a place.
7. **Record the decision status:** provisional, accepted, or open. Do not quietly promote a candidate to canon.
8. **Propagate approved changes:** update the source registry, then check the Atlas, anchor locations, stories, quests, character records, cards, and translations.

## 6. Arabic script and translation policy

Arabic spellings currently supplied beside candidate names are pronunciation aids, not proof that a name has Arabic etymology or that the region's language resembles Arabic. They should be reviewed when the name itself is reviewed.

For each name that will appear in the app, distinguish:
- the canonical in-world spelling or romanized form;
- a pronunciation guide, if useful;
- Arabic transliteration or localized form;
- actual meaning or etymology, only when deliberately established in canon.

Do not invent an etymology merely to justify a sound. Do not translate a proper name semantically unless the in-world name or the localization policy calls for it.

## 8. Continuity checklist

Before a name becomes accepted, ask:
- Does it fit the naming context we actually know, rather than a real-world stereotype?
- Is the intended sound distinct from names already in the registry?
- Does its spelling suggest an unintended real word or cultural association?
- Are its pronunciation and Arabic rendering consistent enough for the app?
- Are aliases and older names stored on the same stable record?
- Does the name reveal a mystery answer earlier than intended?
- Has the name's status been recorded without silently changing other canon?

## 9. Open decisions

- Which languages and writing traditions exist, and where are they used?
- Which naming patterns are shared across regions because of contact, and which are locally distinctive?
- What romanization and Arabic transliteration conventions should the app use?
- Which current candidate names survive a naming audit, and which should be replaced?

These questions remain open until they materially help a story or product feature. This framework should grow through examples, not become a large language-design project before the world needs one.
