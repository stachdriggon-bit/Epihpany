# EPIPHANY — Nirvan: Candidate Settlements

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Provisional place and naming proposals; none are established canon yet.  
**Scope:** A first small registry of settlement candidates for Nirvan, the northern continent in the current working map.

## Ground rules

- Nirvan is the northern continent in the current working map; exact coastlines, relief, watersheds, climate boundaries, and scale remain provisional.
- Cold forests, uplands, lakes, coasts, and seasonal travel conditions may coexist. Nirvan is not assumed to be permanently frozen or culturally uniform.
- These candidates are not a complete settlement list and do not need all to survive later map development.
- Names are working placeholders, not a finalized language system or an intended analogue to any real-world culture. Follow [Naming and Toponymy](NAMING-AND-TOPONYMY.md) before accepting names.
- Before creating another place, check this registry and [Anchor Locations](ANCHOR-LOCATIONS.md). In particular, NI-CITY-01 may overlap with the existing Last Warm Dock anchor.

## Candidate settlement list

### NI-CITY-01 — Orven Reach / أورڤن ريتش

**Working role:** A sheltered harbor where vessels can repair, resupply, and wait for a safe travel window along the northern coast.

**Everyday life:** Dock workers, fishers, rope-makers, repair crews, food sellers, and families who maintain storage sheds and weather markers. The harbor's importance rises and falls with the reliability of nearby passages.

**Story potential:** A dock expansion makes larger vessels safer but reduces access for small local boats. Residents disagree over the costs and benefits without either side needing to be a villain.

**Placement condition:** Requires a naturally protected inlet and plausible access to freshwater and supplies.

**Continuity note:** This may be the named form of anchor NI-LOC-01 — The Last Warm Dock. Compare their roles before treating them as separate places.

### NI-CITY-02 — Pellisar / بيليسار

**Working role:** A lakeside settlement connecting small-boat traffic with roads and trails between nearby communities.

**Everyday life:** Fishers, boatwrights, carriers, reed workers where wetlands permit, and traders moving goods between water and land routes. Ice or seasonal weather may affect some activities, but conditions should not be assumed identical every year.

**Story potential:** A familiar crossing becomes unreliable after several seasons of unusual water levels. Residents compare observations and old records before deciding whether to alter the route.

**Placement condition:** Requires a lake with a suitable landing and a credible connection to surrounding settlements.

**Continuity note:** Do not assume the lake is mysterious simply because its level varies.

### NI-CITY-03 — Varnholt / ڤارن هولت

**Working role:** A forest-edge town that supplies timber, repaired tools, preserved food, and other necessities to nearby settlements.

**Everyday life:** Foresters, sawyers, carpenters, storekeepers, carriers, and households planning work around short or difficult travel seasons. Timber use is governed by local needs and resource limits rather than an assumption of endless forest.

**Story potential:** A supply contract demands more timber than the nearby forest can sustainably provide. The town must negotiate alternatives and face the costs of an earlier decision.

**Placement condition:** Requires accessible woodland and a plausible transport route. Do not position it where timber cannot be moved economically.

**Continuity note:** Its defining role is supply and forest work, not a generic “mysterious northern village.”

### NI-CITY-04 — Iskell / إسكِل

**Working role:** An upland settlement supporting a difficult pass or exposed high route.

**Everyday life:** Guides, repairers, pack handlers, weather observers, innkeepers, and residents whose lives continue when travelers are absent. Local knowledge is valuable but not infallible.

**Story potential:** A new warning system reduces accidents but also closes a route more often than merchants consider necessary. Characters compare safety records, seasonal experience, and economic costs.

**Placement condition:** Requires a real upland corridor connecting useful destinations. A pass must not cross an impossible ridge or ignore watershed and terrain logic.

**Continuity note:** Avoid making the settlement's entire identity depend on perpetual snow or extreme danger.

### NI-CITY-05 — Mireva / ميريفا

**Working role:** A community near wetlands or a lake outlet, known for watercraft suited to shallow channels and for managing seasonal water access.

**Everyday life:** Fishers, boat makers, plant gatherers, path maintainers, and people who guide visitors through routes that change with water levels.

**Story potential:** A mapped channel becomes impassable. Some residents argue that the map is outdated; others suspect a recent change in water flow. Field observations can narrow the possibilities.

**Placement condition:** Requires a credible wetland or lake-outlet system with suitable local ecology.

**Continuity note:** Wetlands are working landscapes, not automatically haunted or untouched wilderness.

### NI-CITY-06 — Caerun / كايرون

**Working role:** A market town in a transitional belt where colder northern routes meet milder inland or coastal routes.

**Everyday life:** Traders, farmers where conditions allow, craftspeople, carriers, and travelers exchanging goods whose availability changes with season and distance.

**Story potential:** A new route shifts the timing of arrivals and threatens one established market day, forcing residents to decide whether to adapt the calendar or defend old agreements.

**Placement condition:** Requires a plausible transition zone and route junction. Do not assume a sharp climatic border merely because the town is a meeting point.

**Continuity note:** Its role is exchange between routes and communities, not proof that all northern peoples share one culture.

## Cross-settlement continuity rules

1. Stable IDs remain even if names change.
2. Store aliases and historical names in the same record unless they identify genuinely distinct places.
3. Search existing IDs, names, aliases, and functions before creating another settlement.
4. Other documents reference IDs and add scene-specific detail instead of copying full entries.
5. Update this registry first when a settlement changes, then check the Atlas, anchors, stories, quests, characters, cards, and translations.
6. Exact positions, resources, political affiliations, routes, and dates remain open until explicitly established.
