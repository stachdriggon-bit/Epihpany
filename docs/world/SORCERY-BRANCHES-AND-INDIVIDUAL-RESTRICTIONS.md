# EPIPHANY — Sorcery Branches and Individual Restrictions

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Working model — provisional; not a finalized universal taxonomy.  
**Purpose:** Preserve the current design of sorcery operations and individual restrictions so later character tests can refine the model without silently changing earlier decisions. This is worldbuilding documentation only; it does not change the app runtime.

## 1. How to read this document

This document separates three kinds of statements:

- **Established foundations:** principles already recorded in the worldbuilding references.
- **Working proposals:** the current branch model and restriction-classification hypothesis, open to testing and revision.
- **Character test results:** not yet recorded. Do not fill this section with invented character abilities until the project owner approves a test scenario.

This document complements, rather than replaces, [Cosmic Laws, Magical Restrictions, and Anomalies](COSMIC-LAWS-AND-ANOMALIES.md) and [Magic and Unusual Phenomena](MAGIC-AND-PHENOMENA.md).

## 2. Established foundations

The following principles are already part of the project's current reference:

1. Magical access is character-specific. Not everyone can use magic.
2. A character's ability, knowledge, control, experience, and mastery are distinct.
3. Individual restrictions constrain how a sorcerer's ability operates. They do not necessarily switch the ability off; they may distort an effect, reduce precision or stability, alter a response, or make use difficult under particular conditions.
4. Learning to predict or adapt to a restriction does not remove it.
5. A visible symbol appears on a sorcerer's hand when they become a sorcerer, remains throughout life, and is visible to others.
6. The hand symbol reveals the **category** of the person's restriction, not the exact individual restriction, powers, skill, or mastery. The exact restriction is learned through practice and experience.
7. The number and names of restriction categories are not finalized. Existing designs for the symbols/categories must not be replaced by names or designs invented in this working model.
8. A failure or unusual result is not automatically caused by a sorcerer's restriction. The environment, the target, the ability's own limits, and other conditions may also matter.
9. Cosmic restrictions, individual restrictions, and anomalies must remain distinct unless a future explicit decision establishes a relationship.

## 3. Working model: main domain and shared operations

**Proposal to test:** Sorcery can be described using separate layers rather than rigid character classes.

- **Main ability domain:** the kind of target, material, process, or phenomenon a sorcerer's ability fundamentally concerns.
- **Operation/branch:** what the sorcerer does to or with that target.
- **Individual restriction:** the specific limitation that changes how that sorcerer's ability operates.
- **Observed result:** what actually happens in a defined situation.
- **Knowledge and mastery:** what the sorcerer understands, can reliably perform, or has learned to anticipate.

Under this proposal, sorcerers share a common conceptual set of operations, but this does **not** mean every sorcerer can perform every operation on every target. A main domain may make some operations natural, difficult, unavailable, or irrelevant. Those relationships must be defined for each ability rather than assumed.

The main domain is not itself a branch. Two sorcerers may share an operation while affecting very different targets; two sorcerers with similar domains may use different operations.

## 4. Provisional branch catalogue

The following seven branches are a **working taxonomy**, not finalized canon. Their names and boundaries may change after examples are tested.

### B-01 — Control and Transformation

**Working scope:** Directing or regulating a target's behaviour; or changing the state, form, or properties of an existing target.

**Boundary question:** Control and transformation may be two distinct sub-operations. Keep them grouped only while testing; split them if examples show that the distinction matters consistently.

**Not automatically included:** Moving a target to a new location, creating an additional target, or cancelling an effect. Classify by the essential operation, not by the visual appearance of the result.

### B-02 — Creation and Replication

**Working scope:** Bringing an additional thing or copy into existence, rather than merely transforming or relocating something already present.

**Boundary question:** Whether creation and replication share one mechanism remains open. A copy may have limits that an original does not, but no universal copy rule is established.

### B-03 — Removal and Cancellation

**Working scope:** Removing a target from the affected scope, or ending/cancelling an existing effect.

**Boundary question:** Removing a physical object and cancelling an ongoing process may prove to be different operations. They are grouped provisionally for testing, not declared identical.

### B-04 — Amplification and Reduction

**Working scope:** Increasing or decreasing the amount, intensity, scale, or strength of a property or an effect while it remains present.

**Boundary question:** Determine what quantity changes and what remains unchanged. Do not treat every transformation as amplification or reduction.

### B-05 — Interference and Disruption

**Working scope:** Disturbing the operation, regularity, or stability of an ongoing process.

**Boundary question:** Interference is not a catch-all for every counteraction, control, cancellation, or failure. Identify the process being disrupted and the actual change observed.

### B-06 — Structural Relation Alteration

**Working scope:** Changing structural connections or relationships between parts of a material or assembled target—for example, separating connected parts, joining parts, or reorganizing how they are linked.

**Boundary:** This branch concerns the structural relation between parts. It does not automatically cover moving an intact object, nor does it automatically cover distant or abstract bonds that do not alter material structure.

### B-07 — Transfer and Redirection

**Working scope:** Moving a target or effect to a different location or destination, or changing the path along which it travels.

**Boundary:** Not every movement belongs here. Directly controlling how something moves may be Control; relocating it or redirecting its route may be Transfer and Redirection. The decisive factor is the essential operation.

### Possible function kept outside the branch list

**Perception and Detection** is not currently one of the seven branches. It may describe sensing, discovering, or interpreting information rather than operating on a target. Test it against real examples before deciding whether it is a branch, a separate function type, or part of particular domains.

## 5. Working hypothesis: restrictions and branch categories

**Provisional hypothesis for character testing:** A restriction category may be associated with the branch it primarily affects. The same individual restriction may also influence other branches, but its secondary effects need not be equal or identical.

This hypothesis is intended to make the visible hand symbol informative without making it reveal the entire person.

- The symbol indicates the restriction category already established for that sorcerer.
- It does not disclose the exact personal restriction or every consequence it causes.
- The personal restriction must be learned through practice and experience.
- The branch catalogue above is provisional. Do **not** assume the existing official restriction categories or symbol designs map one-to-one onto these seven branch labels until that relationship is explicitly confirmed.
- A restriction may affect conditions of use, precision, stability, response, access, or execution; it need not simply reduce power or cause fatigue.
- Secondary effects should follow a demonstrated relationship between operations, not a rule that every branch is weakened equally.
- Adapting to a restriction can improve reliable use without removing the restriction itself.

## 6. How to classify an ability or restriction

When an example is proposed, answer these questions in order:

1. **Target:** What exactly is affected?
2. **Main domain:** What makes this ability this character's ability, rather than a generic operation?
3. **Essential operation:** What changes between the situation before and after the act?
4. **Branch:** Which provisional branch best describes that operation? If none fits, record the mismatch instead of forcing a fit.
5. **Observed result:** What does the ability demonstrably do in this specific case?
6. **Boundary:** What does it not do, or what has not been tested?
7. **Restriction:** What is the individual restriction, stated as a concrete rule rather than a vague weakness?
8. **Primary effect:** How does that restriction affect the most directly related operation?
9. **Secondary effects:** Which other operations are affected, and what evidence connects them?
10. **Alternative explanations:** Could the result instead come from domain limits, lack of skill, the target, environment, or incomplete knowledge?

## 8. Contradiction checks

A character test should challenge the model rather than merely illustrate it. Check for:

- Two branches that describe the same operation with no reliable distinction.
- An ability that fits no branch, suggesting a missing branch or a flawed boundary.
- One branch that combines operations that behave differently in every test.
- A restriction category that fails to explain the restriction's primary effect.
- Secondary effects that are asserted without a causal link.
- A restriction that is confused with lack of training, a domain boundary, environmental interference, or an ordinary failure.
- A symbol that reveals more than the restriction category.
- A restriction that becomes removable merely because the sorcerer has adapted.
- A branch taxonomy that accidentally becomes a rigid class system or implies universal access to all applications.

A mismatch is useful evidence. Record it and revise the model deliberately; do not conceal it by changing the meaning of a branch for one character.

## 9. Revision protocol

Whenever the model changes:

1. Update this document and increment its version.
2. Explain the change and why the prior formulation was inadequate.
3. Mark a replaced proposal as superseded in the revision history; do not silently erase its history.
4. Update the Decision Log if the project owner accepts a foundational decision.
5. Recheck all character test records that depend on the changed branch or restriction rule.
6. Keep official restriction-category names and symbol designs aligned with their existing approved source; do not invent replacements here.

- Should Control and Transformation be separate branches?

- Should Removal and Cancellation be separate branches?
- Is Perception and Detection a separate function type, a branch, or domain-specific?
- Do restriction categories map directly to branches, or is the relationship more complex?
- How strongly should a restriction affect related branches, and what determines the relationship?
- Which of these rules are universal, and which are character-specific?
