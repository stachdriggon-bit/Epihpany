# EPIPHANY — Cosmic Laws, Magical Restrictions, and Anomalies

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Accepted foundational principles with intentionally open explanations.  
**Purpose:** Preserve the agreed model of cosmic laws, individual magical restrictions, anomalies, unknown entities, and the limits of in-world knowledge. This document defines the design boundaries; it does not explain every phenomenon or establish a single hidden cause.

## 1. The central principle

EPIPHANY is a mysterious world under the influence of laws and powers that its inhabitants do not fully understand. The world's rules are real even when people cannot describe them correctly. What people observe is not always enough to identify the cause.

**Anomalies are observable outcomes that arise when cosmic laws interact with the conditions of the world.** They are not limited to effects on sorcerers: the interaction may also produce unusual events, behaviors, or properties in places, objects, environments, and other parts of the world.

This principle does not mean every unusual event is an anomaly, every anomaly has the same cause, or every mystery is ultimately explained by one mechanism.

## 2. Keep five layers distinct

When designing magic or an unexplained event, do not collapse these layers into one explanation:

1. **World conditions and ordinary processes:** geography, water, weather, materials, living beings, built structures, and other local circumstances.
2. **Cosmic laws:** deeper laws whose full nature and operation are not understood by the world's inhabitants. They can be relevant to restrictions affecting sorcerers and to anomalies in the world.
3. **Observable phenomena and anomalies:** what actually happens or what witnesses can observe. An observed effect does not, by itself, reveal its cause.
4. **Individual magical restrictions:** restrictions associated with particular sorcerers that affect how their abilities operate.
5. **Knowledge and interpretation:** what a character, witness, researcher, institution, or culture believes the evidence means. An interpretation may be correct, incomplete, disputed, or mistaken.

An anomaly may be connected to cosmic laws, but the exact relationship and mechanism need not be known. A sorcerer's inability to affect a phenomenon does not prove that their restriction caused the failure. Likewise, a strange outcome does not automatically prove that a cosmic law, a sorcerer, or an entity caused it.

Terminology is deliberately narrow: **Numera** refers to phenomena connected to cosmic laws, not every supernatural or unexplained event. **Aelora Energy** is the named magical energy resource; **Aren** is its general use in sorcery, and **Aren Art** refers to the skills and techniques a sorcerer develops. These terms do not imply that cosmic laws are conscious, that every anomaly is a Numera, or that all mysteries share one cause.

## 3. Magic is open-ended, not a closed class system

- Do not define each sorcerer by a rigid universal school such as “fire mage,” “water mage,” or “support mage.”
- Magical access is character-specific. Do not assume that everyone can use magic.
- A character's ability, knowledge, control, experience, and mastery are distinct. Experience may improve prediction, technique, precision, consistency, and adaptation without guaranteeing unlimited control or greater raw power.
- An ability's behavior and limits must be established from evidence and deliberate character design; do not invent a convenient capability solely to solve a conflict.
- Similar abilities or similar-looking effects do not prove a shared source, identical rules, or a common origin.

## 4. Individual restrictions

Individual restrictions are real constraints on how a sorcerer's ability operates. They do not necessarily switch an ability off completely. Depending on the individual restriction and the situation, it may distort an effect, reduce precision or stability, change how an ability responds, or make its use difficult under particular conditions.

- A restriction may be difficult to understand even for a highly experienced sorcerer.
- Learning to anticipate or adapt to a restriction is not the same as removing it.
- Restrictions may affect different abilities in visibly different ways. An underlying pattern may become recognizable through observation, but this does not guarantee a complete explanation.
- Do not assume that every failure, unusual result, or limitation a sorcerer encounters comes from their restriction; environmental conditions and the phenomenon itself may matter.
- Some restrictions may arise unpredictably for individual sorcerers. The exact process, frequency, and underlying reason remain open.

## 5. The hand symbol

When a person becomes a sorcerer, a visible symbol appears on their hand.

- The symbol remains fixed throughout that person's life and is visible to other people.
- It indicates only the **category of the person's restriction**.
- It does not reveal the exact individual restriction, the person's abilities, skill, mastery, or a complete classification of the person.
- The individual restriction must be discovered through practice and experience.
- The world's restriction categories and their symbols are established and known in-world. Do not invent replacements or treat a sorcerer's familiarity with the category as complete practical mastery of the individual restriction.
- Do not assume the symbol explains, identifies, or predicts cosmic restrictions unless a future explicit decision establishes that connection.

## 6. Cosmic restrictions

Cosmic restrictions are a cosmic phenomenon whose full nature is not understood, even by the strongest sorcerers.

- Do not give them a definitive universal explanation or assume that powerful sorcerers can fully understand or control them.
- Do not assume there is one shared method for handling every cosmic restriction.
- Their relationship to individual restrictions, hand symbols, and particular anomalies remains open unless explicitly established later.
- A character or culture may have a theory about them; record that as an in-world belief unless it has been deliberately confirmed as objective canon.

## 7. Anomalies and uncertain causes

Anomalies are part of the world's observable mystery. They may be local or broad, brief or persistent, harmless or consequential, well documented or poorly understood. Their scope and effects must be recorded individually.

- An anomaly may emerge from the interaction between cosmic laws and specific world conditions, but the interaction need not be understood.
- Anomalies are not necessarily caused by a sorcerer, and not every sorcerous act produces an anomaly.
- A sorcerer's ability may be a possible explanation for an observed event without being the confirmed explanation.
- A local event does not establish a universal law, a global magical zone, or a shared origin with similar events elsewhere.
- Similar appearances are evidence for comparison, not proof of a shared cause.
- Keep observations, measurements, witness reports, folklore, hypotheses, and confirmed facts separate.
- Preserve uncertainty where evidence is insufficient. Do not fill an unknown cause merely because a story would benefit from an answer.

### Example: The Returning Current

At the Three-Arch Ferry, a narrow ribbon of water has been observed flowing upstream briefly beneath the middle arch around dusk. That observed behavior remains the established local phenomenon.

Possible explanations might include an interaction between cosmic laws and local conditions, the effect of a sorcerer's unknown ability, or another cause not yet considered. These are **possibilities, not established explanations**. The phenomenon's existing limits and local scope remain in force until evidence and an explicit decision support a change.

## 8. Unknown entities

The world may contain entities whose nature, motives, capabilities, knowledge, and relationship to cosmic laws are unknown.

- Do not assume every entity is omniscient, all-powerful, responsible for every anomaly, or part of one unified hierarchy.
- Do not assume entities obey, control, understand, or created the cosmic laws; those relationships remain open.
- An entity may act without being recognized as the cause of an event. Conversely, a mysterious event does not prove an entity was involved.
- Characters may live under the influence of forces they cannot identify or comprehend. Their uncertainty is part of the setting, not a gap that must immediately be closed.
- A revelation about one entity must not automatically become a universal explanation for all other mysteries.

## 9. Canon, evidence, and future additions

For every important magical or anomalous event, record:

- **Observation:** what happened, where, and under what observed conditions.
- **Scope and limits:** what the event does and does not affect.
- **Evidence:** who observed it, what was recorded, and what remains uncertain.
- **Interpretations:** competing explanations, folklore, and in-world claims.
- **Status:** accepted fact, provisional proposal, in-world claim, disputed, or open.
- **Continuity links:** relevant places, characters, events, stories, and other reference records.

A proposal becomes established canon only after explicit project-owner approval and appropriate updates to the Decision Log and relevant source records. A possibility mentioned in a story, illustration, conversation, or character's confident claim does not automatically become objective truth.

## 10. Sorcerer restrictions, attempted breach, and life cost

**Status:** Provisional model approved for development; the historical cause and exact mechanics remain open.

The following distinctions govern this topic:

1. **Ordinary use:** Using an ability within the operating limits of its individual restriction does not, by itself, consume the sorcerer's lifespan.
2. **Restriction:** The restriction limits what the ability can do or how it can operate. It is not itself a general weakening effect, and it is not the same thing as fatigue or spent magical energy.
3. **Attempted breach:** Trying to act beyond the restriction is categorically different from ordinary use or simply exerting more effort. A breach is extraordinarily difficult and cannot be treated as a reliable technique.
4. **Magical energy and lifespan:** Magical-energy depletion and loss of lifespan are distinct. One must not automatically be converted into the other.
5. **Life cost:** Attempting a breach may consume lifespan, but the cost is not automatic for every effort, nor is it established as a fixed exchange rate. The circumstances, the restriction, and what the sorcerer actually attempts may matter.
6. **Possibility of success:** Whether a restriction can be breached, even temporarily, depends on the nature of that restriction. Do not assume all restrictions share the same answer.
7. **Outcome variability:** In an exceptional case, an attempted breach might produce a narrow or temporary effect, grow more dangerous as it continues, produce only part of the intended result, or yield an unexpected result. These are possible outcome patterns, not guaranteed stages or a universal sequence.
8. **Exceptions and incomplete knowledge:** A general pattern may be recognized while rare cases remain unexplained. An apparent exception must not automatically be called a new law; it may reflect incomplete evidence, a misunderstood restriction, unusual conditions, or a genuinely rare case.
9. **No free escalation:** A breach does not grant unrestricted power, permanently remove the restriction, or prove that the sorcerer can repeat the result. Any apparent success must be recorded with its observed scope and limits.
10. **No universal recipe:** Do not prescribe one ritual, emotion, injury, energy threshold, or technique as a universal method for breaching restrictions unless a later decision explicitly establishes it.

### What is established versus unknown

The model establishes the separation between ordinary use, restrictions, attempted breach, magical-energy depletion, and lifespan cost. It does **not** yet establish which restriction categories permit a breach, the exact conditions for each, whether the cost can be measured, how a sorcerer detects lifespan loss, or whether all reported cases are interpreted correctly.

When recording an incident, distinguish: what the sorcerer attempted; what observers actually saw; the duration and scope of the effect; signs of energy depletion; evidence of lifespan loss (if any); recovery or lasting consequences; and alternative explanations. Do not infer lifespan loss merely because a sorcerer became tired, collapsed, or lost control.

## 11. The historical change hypothesis

**Status:** Open historical hypothesis; not established history.

One possible history is that sorcery once operated under different constraints and that a major event changed the relationship between sorcery and cosmic laws. If so, its onset might have been sudden while its consequences appeared gradually and unevenly among sorcerers. This could explain why restrictions did not necessarily appear in everyone at the same time, but it does not yet explain why the effects differed.

A related possibility is that some sorcerers lived for centuries or millennia and could develop extraordinary mastery over long periods. Some may eventually have reached a scale of power that threatened the world, and the change may have been a response of the cosmos rather than a conscious punishment. This is only one possible account, not an established cause. Long life does not mean every old sorcerer is powerful, and greater freedom in the past would not mean unlimited ability or the power to do anything.

Keep all of the following open:
- whether such a world-changing event definitely occurred;
- whether sorcerers, researchers, a war, a threat to the planet, an interaction of several causes, or something unknown precipitated it;
- whether cosmic laws responded, changed, or were merely revealed by the event;
- why restrictions appeared at different times or in different forms;
- whether lifespan costs existed before the change or became relevant afterward;
- what evidence, if any, survives and how reliable its interpretation is.

Do not name the event, date it, assign responsibility, describe its exact mechanism, or claim that every restriction originated from it until those choices are explicitly made. Conflicting chronicles, incomplete records, and competing theories may exist in-world, but they remain interpretations rather than objective truth.

## 12. Design rules for stories and tests

- A test scene is not automatically a canonical event.
- A failed breach attempt does not prove that breaching is universally impossible, and a successful-looking result does not prove that a restriction was truly exceeded.
- Similar outcomes across two sorcerers do not prove their restrictions share a category, mechanism, or origin.
- Do not use lifespan loss as a casual penalty for ordinary spell use.
- Do not use a breach to solve a conflict without establishing the attempt, the unusual circumstances, the actual result, and its limits.
- Preserve uncertainty where the evidence does not distinguish between a restriction effect, a skill limitation, environmental interference, a cosmic phenomenon, or a mistaken interpretation.

## 13. Questions intentionally left open


The following are not settled by this framework:

- The ultimate origin and full nature of cosmic laws.
- Whether cosmic laws have intention, awareness, or agency.
- The complete mechanism by which cosmic laws interact with world conditions to produce a particular anomaly.
- The exact origin and distribution of individual restrictions.
- The final number and names of restriction categories.
- The relationship, if any, between individual restrictions, cosmic restrictions, and hand symbols.
- The nature, motives, limits, and origins of unknown entities.
- Whether any two particular anomalies share a cause.
- Whether a specific anomaly was caused by a sorcerer, a cosmic-law interaction, an entity, ordinary conditions, or some combination.

These open questions are deliberate design boundaries, not invitations to silently invent answers.
