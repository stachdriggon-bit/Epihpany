# EPIPHANY — Provisional Magical Phenomena Catalog

**Version:** 0.3  
**Status:** Three local magical phenomena accepted for story canon at their named anchor locations. Their causes and exact conditions remain open; no shared origin, global mechanism, or wider distribution is established.  
**Purpose:** Test how magical phenomena can shape ordinary life without replacing evidence, geography, or character choice. Stable IDs are reserved for continuity if a proposal is retained.

> These entries are accepted local phenomena because the project owner approved the three story drafts. Their effects remain local to the named anchor unless later evidence and an explicit decision support a wider scope. Acceptance does not settle their causes, dates, or any connection to one another.

## MP-IL-001 — The Returning Current

- **Status:** Accepted local phenomenon
- **Place:** IL-LOC-01 — The Three-Arch Ferry
- **Design lens:** Place-bound; water; cyclical or conditional
- **Observable effect:** On some evenings, a narrow ribbon of water beneath the middle stone arch briefly flows upstream against the river's main current. Local witnesses describe the interval as “seven breaths”; that is a traditional estimate, not a measured universal duration.
- **Conditions:** The event has been observed around dusk, but the exact conditions and frequency are not yet established. Do not assume it occurs every evening or during every season.
- **Limits:** It affects only a narrow band beneath the middle arch. It does not reverse the whole river, lift boats, predict floods, or make the crossing automatically safe.
- **Risks and consequences:** A ferry rope may slacken or pull sideways; people who mistake the brief reversal for a safe passage can misjudge a crossing. The arch and ferry still need ordinary inspection and repair.
- **People and knowledge:** Ferrymen may recognize the signs, but different observers may disagree about timing or what triggers it. No formal magical guild or universal technique is established.
- **Daily-life use:** The crossing community must decide how to maintain the bridge and ferry equipment, and whether tolls should fund shared repairs. A story draft tests a pale thread tied to the ferry rope as a practical way to spot fraying in dim light; it is not a charm or protection.
- **Evidence versus interpretation:** The visible reverse-flow is the phenomenon in the proposal. Claims that the river “chooses” who may cross, that the arch is alive, or that the event is a warning are folklore/hypotheses only.
- **Story test:** A repair dispute becomes urgent when the current appears during a crossing. The phenomenon reveals a practical hazard but does not decide who should pay for repairs.
- **Open questions:** Why the effect occurs; how frequently; whether weather, river height, or the arch's condition matters; how long the phenomenon has been known locally.
- **Continuity guardrails:** Do not extend it to the entire Ilan River. Preserve the bridge/ferry's established economic tension. No exact date or historic origin is assigned.

## MP-TH-001 — The Climbing Thread

- **Status:** Accepted local phenomenon
- **Place:** TH-LOC-02 — The Spring Steps
- **Design lens:** Place-bound; water; perceptual/diagnostic
- **Observable effect:** Shortly after sunset, a thread-thin line of water sometimes climbs a shallow carved groove on one terrace step for a few moments, against gravity, before settling back into the channel. The effect is visible in dim light and is not a large or continuous stream.
- **Conditions:** It has been reported at the Spring Steps, but its frequency and exact conditions are open. The proposal does not claim that every channel or spring behaves this way.
- **Limits:** The thread carries very little water. It cannot fill a terrace, raise the spring's total output, carry objects, or explain why the source flow is weak. It may fail to appear; absence is not proof that a person has damaged the channel.
- **Risks and consequences:** People may over-trust it as a perfect diagnostic or blame a neighbor when it stops short. Ordinary inspection remains necessary.
- **People and knowledge:** Channel caretakers have learned to watch it, but its interpretation is local practical knowledge, not a universal magical science. Newcomers may not know the custom.
- **Daily-life use:** It can prompt a careful inspection of the terrace channels and encourage people to record where the thread appears, while water sharing still requires fair agreements. A story draft tests chalk marks on damaged stone and daily flow records; neither turns the thread into a precise gauge.
- **Evidence versus interpretation:** The brief uphill movement is the observable claim. It does not prove that the spring is conscious, that the water judges fairness, or that the source has an unlimited reserve.
- **Story test:** During a period of reduced flow, a missing thread leads to accusations. A physical obstruction is found in one channel, but removing it does not fully explain or solve the spring's weakened supply. The community must repair the channel and agree on temporary sharing.
- **Open questions:** Why the thread climbs; whether the groove's shape matters; how often it appears; whether it responds to conditions not yet recorded.
- **Continuity guardrails:** Maintain a plausible recharge area and water balance. Do not make the spring limitless or turn the phenomenon into a moral test that magically rewards “good” people.

## MP-AZ-001 — The Stormglass Echo

- **Status:** Accepted local phenomenon
- **Place:** AZ-LOC-02 — The Rain-Cistern Island
- **Design lens:** Made or maintained; water; sound
- **Observable effect:** A small resonant stone fitted into a communal cistern returns a faint echo of the rainstorm that last filled it when tapped gently. As the stored water level falls, the echo's pitch changes. Locals call the stone “stormglass,” though it is stone, not glass.
- **Conditions:** The effect belongs to the fitted stone and its cistern, not every body of water. The proposed behavior depends on the resonant fitting being intact and the cistern being maintained.
- **Limits:** The echo offers only a rough indication of water level. It cannot measure exact volume, reveal water purity, predict future rain, or replace a marked measuring rod and inspection.
- **Risks and consequences:** If the fitting cracks or is removed for repairs, the cistern falls silent. Residents may mistake silence for emptiness, or may continue trusting an old tone after the cistern's shape has changed.
- **People and knowledge:** Cistern keepers maintain the fitting and compare its sound with physical measurements. Knowledge can be lost if maintenance instructions are not passed on.
- **Daily-life use:** The echo helps residents notice a falling reserve, but rationing still requires transparent records, estimates, and community decisions. A story draft tests a public tally board that records measured levels and water drawn; the board is a proposed local practice, not a magical feature.
- **Evidence versus interpretation:** The changing echo and the stored water level can be compared. The stone does not remember every event, speak in words, or foretell weather.
- **Story test:** A cracked cistern fitting is removed during repair, making the usual echo disappear just as a dry spell approaches. The community must decide how to ration water without its familiar signal and use direct measurements until the fitting is repaired.
- **Open questions:** Why the stone reproduces the storm's sound; how the fitting is made; whether suitable stones are common; how accurate the pitch-to-level relationship is.
- **Continuity guardrails:** The cistern's water supply must still balance with rainfall, storage, population, and trade. Do not turn the fitting into ancient lost technology or a limitless source of water.

## 4. Comparison and variety check

| Feature | Returning Current | Climbing Thread | Stormglass Echo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main sensory signature | Visible reverse-flow | Water moving uphill | Stored storm sound |
| Main social pressure | Repair funding and safe crossing | Fair water-sharing and blame | Rationing and trust in measurements |
| Practical limit | Narrow location and short duration | Tiny volume; incomplete diagnosis | Approximate level only; no purity or forecast |
| How it can fail socially | People mistake a hazard for a signal | People treat absence as proof of guilt | People trust a silent or outdated fitting |
| What it must not solve | Bridge funding dispute | Entire spring shortage | Water scarcity itself |

The three proposals intentionally use different magical signatures and different kinds of community decision. Their similarities in involving water do **not** imply a shared origin or mechanism.
